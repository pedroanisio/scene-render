## Unreleased

- Clay objects mesh their field on every core: each pass of the surface-nets extraction runs over independent slabs and joins them in order, so the mesh is the same, vertex for vertex and index for index, as before. A fingerprinted seven-blob object at `resolution="160"` meshed in 359.5 ms on one core and meshes in 45.4 ms on a 12-thread Ryzen 5 5600X; boiling clay re-meshes on every boil frame, so a 5 s clay film encoded in 4.6 s instead of 21.7 s there.
- A geodesic camera's picture reads each sample's orbit from a table instead of integrating it. A ray's orbit depends only on its impact parameter and on whether it starts toward the hole, so each frame tabulates u(φ) and du/dφ for 2048 impact parameters (f64 RK4, crowded round the critical 3√3 M where the higher-order images are thin) and each sample interpolates its crossings and its escape angle from the two rows around it. A 1280 × 720 film at 64 samples per pixel encoded in 2.4 s instead of 16.0 s on an RTX 3050; its frames differ from the traced ones by 54.4 dB PSNR at worst (56.1 dB on average).
- Documents with a simulation evaluate the next frame on a helper thread while the current one renders, as documents without one already did. A simulated document gives each frame the same graph whether or not the frame after it was evaluated first, which every simulated corpus document is now tested for; the frames are unchanged. A 5 s film of a flock, a slime mould and a smoke solver spent 3.3 s instead of 4.0 s waiting on evaluation.
- Rigid bodies that are closed meshes, the pieces of every fracture among them, are decomposed into convex parts on every core when their world is built, each body then taking its own in order, so the world and every frame of it are the same as before. Twelve mesh pieces built in 1.32 s on one core and in 0.40 s on a 6-core Ryzen 5 5600X; a 5 s fracture film encoded in 6.7 s instead of 8.7 s there.
- `slime` and `fluid` step on every core with the same results to the bit: Physarum agents move in parallel from the trail as it was at the start of the step and deposit in agent order, the trail diffuses by rows, and the smoke solver relaxes its pressure one colour of its red-black order at a time with the colours held apart, advecting velocity and dye by rows. At the size of a 384 × 480 panel a 60 000-agent step took 4.97 ms on one thread and takes 1.22 ms on a 12-thread Ryzen 5 5600X, a 128-cell smoke step 7.70 ms and 4.10 ms; a film of both and a flock encoded in 3.9 s instead of 6.5 s there.
- Clay evaluates its fingerprint roughness only near the surface. The roughness moves the surface by a bounded amount and the joined field is 1-Lipschitz, so beyond that reach and two cell diagonals the smooth field alone gives the side a grid point is on, which is all the mesher reads there; the mesh is the same. A fingerprinted seven-blob object at `resolution="160"` evaluates it at 10 % of its 2.4 million grid points and meshes in 31 ms instead of 45 ms.
- Clay also skips the field itself away from the surface: a block of 4 × 4 × 4 grid points whose centre lies further from the surface than the block's half-diagonal is wholly on one side of it, and keeps the centre's value for its sign. The fingerprinted showcase body evaluates its field 0.45 million times instead of 2.4 million and meshes in 22 ms; a 5 s clay film encoded in 3.0 s instead of 3.7 s on a Ryzen 5 5600X, its mesh and frames unchanged.

## v0.7.1 — 2026-10-21

This release fixes the path tracer's hardware ray queries on Mesa's lavapipe, which missed most surfaces of a path-traced scene, and falls back to the BVH walk for every scene too large for the adapter's acceleration structures. The vendored schema is still sr-core 1.7.0.

### Path tracer

- The hardware traversal builds one bottom-level structure of the scene's triangles and one of the bounds of its splats and instances, each holding its own primitives only, and maps a hit to its primitive through a table. Before, each structure also held the other kind's primitives as inactive entries; Mesa's lavapipe (observed on 25.0.7 and 25.2.8) let those into its bounds and then missed nearly every hit, so path-traced frames lost most of their surfaces. A scene with any volume is path-traced, so on lavapipe this also reached scenes whose camera does not ask for the path tracer, such as one with an empty pyro volume.
- The fallback to the BVH walk compares the triangle structure's vertex count with the adapter's `max_blas_primitive_count`, the count wgpu validates. Before, it compared the triangle count, so a scene with more vertices than that limit but fewer triangles failed to build its structures instead of falling back.

### Changes that can alter output from v0.7.0

- On lavapipe, path-traced frames that use hardware ray queries change: they now show the surfaces the BVH walk and OpenGL show. `SR_GPU_RAY_QUERY=0`, the BVH walk and OpenGL are unchanged.

## v0.7.0 — 2026-10-20

This release lets the path tracer stop tracing pixels whose mean has converged, denoises lighting apart from surface colour, finds its hits with hardware ray queries where the adapter offers them, and compiles its media shaders on NVIDIA's OpenGL driver. Validation warns about, and `inspect` reports, the cost of each path-traced camera, and more of the declared 3D and layout attributes are implemented. The vendored schema is still sr-core 1.7.0.

### Path tracer

- `render --path-noise REL` and `encode --path-noise REL` stop tracing a path-traced pixel once, from 16 samples on, its luminance and coverage are within REL relative standard error of their means; `pathSamples` caps the rest. A stopped pixel keeps its mean, so denoised and tiled output match a full render's statistics. `render --stats` reports `paths` with the `traced` samples against the `budget`. Without the flag rendering is unchanged.
- The path tracer's denoiser divides each pixel by its coverage and first-hit albedo before filtering and multiplies it back after, and sets how far a tap may differ in luminance from the variance of each pixel's own samples. Against a 4096-sample reference it halves the error at 8 samples while keeping fine albedo texture (the previous filter kept 46 % of it and its error exceeded the noisy frame's), no longer raises the error of an already clean 256-sample frame (previously 5.7 times), and leaves silhouette coverage as traced (previously 1.6 times the noisy error). `denoise` defaults to true, so path-traced output intentionally changes; set `denoise="false"` for the raw estimate.
- Where the adapter offers hardware ray queries, the path tracer finds its hits through acceleration structures the driver builds for each pass, on the GPU's ray-tracing units where it has them, and otherwise walks its own BVH as before. Both trace the same paths with the same random numbers, so images agree up to rays whose nearest hit differs by rounding. `SR_GPU_RAY_QUERY=0` keeps the BVH walk. `gpus` reports `ray_query` for each adapter and `render --stats` reports `hardware_rays` under `paths`. The JSON envelope is `schema_version` 1.2.0.
- On NVIDIA's OpenGL driver, which inlines every function call, the path tracer's media shaders compile in bounded memory. A lit medium gathers its in-scattered light in one loop over lights, crossed domains and surfaces instead of marching a shadow ray inside every step of the transport march, and the shader specializer removes code under overrides that are false. Before, compiling a frame with lit smoke or gas could exhaust memory. Images do not change beyond rounding.

### Path-traced cost

- W15 warns when a `renderer="pathtrace"` camera traces more than 1920 × 1080 × 256 × 4 path segments per frame: its frame's width × height × `pathSamples` × `maxBounces`, the frame being its `viewport3D`'s or the project's. 320 samples at 20 bounces in 1080p is 6.25 times that.
- `inspect` lists each path-traced camera's frame size, samples, bounces and path segments per frame, marking those W15 warns about; in JSON they are `path_costs`.

### Command line

- `encode --output ID -o PATH` writes that output to PATH with every setting it declares. Before, `-o` silently replaced the named output with an ad-hoc one at the project size with default settings. PATH must keep the output's file type; several `--output` ids, an unknown id, or `--codec` with `--output` are usage errors (exit status 2).

### Declared attributes now implemented

- Instances honor `clipOut` for their playback span and automatic end, and `fit`/`boxWidth`/`boxHeight` for symbol placement, including cover clipping and a blurred cover behind contain-blur.
- Physical cameras use the sensor height for frames narrower than the sensor aspect, including depth of field. With default sensor dimensions, square and portrait scenes using `focalLength` intentionally change; wider frames retain the horizontal gate. Geodesic `pathSamples` above 4096 now emit W14 before clamping.
- `antialias3d` selects 1 (default), 2 or 4 raster samples, rounding 3 up to 4. Unsupported counts fall back with a warning. Previously the renderer always used four samples: default 3D edge coverage therefore intentionally changes; set 4 to retain that sampling.
- Plain fit output reframing honors layout and segment focus, including animated segment focus.
- A voxels object whose `palette` lists fewer materials than the indices its cells use, and that has no `material` of its own, colours those cells from the `.vox` file, as the schema states. Before, the frame failed with "the palette index N has no material" although the file has colours; `tests/corpus/valid/voxels.scene.xml` now renders.
- Medium albedo defaults to white in the volume library and renderer, using the library default as the renderer fallback.
- NaN opacity from animation keys becomes zero before inheritance, and repeat-copy opacity factors are clamped to 0..1. INERT-I17 identifies 2.5D attributes without threeD and alignment attributes without alignment; centered alignment still ignores margin.

### Examples

- `examples/glass-of-water/` drops a drop into a glass of water with native elements only: transmissive glass and water materials under the path tracer, an ocean bounded by the glass that the drop's `waterImpulse` drives, and the ocean's `whitewater` spray as the splash. A test holds it to those elements.

### Changes that can alter output or verdicts from v0.6.0

- Path-traced frames are denoised by the new filter, so their output changes; `denoise="false"` gives the raw estimate as before.
- With default sensor dimensions, square and portrait frames from physical cameras that set `focalLength` change framing and depth of field.
- 3D raster passes take one sample per pixel unless `antialias3d` asks for more; v0.6.0 always took four, so default 3D edges change.
- Path-traced cameras past the W15 threshold now warn, and geodesic `pathSamples` above 4096 warn W14 before clamping.
- On adapters with hardware ray queries, path-traced images can differ from v0.6.0 at rays whose nearest hit differs by rounding.
- `encode --output ID -o PATH` writes the named output with its declared settings, where v0.6.0 wrote an ad-hoc output at the project size.
- The JSON envelope is `schema_version` 1.2.0: `gpus` adds `ray_query`, `inspect` adds `path_costs` and `render --stats` adds `paths`.

- scene-render is licensed under the PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0, as from 0.5.0 (`LICENSE`, `NOTICE`). Commercial use needs a separate licence (`COMMERCIAL.md`).

## v0.6.0 — 2026-10-14

This release draws 3D objects on OpenGL, gives every command a versioned JSON output and a read-only MCP server, adds image scopes and fuller loudness measurement, implements schema attributes that earlier versions declared but did not apply, and turns malformed or oversized input into reported errors instead of panics. The vendored schema is still sr-core 1.7.0.

### OpenGL

- The 3D pass draws on OpenGL as on Vulkan, Metal and DirectX 12. A document with 3D objects now uses the best-ranked adapter whatever its backend; before, it moved to a Vulkan adapter by default and failed with exit status 2 when OpenGL was named.
- Opening an OpenGL device refuses a driver older than OpenGL 4.3 or OpenGL ES 3.1, and an adapter that cannot give the 3D pass's colour and depth formats 4x multisampling, at device creation rather than at the first frame.
- The vendored wgpu-hal asks EGL for desktop OpenGL 4.6, 4.5, 4.4, 4.3 and then 3.3, newest first, so the NVIDIA driver, which answers a 3.3 request with exactly 3.3, gives a context new enough for the shaders.

### Command line

- Every command accepts `--format json` (or `--json`) and prints one envelope, `scene-render-cli/1`, with `schema_version` 1.1.0. `crates/scene-render/schema/cli-output-1.schema.json` describes it and the README lists each command's result.
- `scene-render mcp` serves `validate`, `inspect`, `eval`, `captions`, `capabilities` and `explain` to a Model Context Protocol client over standard input and output. Each tool returns the command's JSON envelope. Messages and arguments are parsed into typed requests that refuse unknown keys; a message holds at most 1 MiB and a session at most 100,000 messages.
- `scene-render scopes FILE` measures clipping, legal range and saturation on a still or on every `--step`-th frame of a video, as text or JSON; `--chroma-limit` sets the oversaturation threshold. The production gate runs it on candidates and fails them past `--max-illegal`, `--max-out-of-gamut` and `--max-oversaturated`.
- A panic is reported as one `internal error: MESSAGE at FILE:LINE` line and exits with status 2.

### Audio measurement

- The loudness meter gives loudness range (EBU Tech 3342) and the momentary (400 ms) and short-term (3 s) maxima beside integrated loudness and true peak. The delivery report records them, and `encode` prints them on its audio line.
- True peak and the true-peak limiter oversample to at least 192 kHz: 8x at 32 and 44.1 kHz, 4x from 48 kHz up as before. At 44.1 kHz true peak can read up to about 0.2 dB higher than in v0.5.0.
- The momentary windows and the gating blocks step by the same rounded 100 ms, so the momentary maximum at sample rates such as 11025 Hz comes from the windows momentary reports.

### Declared attributes now implemented

- Mesh-gradient points now honour animated box-relative `x` and `y` coordinates, defaulting independently to `col/(cols-1)` and `row/(rows-1)`. Geometry and colour share the Catmull–Rom surface; absent coordinates preserve existing pixels.
- Inline vector assets (primitives and paths) now honour `strokePosition` and `paintOrder` through the shape stroke implementation. No existing corpus, example or golden vector asset sets these attributes.
- Curved connectors use the shared SREP 58 32-sample length table and evaluate outline cuts, labels, gaps and trimmed endpoints on the true cubic. Curved output can change away from the symmetric apex, including `tests/corpus/valid/connector-points.scene.xml`; straight and orthogonal routes retain their geometry.
- Trim shape modifiers default to simultaneous, matching `shape/@trimMode`.
- Mesh-warp and soft-body grid dimensions share the schema's defensive 2–16 clamp before allocation.
- Text animator `easeHigh` and `easeLow` independently ease the high and low ends of range selection. `rotationX`, `rotationY`, and `zDepth` project complete glyphs through the layer's 3D camera, or the default 60-degree camera for ordinary 2D text. Authored easing and glyph depth/rotation can intentionally change output.
- Caption emphasis uses the active colour for karaoke/highlight/pop/enlarge and bold weight for other presets. WebVTT cue settings round-trip; percentage `line` and `position` place burned captions, and unsupported settings produce render notes.
- Caption tracks stack by `z` among top-level composition entries (default 1000000), preserving track document order on ties. Groups remain compositing units.
- Nodes, symbols, text animators and puppet pins share human labels in diagnostics, findings and checkpoint companion metadata: nonblank name, else id, else element index. IDs and resume keys remain stable. Inspect lists symbol labels. The JSON envelope is `schema_version` 1.1.0: a diagnostic may carry `label`.
- Lighting implements `falloff`: linear, quadratic, smooth (the schema default) and none. Omitted falloff now uses smoothstep instead of the previous fixed quadratic attenuation; use explicit quadratic to retain that curve.
- Pixel motion blur declares its amount and sample controls and respects the quality-tier sample budget (8 in draft), retaining the existing 64-tap maximum.
- Matte-choke implements position: positive amounts erode inside, dilate outside (the schema default), or average both results at center. Negative amounts reverse inside/outside. Existing sign-only chokes can retain their former erosion/dilation direction by setting position to inside.
- Stroke and outline implement softness with the schema default 0.1, including when omitted. At ordinary scale that default stays below the one-pixel blur threshold; scaled nodes can now have softer edges. Their declaration lists and matte-choke’s list advertise only controls the kernels use.
- De-esser gain supplies post-processing make-up gain. Limiter gain drives the input before limiting so the documented threshold remains the processed signal’s ceiling. Zero gain retains both filters’ previous processing.
- Ocean gravity is consistently m/s²: solver and default wave speed convert through `pixelsPerMeter` and the ocean's world scale; impact cavities use physical gravity. Ocean motion changes when world units differ from metres.
- Particle and flock sprites accept image sequences (looped by particle age), videos and generators (layer time), with sequence missing-frame policies and particle blend order preserved.
- `scene-render params FILE` prints parameter metadata and variant labels as JSON. Parameter diagnostics include labels alongside stable ids.
- Unknown chart kinds produce render errors instead of column charts. Stepping programs require explicit dimensions. Both fracture paths share the documented 0.3 energy default; the existing 2D mass floor remains `1e-6` kg.
- Render-report asset records carry the licence and credit of every asset, generated media included. Video layer records include source in/out timecodes at the asset frame rate, with drop-frame labels preserved.
- Image, video, audio and mesh proxies are selected through the representation resolver. Explicit names win; audio and image/video representations can also be selected by language tag, then primary language subtag.
- Representation dimensions and aspect ratios are checked (A08), as are declared audio duration, sample rate and channel count (A09). Layout retains the original asset dimensions; video display width includes its pixel aspect ratio.
- An audio track without a language takes its asset's, or the selected representation's, when tagging the encoded stream; audible video layers contribute theirs. Several languages remain `mul` and none `und`.
- Tracking-kind/data mismatches, and camera tracks used by 2D constraints or stabilisation, are rejected with E17. Planar tracks continue to supply their centre, rotation and scale to 2D constraints.

### Input bounds and errors

- A scene document is held to 64 MiB, 4,000,000 XML nodes and 256 levels of nesting. Deeper nesting is S12; the other limits are XML errors. Program outputs re-parsed in place are held to the same bounds.
- Includes are keyed by canonical path: a cycle is one E13 at its first repeat, and includes nested past the include-depth limit are E13 with their own message.
- LUT sizes are bounded to 2–256 a side for 3D tables and 2–65536 entries for 1D tables, with checked products, for `.cube`, CLF and OCIO bakes. IES files with impossible angle counts, out-of-order or non-finite angles, or negative or non-finite candela are errors.
- A schema default that does not parse as its attribute's type is a model error naming the element, and a timecode whose frame number overflows is a value error. A panic in delivery's look-ahead evaluator is a delivery error.

### Changes that can alter output or verdicts from v0.5.0

- 3D LUTs are interpolated in f32 in the shader instead of through the hardware filter, which keeps as few as 4 fractional bits under Vulkan. Graded colours can change by several code values; an identity table now leaves every colour unchanged.
- Documents with 3D objects can render on an OpenGL adapter that v0.5.0 would not choose.
- True peak at 32 and 44.1 kHz, and the momentary maximum at rates whose 100 ms is not a quarter of the rounded 400 ms, can differ from v0.5.0.
- Documents past the size, node or nesting bounds, self-including documents, and out-of-range LUT or IES tables now fail validation instead of being expanded or crashing.
- The attributes listed above under *Declared attributes now implemented* change output where documents set them, and in the cases their entries name, where they are omitted.

## v0.4.1 — 2026-10-11

This release vendors sr-core schema 1.7.0 byte-identical (document version 1.7) and implements its one change, SREP 87: ids that a program emits are scoped by the program's id. It also reports SREP 72's precision findings under the SREP's codes, and fixes how an included document's references are resolved.

### Schema 1.7.0 and SREP 87

- **Document version 1.7.** `scene/@version` accepts `1.7`. Version 1.7 adds no syntax and gates nothing, so a 1.7 document validates and renders as the same document at 1.6.
- **Program-emitted ids (SREP 87).** An id `E` that the build-mode program `P` emits becomes `P.E` in the document, so two programs, or a program and the document, can use the same local ids.
  - Inside an output, IDREF and IDREFS tokens, `prop` and `propAtTime` ids and the node id of `link/@source` resolve to the output's own ids first.
  - A bare reference from outside resolves when exactly one program emitted the id, with the new warning W13, and fails with the new error PRG17 when several did. The same holds for `override/@target` of a layout, a variant, an instance or an include.
  - The bone names in an emitted skeleton's skin weights are its local ids.
  - Local ids are kept per document of the build, so a written id of one document that equals an emitted id of another keeps its seed.
  - PRG14 covers a repeated id within one output and a namespaced id equal to another id.
- **Kit.** sr-core's 15 SREP 87 cases and the amended SREP 66 collision case pass on this engine. Four of them are failure cases that `run.py` cannot check; they were checked by hand.

### SREP 72 follow-ups

- **PREC1.** `project/@precision="f32"` in a document with 3D content gives the information finding PREC1, once, in `validate` and in the render report. Its message starts with SREP 72's words, "3D internal buffers stay f16". It replaces the engine's E23, which said the same and had no other use. PREC1 covers every kind the 3D pass draws: `object3D`, `particles3D`, `ocean`, `blackHole` and `accretionDisk`. sr-core's erratum of 2026-10-11 adds the last two to SREP 72's list, and SREP 72 is now Final.
- **PREC10.** A device without float32 blending or filtering already failed an f32 render, naming the missing feature. The error message now starts with "PREC10:", and the render report gives it the code PREC10, also when an output layer meets it first.

### Fixes

- **References inside an include name the include's own document.**
  - A transformConstraint's `pole` resolves in the constraint's own scope, as its `target` already did.
  - A target or pole that names a bone resolves to the bone of the skeleton in that scope. Before, the rig took the first skeleton in document order that declared a bone of that id, in any document.
  - A fracture's `source` resolves in its owner's scope (E11 when it names nothing there).
  - The 3D renderer's references (a parent, a camera's target and focus target, a joint's look-at) no longer fall back to the including document's node or light of the same id.
- **A constraint target or pole that names nothing** in its scope now gives an E11 warning. A top-level constraint no longer binds to a bone inside a symbol instance or a repeat copy, whose ids are scoped to that copy.
- **Each included document reads its own parameters:** `repeat/@over`, `param:` links, text placeholders and `param()` read the included document's defaults and data sources, never the including document's.
- **CI.** The Mesa shader cache is keyed by the runner's CPU. A cache written on a runner with other instruction-set extensions made llvmpipe crash with SIGILL in encode tests.

### Changes that can alter output or verdicts from v0.4.0

- `validate` and the render report give PREC1 where v0.4.0 gave E23 (in the report, `X-rs-scene-render-E23`). `scene-render explain E23` is an unknown code (exit 2); `explain PREC1` and `explain PREC10` describe the new codes.
- A render that fails for want of float32 blending or filtering is reported as PREC10 instead of `X-rs-scene-render-RENDER`.
- Documents with programs:
  - one that v0.4.0 refused with PRG14, because two programs or a program and the document used the same id, is now valid;
  - one that writes an id equal to a namespaced id (`<shape id="p.g1">` beside a program `p` that emits `g1`) is refused with PRG14;
  - one in which two programs' namespaced ids compose to one value is refused with PRG14.
- Documents with includes, constraints, fractures or 3D references that named the including document's element of the same id now resolve to the include's own element, or report E11. Their frames can change.
- Other rendered frames do not change.

## v0.4.0 — 2026-10-11

This release vendors sr-core schema 1.6.0 byte for byte. It also carries the follow-ups that SREPs 76 to 85 ask of the engine, the version gates of SREP 83 and the value forms of SREPs 84 and 85, plus include, 2.5D, rendering and memory fixes found while testing v0.3.2.

### Schema 1.6.0, vendored byte-identical

`schema/scene-render-1.1.xsd`, `schema/scene-render-1.1.sch` and `schema/capabilities-1.schema.json` are byte-identical to sr-core's release 1.6.0 (tag `schema-1.6.0`, commit 54af5c1). `schema/UPSTREAM` gives their SHA-256 and lists the SREPs this engine implements. The capability manifest names schema 1.6.0. Earlier releases carried the 1.6 additions as local extensions with a few differences from sr-core. Those differences are gone:

- The sr-model generator reads a union that holds an anonymous member type (`tonemap/@reference`, SREP 67), so the vendored XSD needs no renamed types.
- sr-core does not declare `program/@effects`, which v0.3.0 to v0.3.2 accepted: a `program` node that writes it is refused with S04.

SREP 86 (Process) records why the gates of `blackHole`, `accretionDisk`, `camera/@geodesics`, `pyroBlast`, `voxelAsset` and voxel objects admit version 1.3 and later, while every other addition of schema 1.6.0 requires version 1.6.

### SREP follow-ups

- **Version gates (SREPs 76, 80 and 83).** BH1, VOX1 and SREP 40's gates V8, MSQ1, P3D1, OCN1, CRT1, FRX1 and GEO1 refuse versions 1.0 to 1.2 only, and their messages say "1.3 or later". In v0.3.2 they refused every version but 1.3, so no document could hold a black hole, cells, a volume, pyro, an ocean, a crater, a fracture or globe relief together with a 1.6 feature such as `viewport3D`.
- **Warning renames (SREP 76).** The black-hole warnings are W11 (a hole or disk that no geodesic camera draws) and W12 (`denoise` on a geodesic camera). In v0.3.2 they were W03 and W04, codes that SREP 57 gives to its generated-marker warnings.
- **Boolean values (SREPs 76, 77 and 84).** Rules read an `xs:boolean` attribute by its value: "true" and "1" are true, "false" and "0" are false, and surrounding whitespace is ignored. This covers BH5 to BH8, W11, W12 and W05 (`geodesics`, `denoise`), CRT11 and CRT12 (`mantle`), C15, C43, R15, R48, VOL5, C80, GEO2, PYRO9 to PYRO11 and the warning W02. Integer attributes in these rules are read whatever their sign, leading zeros or padding.
- **Decimal and double values (SREP 85).** C13, C14, C15, C37, C38, C44, C52, C53, C54, C56, PYRO1, PYRO2, PYRO4, VOL4, P3D3, P3D10, OCN3, OCN5, PYRO11, PYC6, FRX11, ITR1 and SRT1 read their numeric attributes by value, so "+1" is 1. In v0.3.2, XPath's `number()` read "+1" as NaN. Exponent forms such as `1e0` follow the native validator, which is normative for them. INF, -INF and NaN compare as the Schematron compares them.
- **Disk profile (SREP 76).** The geodesic pass divides the thin-disk profile by its exact maximum, (6/7)^(3/2) 7^(-1/4) ≈ 0.48787, instead of 0.4880.
- **Supersample filter (SREP 89).** The down filter clamps each channel to [0, max(1, M)], where M is the largest sample of non-zero weight, instead of [0, 1]. Colour finishing (exposure, looks, tone mapping) runs on the filtered frame. A frame whose samples all lie within [0, 1] gives the same bytes as before.

### Fixes

- **Includes and instances.** References written inside an included document, a symbol instance or a repeat copy resolve in that scope. Before, the 3D renderer and the rigid-body world compared them as written, so an included black hole lost its disk and its camera lost its look-at target.
- **Included definitions.** An included document's materials, material maps, effects (and their shader files), fonts and maps resolve in that document. Before, the renderer looked them up in the including document only, so an included `object3D` failed with "material not found" or took the including document's material of the same id. The assets, effects and materials an include merges animate on the include's clock, as its content does. A compute node's `src` and a transition's `shader` resolve from the directory of the document that writes them. Sections an include does not merge (lights, paints, styles, physics, audio, markers, parameters and the others) are reported as E12.
- **threeD groups.** A group, instance, include or repeat with `threeD` is placed in 2.5D as one picture, with or without effects. Before, a group without effects drew its children flat and dropped its `zDepth`, `rotationX` and `rotationY` without a warning. A `threeD` child of such a group goes through the camera once, and the group's sheet keeps what it draws up to 2048 px beyond the frame. Repeat copies are not placed one by one. In 360 renders, a group with a `threeD` of its own is placed as one picture.
- **Validation with programs.** `validate` reports errors that appear only once build-mode programs run, such as two programs emitting the same id (PRG14). Before, these documents passed validation and failed at render.
- **Bloom knee.** The soft knee of bloom and halation never reaches below black. With a threshold under 0.5, dark ground was lifted (#050505 became #1C1C1C).
- **Texel-aligned composites.** An offscreen or layer whose texels land one to one on whole target pixels is copied with `textureLoad` rather than sampled. Sampling weighed in the neighbouring texel on llvmpipe and rounded about a quarter of the edge channels one step down.
- **ISF pass buffers.** Reads at a texel centre return that texel exactly, so cellular automata and jump-flood passes do not freeze in bands or stripe as they did on llvmpipe. Sampler names are bound per scope, and a failed compile names `[GLSL-F64]` or `[GLSL-SAMPLER-ARG]` with the user's line.
- **Memory.** Decoded images unused for a few frames are evicted. Vector targets beyond a two-frame budget are rasterised just before their draw and released after it, instead of all at once for the whole frame. The text face cache keeps only faces whose font bytes are still in use.
- **Speed.** Zero-opacity leaves are skipped, adjustment layers reuse their backdrop snapshot, and parsed font faces are cached. Renderer CPU per frame fell by 20 to 23 % on a measured film, with byte-identical frames.

### Known gaps

- SREP 72's information finding PREC1 (`precision="f32"` with 3D content) is not implemented; the engine reports E23 there. sr-core 1.6.0 keeps SREP 72 Accepted for this reason.

### Sound across transitions (SREP 82)

A transition now mixes the sound of the video layers on its sides, including layers inside a side, by its `audio` attribute, at the eased progress p:
- `crossfade` (the default) gives the outgoing side 1 − p and the incoming side p;
- `equal-power` gives cos(πp/2) and sin(πp/2);
- `cut` switches from one side to the other where p reaches ½;
- `none` leaves both sides at their own volume.

Sequence junctions crossfade, except that a `cut` junction cuts its sound with its picture. The gains multiply the layers' volume automation, one value per frame, interpolated between frames. Before this release `audio` was read by no code, and both sides played at full volume over the whole window.

### Fixes

- **Prewarm and stepsPerFrame memory.** A frame that takes several steps now runs them as one looped pass over two textures, as `iterate` does. Before, every step kept a new texture until the frame was submitted. At 1,000 prewarm steps (1280×720), peak memory fell from 4,088 MB to 258 MB; it is 278 MB at 6,000 steps. The pixels are byte-identical.
- **Odd frame sizes.** New error OUT1: H.264 and H.265 outputs whose frame the chroma format halves must have even dimensions. Validate reports it with the even size to set, and `encode -o` refuses it before rendering. An odd-sized AV1 frame is encoded with libaom.
- **Silent 3D cases.** Two new render-report warnings:
  - `3D-OFF-CAMERA`, for an object3D outside the view in every frame it is drawn;
  - `3D-METAL-NO-ENVIRONMENT`, for metallic 0.5 or more with no environment image.

  As warnings, they fail `encode --strict`.
- **Morph transition.** The luminance displacement described in the README is restored; a rewrite of the transition shader had reduced morph to a crossfade.
- **`--parallel auto`.** The split now depends only on the programme: one chunk per 10 s, at most 12, and one stream under 20 s. The host decides only how many chunks render at once, so the same scene splits the same way on any machine.
  - Auto renders of 20 s or more on software adapters are now joined chunks rather than one stream, and their bytes differ from v0.3.1.
  - On a 40 s scene the joins cost 0.8–1.7 dB PSNR on single frames, within the stream's own frame-to-frame variation, with no visible change.
- **README.** It adds a "Document version 1.6" section covering programs, compute, tonemap, iterate, stepping, parametric, pixel-exact and flock options, with examples that validate.

Download the `linux-x86_64.tar.gz` archive and its `.sha256` file. Verify it with `sha256sum -c scene-render-v0.1.0-linux-x86_64.tar.gz.sha256`, extract it, and run `./scene-render --help` from the extracted directory. The archive contains the binary, README, license, and source commit identifier.

The binary is built on Ubuntu 24.04 and requires Linux x86-64 with glibc 2.39 or newer. Rendering requires a Vulkan driver (Mesa software Vulkan also works); media decoding and encoding require FFmpeg and ffprobe 5.1 or newer. Install fonts appropriate to your scenes. On Ubuntu 24.04: `sudo apt install ffmpeg libvulkan1 mesa-vulkan-drivers fonts-dejavu-core`.

Validation: 120 delivery, CLI, and evaluator tests passed locally, along with formatting, Clippy, and release hygiene checks. The release workflow smoke-tests validation, PNG rendering, and a two-frame FFV1 encode using the packaged binary.

Known CI limitation: the existing workspace CI failed its GPU golden-image comparison before this release work (frame 0 maximum delta E 1.1755). The full workspace suite is not claimed to pass; the release build and its smoke tests are reported separately.
