# Changelog

The release notes of every tagged release, newest first. Generated by `tools/changelog.py` from `RELEASE_NOTES.md`
at each tag; edit `RELEASE_NOTES.md`, not this file. Entries are kept as released, so a document they cite may have
been removed since: "the cinematic impact SREP" is sr-core SREP 40, whose working draft this repository removed after v0.3.2.
Versions up to v0.4.1 were released under the MIT License from github.com/pedroanisio/rs-scene-render.

## Unreleased

- A 1.8 document may hold the `field` node of the fields draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-fields.md`): a `vector`, `scalar` or `complex` field over a box of `width` × `height` pixels showing `xMin`..`xMax` × `yMin`..`yMax`, or over the box and ranges of its enclosing `axes`. Its one source is expressions `u` and `v` of `x`, `y` and `time` (`u` alone for a scalar field); a complex `w` written "re; im", or a built-in complex `function` (`mobius` with eight `coefficients`); a `grid` naming a data source of rows x, y, u, v (x, y, s for a scalar field), bilinear between its lattice nodes; a physics `forceField`, with its animated values at the frame; or `charge`, `current`, `dipole` and `wire` children, a `wire` being a straight segment in the plane from (`x1`, `y1`) to (`x2`, `y2`) with the charge `q` per unit length, whose finite line charge adds to the field, and the current `i` from its first point to its second, whose magnetic field is out of the plane. Its presentations are `arrows` on a grid, along the field or, with `show="poynting"`, along the Poynting vector E × B of the charges, dipoles and wires, evenly spaced `streamlines`, the field `lines` of its charges and the `contours` of a scalar field, each a stroked path in its own `stroke`, `strokeWidth` and dash or else the field's, and a `lic` (line integral convolution of lattice-hash noise, from its `seed` or else the project seed) or `portrait` (hue by arg w, shaded by the fractional part of log_base |w| when `modulus` is set), a raster that fills the box at its `opacity`. The evaluator recomputes every part at every frame; a scalar field draws only its contours and the lic of its gradient. FLD1 to FLD6 check that the source fits the kind, the ranges, that each presentation fits its field, the streamline bounds, that a field outside an axes gives its box and that Poynting arrows have a wire carrying a current and a charge, and V17 refuses fields before 1.8. A field the evaluator cannot evaluate, such as a grid whose rows are not a lattice or a `forceField` that names no force field of physics, is the build error FLDE1. `sr_eval::field_node` lowers the node, and `FrameNode::raster` carries a lic's or portrait's pixels to the renderer. Output changes only for 1.8 documents.
- A 1.8 document may hold the time-frames draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-time-frames.md`): a `space` after `physics`, whose `epoch` is UTC text (Z or a ±hh:mm offset, from 1972-01-01) or a TT or TDB Julian day, whose `rate` is seconds of ephemeris time per second of scene time, about an `origin` body in the `icrf`, `eclipticJ2000` or `bodyFixed` frame of a `frameBody`; an `ephemeris` asset, a JPL SPK file of type 2 segments pinned by `digest="sha256:…"`, or the approximate Keplerian elements (planets 1 to 8 about the Sun, years −3000 to 3000); and `object3D` `body`, `positionFrom` and `orientation`. At each frame an object with `positionFrom="ephemeris"` stands at its body's position about the origin, (x, −z, y) × 1000 × `physics/@pixelsPerMeter` in the frame's axes, and one with `orientation="bodyFixed"` turns with its body's IAU fixed axes; scale and parenting stay the object's. TF1 refuses a file of another kind or digest, TF2 an epoch out of its form or range, TF3 a body-fixed frame without rotation elements, TF4 a placed object without a body, a space or a body the ephemeris holds, TF5 a non-finite rate, an ephemeris that names no asset or an origin the ephemeris does not hold, and V17 the syntax before 1.8; W16 warns that `x`, `y` and `z` are ignored under `positionFrom="ephemeris"`. A frame the ephemeris cannot place, outside the file's span or past the elements' years, fails and names the object. The `sr-space` crate reads SPK files, the time scales, the elements and the frames, and `sr_eval::time_frames` places the bodies. Output changes only for 1.8 documents.
- A 1.8 document may hold the star-fields draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-star-fields.md`): a `catalogue` asset, the draft's gzipped CSV of stars pinned by `digest="sha256:…"`, and a dome light's `starField` child, which draws the catalogue's stars to `limitMagnitude`, each a disc `pointSize` pixels across whose pixels sum to `magnitudeZero` × 10^(−0.4 V) times the star's blackbody colour and the dome's gain, at its direction moved by proper motion to the space's epoch or to `epoch`. The field is behind every surface and turns with the dome; the raster pass draws it, or, when the frame has no 3D pass, it is the frame's background. `constellations="labels"` draws each constellation's abbreviation 14 output pixels high in the default sans-serif face, centred on the mean direction of all its catalogue stars, and `constellations="boundaries"` draws the constellation boundaries one output pixel wide, precessed from 1875 to J2000; both are grey at 0.18 times the dome's gain. SF1 refuses a file of another kind or digest or a proper motion beyond 100 arcseconds a year, SF2 a field off a dome light, a second field or one naming no catalogue asset, SF3 a non-finite `limitMagnitude` (the schema keeps `magnitudeZero` and `pointSize` positive), SF4 an epoch outside −8000 to 12000, and V17 the syntax before 1.8; W17 warns that `epoch` is ignored beside a space. `sr_space` reads the catalogue and its flux, colour, directions and constellations. Output changes only for 1.8 documents.
- A 1.8 document may give a dome light the physical sky of the physical-sky draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-physical-sky.md`): `sky="physical"` is the sky of its `date` and observer (`latitude`, `longitude`, `altitude`, `pressure`, `temperature`, `deltaT`), the sun of the SPA and the moon at their places seen through a Rayleigh, Mie and ozone atmosphere with multiple scattering (`atmosphere`, `aerosol`, `ozone`, `planetRadius`, `atmosphereHeight`, `groundAlbedo`), lit by `sunIrradiance` × `sunIntensity`, an absent `sunIntensity` reading as 1. The raster pass and the frame's background draw the sky with the sun's disc of `sunSize` in `sunColor` and the moon's lit disc, clipped at the horizon, and the scene is lit through a bake that holds the sky and their power. The surfaces of a 3D pass are seen through the air (`aerialPerspective`, true by default) and a height fog of `fogDensity` per metre and `fogHeight` metres, marched along each camera ray or, without the march or the air, in the fog's closed form toward the horizon's radiance; lengths and heights are in metres through `physics/@pixelsPerMeter`, and a height below the planet's surface reads as 0. The path tracer applies both to the first surface each camera ray meets and sees the bake as its sky. A dome's `starField` under the physical sky is dimmed star by star by the transmittance from the observer toward each star, and the ground hides the stars below the horizon (the star-fields draft's rule 6). PSKY2 checks the date's form and years (−2000 to 6000), PSKY3 the observer's and the ground's ranges, PSKY4 refuses a hand-placed sun beside the physical sky, V17 refuses the sky before 1.8, and W18 (the draft's PSKY1) warns that the physical sky's attributes under another sky are ignored; `sunIntensity` has no schema default, so under `sky="gradient"` an absent one still reads as 0. `sr_space` computes the sun and moon, and `sr_3d::atmosphere`, `sr_3d::physical_sky` and `sr_3d::aerial` the sky, its discs and the air along a camera's rays. Output changes only for 1.8 documents.
- A 1.8 document may hold the planets draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-planets.md`): `object3D primitive="planet"`, a spheroid of `segments` around with geodetic vertices, the north pole at −y and the prime meridian at +x, whose radii come from a `body` with IAU radii (the Sun, the eight planets and the Moon) through `physics/@pixelsPerMeter`, else from `radius` and `flattening`; it turns with its body's rotation elements under `orientation="bodyFixed"`. Its maps are equirectangular. With `nightLights` (true by default) an emissive map is masked to the night side across a band of `twilight` degrees of the Sun's elevation, the Sun being a physical sky's, the first directional light's or the space's; `limbDarkening="solar"` darkens the emission toward the limb by the Hestroffer-Magnan law. A `cloudMap` is a shell `cloudHeight` above the ground, the spheroid scaled, drifting eastward by `cloudRotation` degrees per scene second, whose alpha shadows the ground under the first directional light with `cloudShadow` (true by default). A `ringMap` strip draws the annulus from `ringInner` to `ringOuter` equatorial radii in the equatorial plane, both faces lit alike, shadowed by the body and shadowing it. `atmosphere="earth"` wraps the body in the physical sky's atmosphere to `atmosphereHeight` metres, lit by the first directional light's colour × intensity: the raster pass and the path tracer draw the shell for their camera, perspective or orthographic, from a radiance and transmittance table, a visible dome and the stars seen through it, and the surfaces through a march of the air. Under a physical sky's sun the cloud and ring shadows darken the sky's diffuse light in the raster pass and stop the path tracer's rays. Reported as not drawn: those shadows on a ground's specular light in the raster pass, `cloudShadow="false"` under a physical sky's sun in the path tracer, the cloud shadow on a ground with an `occlusionMap` in the raster pass, and the shell under a dome with `sky="physical"`, through an orthographic camera whose rays start inside the air and for a second planet with an atmosphere. PL1 to PL5 check the body, the attributes' primitive, their ranges, the cloud and ring attributes' maps and the solar limb's emission, V17 refuses the primitive before 1.8, and W19 warns that `flattening` is ignored beside a body's radii. `sr_3d::planet` and `sr_3d::planet_shell` compute the body, its shading weights and its air. Output changes only for 1.8 documents.
- The path tracer's denoiser divides each pixel's colour, albedo and normal sums by their counts once, in a preparation pass, instead of in every à-trous tap that reads the pixel. On lavapipe, the denoiser's time on 02-fracture frames 80-84 fell from 290-308 ms to 238 ms per frame (alternated rounds, measured in one binary with the CPU tracer changes below). Frames are the same on lavapipe; on an NVIDIA GPU, one of five frames differs by one 8-bit code in the blue channel (PSNR 107.8 dB), from the driver's float arithmetic (two runs of the code before this change give the same frames there).
- The CPU path tracer answers a shadow ray at the first hit when every material of the scene is opaque and casts shadows, instead of stepping from one nearest hit to the next; it reads a top-level leaf's triangles from a table of corners and edges laid out in leaf order; and it visits the nearer child of a BVH node first. On lavapipe, a 02-fracture frame (80-84, median of four) rendered in 1885 ms and 1920 ms instead of 2001-2117 ms in alternated rounds, measured with the denoiser change above and the first two of these three; the near-first order keeps the same frames. Frames are the same on 02-fracture and 06-marx-engels-clay.
- Clay extraction reads its roughness noise from lattice tables built once per mesh over the grid's bounds, instead of hashing the eight lattice corners of every sample twice. The noise is the same bit for bit, so meshes and frames are the same.
- Encoding on a software adapter renders two automatic segments at once on a machine of 8 cores or more, instead of one. The automatic split depends on the output's duration alone, so the segments, and the file, are those of a render one segment at a time. The 736-frame 1280x720 06-marx-engels-clay film encodes on lavapipe to the same frames in 63.11 s and 68.11 s instead of 75.23 s and 75.71 s (alternated runs, with every other change in this section; user CPU 601 s and 608 s instead of 587 s and 587 s, peak RSS 2.33 to 2.34 GiB instead of 1.88 to 1.99 GiB, the second segment's renderer).
- A positive matte-choke `amount` erodes the matte and a negative one dilates it, whatever the effect's `position`, as the schema and SREP 29 define. Before, a positive amount at the default position (`outside`) dilated and at `inside` eroded, and a negative amount reversed both, so a matte-choke with a positive amount and no position now erodes where it grew. sr-core's case `srep-0029-matte-choke-grow` passes.
- `resample="bicubic"` and `resample="mitchell"` read the sixteen texels of a magnified sample with their exact weights instead of nine bilinear fetches, and a sample within 1/1024 texel of a texel centre is at that centre (t = 0), as SREP 32 states. Before, the interpolated position fell a few ulp short of the centre, so the anti-ringing clamp took the texel to the left: on sr-core's case `srep-0032-resample-mitchell`, pixels 34, 38, 42, 46 and 50 were 28, 0, 125, 128 and 250 where SREP 32 gives 0, 28.6, 128, 139.3 and 255. The case passes. Other magnified bicubic and Mitchell samples can change too, since the weights are no longer the sampler's bilinear ones.
- On a software adapter, shadow maps are at most 1024 × 1024: the rasteriser shades every shadow texel on the CPU, and a 2048 × 2048 map, the default `shadowMapSize`, costs four times as many. GPU adapters keep the asked size within the 512 MB budget. On lavapipe, perf_3d's frames (four spot lights at 2048, lights orbiting) have a PSNR of 50.05 to 50.69 dB against those with 2048 × 2048 maps, the change lying at shadow edges, and a frame (0-30, median) renders in 284.7, 234.1, 263.4 and 261.3 ms instead of 302.5, 303.4, 289.4 and 271.6 ms in alternated runs. 06-marx-engels-clay's frames are the same: its one shadow map is drawn once and kept.
- The CPU path tracer's top-level BVH has four children per node: the binned-SAH binary tree is collapsed so that each node holds the children of a binary node with its largest inner children opened, their boxes side by side so one pass tests all four. The walk visits the hit children nearest entry first and skips a child whose box begins beyond a hit found since it was queued. Its stack holds every child a tree of the build's depth can queue; the binary walk dropped children when its stack of 64 was full. On lavapipe, a 02-fracture frame (80-84, median of four) renders in 1540, 1515 and 1485 ms instead of 2048, 1834 and 1846 ms in alternated rounds. Frames are the same on 02-fracture and 06-marx-engels-clay.
- Threads that submit and wait on one GPU device at once do not panic inside wgpu-core with "queue empty with a fence below the wait index": wgpu-core 30.0.1 reads the fence and then triages the queue as two steps, so a second poll or a submit, which triages the queue too, between them could empty the queue first. The engine polls and submits to each device from one thread at a time, in waits of at most 10 ms so that a long wait does not hold back the other threads (`sr_gpu::gpu::poll_wait` and `sr_gpu::gpu::submit`).
- Through an orthographic camera the raster pass shades every surface point along the view axis, the line of sight of every pixel of an orthographic projection. Before, it took the direction from the point toward the camera's position, so specular highlights, Fresnel terms and image-based reflections leaned toward that position, more so the nearer the camera stood: with the camera 50 units in front of a sphere of radius 100, the surface point 50 units off the axis was shaded as if seen 38° off the axis. Frames of lit surfaces through an orthographic camera change; the path tracer already traced along the axis. A visible dome's sky behind an orthographic camera is its radiance along the axis over the whole frame, in the 3D pass's background and in the frame's background without a 3D pass, as the path tracer's rays that miss every surface read it; before, each pixel read the direction from the camera's position through its point on the far plane, so the sky's gradient showed across the frame.
- An `exr-sequence` output keeps values above 1.0: the renderer hands FFmpeg the three float planes of an opaque EXR (`gbrpf32le`, `sr_media::encode::InputFormat::Gbrpf32`) or the four of one with alpha, which FFmpeg writes as they are. Before, an opaque EXR was handed four planes, and FFmpeg's conversion to the three it writes passes through 16-bit integers, so every value was clipped to 1.0 and quantised; `twoPass` on an EXR sequence went through a 16-bit intermediate and was clipped the same way. An EXR sequence encodes in one pass, and a `maxFileSize` on a sequence output is refused, since a sequence has no bitrate to fit a file size (before, the fit failed on a missing file).
- The diffuse light of a sky's sun and moon comes from their own directions. The bake of a gradient or physical sky projects its diffuse light from a 32 × 16 copy of the sky, whose texel carried each disc up to half its 11° width away, so a Lambert surface lit by the sun of a black gradient sky was off by up to 10 % of its brightest light; the share of a physical sky's sun over its lobe varied by 15 % across a ball. The bake now projects the sky without its discs and adds each disc as a point of its power at its direction, and the specular mips spread it over its texels as before. Frames of surfaces lit by a gradient or physical sky's sun or moon change.
- A material's diffuse keeps the light its specular layer does not reflect, one minus `specular` times the Fresnel term, in the raster pass and both path tracers, as the schema defines `specular` as the strength of the specular reflection. Before, the diffuse lost the whole Fresnel term at every `specular`, so a material with `specular="0"` reflected nothing yet darkened toward grazing light: a sphere of such a material lit from 135° off the line of sight was 4.4 % darker at one end of its lit crescent than a Lambert surface. Frames of materials with `specular` below 1 change; at the default 1 they are the same.

## v0.8.0 — 2026-10-25

This release accepts documents of version 1.8, which carry six drafts of sr-core: random streams, media phase functions, plots, space transforms, semantic TeX and reactive values. Simulations of a 1.8 document draw from the lattice-hash streams. A medium takes a Henyey-Greenstein, Rayleigh, Draine or fog phase function. An `axes` node and an axes object draw grids, ticks and plots of expressions. The `matrix`, `map` and `complex` modifiers map outlines through functions of the plane. A formula's tagged parts animate and morph. `value` assets hold numbers that step in fixed time steps or fold over an index, and charts follow them. Version 1.8 follows sr-core drafts that sr-core has not yet accepted, so a later sr-core release may change what a 1.8 document means. A document of version 1.7 or earlier renders as in v0.7.5, except for formula placement and the rounding listed below.

### Random streams

- Documents of version 1.8 are accepted: the version of the random-streams draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-random-streams.md`), vendored as sr-core 1.7.0 plus the one `1.8` value of the version enumeration. Their simulations draw from the lattice-hash streams of the draft. A draw is S(e, p, t, i) = U(e, p + t · 2³², i), addressed by the element seed e = E(project, id, `@seed`, "stream") (the CRC-32 of the four, so the project seed enters every simulation), a purpose p per drawn quantity, the step t and the index i. 2D particle emitters, flocks, slimes, erosion, 3D particle emitters and the ejecta of their crater bursts, whitewater, pyro turbulence and force-field noise draw this way. A whitewater and a pyro have no id of their own: they use their ocean's or object's under the purposes `stream:whitewater` and `stream:pyro`. Where the schema gives `@seed` a default, the default is the `@seed` of the element seed. A document of version 1.7 or earlier draws as before, bit for bit; so do, at 1.8, the agents of programs, program nodes, clay, voxel crater cuts and ocean wave spectra, which the draft leaves out. `sr_vector::d24` adds `stream`, `stream_signed`, `stream_normal` and `unpack`, and the draft's 37 reference values are tests. Output changes only for 1.8 documents, which were refused before.

### Media

- A medium of a 1.8 document takes a phase function from the media draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-media.md`): `phase` is `hg` (the default, Henyey-Greenstein with `anisotropy`), `rayleigh`, `draine` (with `anisotropy` and `phaseAlpha`, default 1) or `fog` (the fitted mixture of a Henyey-Greenstein and a Draine lobe for a `dropletDiameter` of 5 to 50 µm, default 10). The schema and the native rules check the draft's MED1 to MED3, and MED4 refuses the three attributes before version 1.8. `sr_volume::medium::Phase` evaluates and samples each kind: Henyey-Greenstein by inversion, Draine by bisection of its cumulative, fog by drawing a lobe first. The cumulative of Draine is written in a form that never divides by g; the draft's form loses its digits for 1e-6 ≤ |g| ≲ 1e-4. The path tracer packs each medium's phase as a mixture of the two lobes and samples it the same way, and a medium whose phase is not isotropic is lit from the directional dome grids. On the GPU the Henyey-Greenstein denominator 1 + g² − 2gμ is now evaluated as (1 − g)² + 2g(1 − μ), which keeps its digits at a forward peak, so a medium without the attributes can differ from before by f32 rounding.

### Plots

- A 1.8 document may hold the `axes` node of the plots draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-plots.md`): a box of `width` × `height` pixels showing the data ranges `xMin`..`xMax` × `yMin`..`yMax` as a `cartesian` plane, a `complex` plane, `polar` circles and rays, or a `number-line`, with `plot` children of the kinds `explicit` (y of x), `parametric` (x and y of t), `polar` (r of θ) and `implicit` (f(x, y) = `level`, by marching squares). The evaluator lowers an `axes` to path shapes for its grid, axis lines, ticks and plots, a group of its box that clips its child nodes, and its tick labels, which the renderer typesets as formulas in the axes' `stroke` colour at `labelSize`. The ranges, steps and the plots' expressions are read at every frame, so an animated range moves the plots and the children. A child node's `x` and `y` are data coordinates and its `anchorX` and `anchorY` data offsets; its other attributes stay in pixels. A frame at which an animated range closes (`xMin` ≥ `xMax`) reports the problem and draws the axes' parts empty. `sr_eval::axes` computes each frame's parts from the draft's rules in `sr_eval::plots`.
- A 1.8 document may hold an `object3D primitive="axes"` of the same draft: its `axes3D` maps data to the object's local space, the data centre at its origin and +y data up, and draws the three axis lines and their ticks as cylinders of `lineWidth` and `tickLength` in its `color`. Each `plot3D` is a tube of `tubeRadius` and `tubeSegments` around the curve (x, y, z)(t), sampled at `samples` values of t from `t0` to `t1` and read at every frame, in its `color`. A ring whose point is not finite is left out with its two cells, and the tube's parallel-transport frames run over the finite points. The colours are vertex colours, which multiply the object's material, and a `plot3D` with a `material` is drawn with that material instead. The `axes3D` attributes are read from the document, not animated. A colour given as a token or a paint reference draws white, leaving the material's colour unchanged. A `plot3D` whose points make no tube (fewer than two distinct finite points) is reported as a problem and left out. `FrameNode::param_parts` holds the meshes of the plots with their own material.

### Space transforms

- A 1.8 document may give a `deform` the modifier types `matrix`, `map` and `complex` of the space-transforms draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-space-transforms.md`), and a `group` a `deform`. `matrix` is SVG's `matrix(a, b, c, d, e, f)` about `centerX`/`centerY`; `map` gives the point's new data coordinates as expressions `x` and `y` of `u` and `v` (+v up, `unit` pixels a unit), read at every frame; `complex` applies one of ten complex functions (`square` to `joukowski`, and `mobius` with eight `coefficients`) in the same frame. With one of them in a node's chain, a shape's outline is mapped before its trim, fill, stroke and markers, so a stroke keeps its width; each segment is split at its midpoint until its mapped chord is within `tolerance` (0.25 px by default), at most 10 times, and a point that maps to infinity breaks the outline into open pieces. Text and vector layer content maps its drawn outlines the same way, and a chain of the fourteen existing types deforms as before. A group's deform maps all its descendants in the group's space, after their own and centred on the union of their boxes. The rules SPT1 and SPT2 check the types' attributes and that a Möbius map is invertible, and V17 refuses the types and a group's deform before 1.8. An image layer under one of the three types deforms its grid mesh by the same chain, without the outline breaking of the shapes. `FrameNode::deform_maps` and `FrameGraph::point_map` give a map's point function.

### Semantic TeX

- A 1.8 document may use the semantic-tex draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-semantic-tex.md`). A formula's TeX may tag parts with `\part{name}{body}`, and the tags change nothing in the layout. A `formulaAnimator` child of a formula layer moves, scales, turns, fades, recolours, strokes or blurs the glyphs of one part about the part's box. It takes `x`, `y`, `scale`, `scaleX`, `scaleY`, `rotation`, `opacity`, `fill`, `stroke`, `strokeWidth`, `blur`, `anchorX` and `anchorY`, animated as a `textAnimator`'s are and combined as one with a selection amount of 1. A nested part moves with its container. A part the formula lacks is an error of the layer. A `formulaMorph` asset turns its `from` formula into its `to` formula at its animated `progress`. Both are typeset at its `size` and share the fit of their union box. Outermost parts pair by `name` or `tex`, and pairs with equal TeX move along the box path. Pairs with different TeX morph their outlines, resampled to 64 points by arc length, aligned and paired by centroid. Unpaired parts and untagged glyphs fade. A `shape` with `morph="true"` interpolates the keys of its `animate property="path"` by the same path morph. The rules FRM1 and FRM2 check that an animator sits on a formula layer and that a morph names formulas, and V17 refuses all of it before 1.8. INERT-I18 reports a `morph` without path keys. `sr_text::parts`, `sr_text::formula_anim` and `sr_vector::morph` hold the draft's rules. Output changes only for 1.8 documents, which were refused before.

### Reactive values

- A 1.8 document may hold the `value` assets of the reactive-values draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-reactive-values.md`): a named number or number list that draws nothing. `prop("id.value")`, `propAtTime` and `link source="id.value"` read it from anywhere in the document. Its keys, expression or link give it as for any property. With `update` and `step` it is a time fold: from its keyed value at `start` it advances in fixed steps of `step` seconds, and its value at t is the state after ⌊(t − start) / step + 10⁻⁹⌋ steps. With `between="linear"` it interpolates between steps. With `update` and `count` it is an index fold, whose update runs `count` times at every frame. An update reads `value`, `index`, `count`, `time`, `dt`, `random()`, which draws by step instead of by frame, and `draw(channel, i)`, a draw shared by every value of one seed at one step. Time folds of one `step` and `start` that read each other step together in gather form: each reads the others' state before the step. A read across steps or starts, or of an index fold inside an update, is the build error RVE1. A time fold of more than 1,000,000 steps by the composition's end is RVE2. An update that gives a non-finite state is a render error. The evaluator keeps a checkpoint of each system every 1024 steps, so a frame rendered alone equals the frame played in sequence, exactly. A chart `series` whose `values` is linked or expressed reads them at every frame, so a counter chart shows a live count. The rules RV1 and RV2 check that `update` goes with exactly one of `step` and `count` and that `between` belongs to a time fold, and V17 refuses values before 1.8. `sr_eval::fold` holds the draft's rules, and the draft's numeric table, its Fourier centre of mass and its Einstein solid are tests. Output changes only for 1.8 documents, which were refused before.

### Fixes

- A formula is centred in its box on its layout's extent. Before, it was drawn higher than its box's middle by its ascent less its descent, so a formula asset sat high in its box and could leave it at the top. Every `formula` asset draws lower than before by that amount.
- `watch` renders a change once the watched files settle: after a stamp changes it polls until two polls one interval apart agree. Before, a writer that truncated a file and then wrote it could be caught between the two, and `watch` rendered the empty document, reported an XML error and, as the last run of `--max-runs`, exited with code 1. With a scene held truncated from 45 ms to 75 ms after the ready line at the default 50 ms interval, the previous loop failed 30 of 30 runs and this one none of 30.

## v0.7.5 — 2026-10-24

This release keeps what renderers make from one frame to the next and shares it between them, bounds what simulations and caches hold over a long run, and moves delivery work off the render thread. 3D passes, vector raster jobs, particle batches, video layers, looped shaders and the path tracer keep their buffers and bind groups between submits; renderers on one GPU device share their pipelines; the compositor batches its draws by dependency. The 2D physics world and emitter keep a bounded set of checkpoints, the 3D engine and the volume cache drop their least recently used entries, and audio sources, mesh sequences and the geo caches hold one copy of their data. Delivery reads the flash check one frame late, evaluates motion blur samples in parallel, packs planar YUV on the GPU and reads two-pass intermediates in FFmpeg, and `render` writes PNG files on a writer thread. A path traced scene with water and media compiles one march through the media, which cuts the NVIDIA OpenGL link time of its scattering variants by about 40 %. Each change is measured below. The 736-frame 1280x720 06-marx-engels-clay film, whose time is the 3D passes and x264 on the CPU, encodes on lavapipe to the same frames as with v0.7.4 in 69.64 s and 70.97 s instead of 69.80 s and 76.75 s (user CPU 573 s and 575 s instead of 570 s and 573 s, peak RSS 2.02 to 2.10 GiB instead of 2.02 to 2.04 GiB). The changes that can alter output are listed at the end. The CLI output schema is 1.3.0, and the vendored schema is still sr-core 1.7.0.

### Path tracing

- On a GPU adapter the path tracer keeps the scratch its tiles work in from one pass to the next: the sums and guides of each pixel, the stand-in buffer, and the denoiser's two halves with their bind groups, grown only when a tile needs more and dropped when the tile budget changes. Before, every pass created and released those buffers, four of 32 bytes per pixel, about 118 MB for a 1280x720 pass. A CPU adapter still makes them for each pass: they are host memory its allocator recycles, and keeping them only raised the peak. The numbers are under the next entry, which was measured with this one.
- The CPU path tracer keeps what it builds from a scene for the passes over the same geometry and textures: the dense instance records, the atlas packed into words and its own top-level BVH, by geometry identity and atlas key, at most four scenes and 16 MB in all. It also reads the dome from the texels the engine keeps instead of copying them into every pass, 32 MB for a 2K dome. `Stats3.pt_cpu_scenes` counts the scenes a pass built: 0 when a kept one served or the GPU traced. With the kept scratch above, on 02-fracture frames 60-120 on an RTX 3050 (serial, alternated runs) `pt_pack_seconds` fell from 8.5-8.6 ms to 4.2-4.6 ms per frame, the frame median from 77.8-79.4 ms to 76.2-76.8 ms and peak host RSS from 930-936 MB to 878-879 MB; GPU time per frame is unchanged (63.3-63.9 ms). On lavapipe (pipelined) the frame median went from 1902-1921 ms to 1805-1877 ms and peak RSS from 1332-1341 MB to 1347-1356 MB: the fracture pieces move every frame, so every pass is another scene to the tracer there, and the kept scenes add their bound to the peak. Frames are the same.
- A path traced scene with water and media (smoke, fog, volumes) compiles one march through the media instead of two: the water's march, which dims the media's light by the water in front of it, also marches the media outside the water, where it absorbs nothing. NVIDIA's OpenGL driver inlines every call, so each march carried its own copy of the media's scattering and of the scattering random walk. With the driver's shader cache off on an RTX 3050, a lit water variant links in 21.9 s instead of 28.9 s and with scattering in 35.6 s instead of 59.0 s, and its peak host memory falls from 4.63 GB to 2.47 GB; with a volume grid, 33.0 s instead of 42.4 s and 48.3 s instead of 77.6 s (5.30 GB to 2.70 GB). Variants without water are unchanged. Frames are the same.

### Evaluation

- Per-frame evaluation does less work that nothing reads. A node's keyframes, links and expressions are left unevaluated while the node is off screen and nothing reads them. Nodes are still evaluated when something reads them off screen: an expression, a link or z-order; a node another one is parented to, and that node's ancestors; or a transition. Constraints are looked for only on the nodes that have one, found when the program is built, not by walking every node's children each frame. Each slot keeps the noise channel of its property instead of hashing the property name on every `wiggle()` call. A number wiggled by a number takes a scalar path that allocates nothing. A map's fitted camera is kept for the program, so `geo()` no longer reads the fit files' metadata on every call. The frame's output lists are reserved for the nodes alive in it. On sr-eval's benchmark scene (1,020 nodes, 5,500 slots, 500 expressions, all on screen, no maps), the frame median fell from 0.864 ms to 0.805 ms: ten alternated pairs, each one faster. The off-screen and map savings scale with a document's off-screen nodes and `geo()` calls, which that scene does not have. Frames are the same.

### Simulation

- A 2D particle that collides is tested only against the colliders whose bounding box holds it. The boxes are found once per physics step, when the step's colliders are kept for the particles. Before, every live colliding particle was projected onto every collider on every step. On the physics benchmark scene (about 1,200 live sparks against 205 colliders), evaluating frame 300 went from 1.69 s to 1.17 s (five alternated runs each), with the same graph.
- A 3D particle emitter reserves room for the particles it carries rather than for its `maxParticles`. With the default cap that was about 1.8 MB on every fixed step and in the frame it publishes. The death times of the live particles are heaped in one pass.
- A smoke domain's solid mask is computed on every core. A domain with no colliders skips the mask altogether. In the profile of a 128x104x128 domain, the obstacle stage went from 24 ms to 14 ms a step with a slab and a sphere, and from 8 ms to 0.1 ms with none. The mask and the solid cells' face velocities are the same.

### Memory

- The 2D physics world and the 2D particle emitter keep a bounded set of replay checkpoints, as the 3D world and the other simulations already did: at most 65 under the shared 256 MB budget, thinned by doubling their spacing. Before, they kept one a second for the whole run. Evaluating the last frame of a 600-second copy of the physics benchmark scene (one run each) peaked at 912,404 KiB of RSS before and 115,740 KiB after, with the same graph.
- The 3D world charges a collider shape, or the kept cells of a body that can be cut, once for all the checkpoints that share it rather than once per checkpoint, and measures it only when a checkpoint first holds it. What the checkpoints share with the initial checkpoint, which is outside the budget, is not charged. A world with a 5,000-triangle ground used to thin to two checkpoints under an 8 MB budget, and now keeps one a second.
- A mesh sequence's blended frame shares the texture pixels of the frames it blends rather than copying them. With two 2048x2048 textures on a 50,000-vertex model, a blended sample went from 24.3 ms to 6.5 ms, and it no longer holds a 32 MB copy.
- Audio sources are decoded straight into one buffer per channel as FFmpeg writes them. Before, a source was held twice at its peak: the encoded bytes beside the samples, then the interleaved samples beside the per-channel buffers. A 20-second stereo source held 16.1 MB at once for 7.7 MB of audio; it now holds the audio once, plus at most a sixty-fourth and a second of room, which is given back.
- The process-wide geo caches (features, fitted cameras, tile archives and style files) keep one reading per file and replace it when the file changes on disk. Before, every revision of a file a preview session saw stayed cached. Each cache is also capped at 256 entries. The tile and elevation caches hold their archive weakly, so a replaced archive's address cannot be taken for the new one's.
- The grids of a cut body of cells are dropped with the revisions the 3D world drops from its budgeted history.
- The 3D engine drops the meshes and textures no pass of a frame used, least recently used first, once the idle ones of each kind exceed 256 MB (shared between delivery workers like the other budgets). Before, every mesh and texture a session uploaded was kept until the renderer closed. What a frame draws is never dropped, whatever the budget. `--stats` reports the count as `assets_evicted`.
- The volume cache drops the least recently used decoded volumes and baked frames until a new one fits its 256 MB budget or its 64 entries of a kind, instead of clearing every entry. A volume that does not fit the budget alone is used for the frame but not kept, and pushes nothing out.
- Renderers on one GPU device share their pipelines instead of each compiling and holding its own. The compositor's layouts, shader and pipelines are shared per device and working format. The 3D engine's are shared per device, G-buffer format and sample count, the path tracer's and the rasterizer's per device and format, and the video engine's and the built-in effects' per device. Custom shader effects keep their own. A pipeline is still compiled the first time a draw uses it, now once for every renderer on the device, and the shared set is dropped when the last renderer on the device that uses it is. Parallel delivery workers render on one device, and so do concurrent tests. On lavapipe, which holds about 35 MiB for a compositor pipeline, the sr-gpu test binaries run with 20 test threads in two alternated runs each: `volume_grids` peaked at 536-539 MB of RSS instead of 2.38-2.44 GB and took 11.5-12.1 s instead of 92.5-93.9 s; `include_scope` peaked at 559-567 MB instead of 1.88-1.91 GB and took 2.3 s instead of 17.2 s. Frames are the same.

### 3D

- Each 3D pass writes its frame, light, tile, shadow-matrix, object, material and draw-order data into buffers the engine keeps from one submit to the next, grown when a pass needs more, and keeps the material bind groups with them, remade only when the material buffer grew or a material's maps changed. Before, every pass created those seven buffers and all of its material bind groups. Passes recorded between two submits each get their own set, as a `queue.write_buffer` lands before the next submit's first command; the renderer tells the engine after each submit, and up to 16 sets are kept. `Stats3.buffers_created` and `Stats3.material_binds_created` count what a pass made: 0 for a warm pass. With the entries below, on an RTX 3050 (serial, alternated runs of 48 frames) perf_3d's `submit_ms` went from 0.75-0.79 ms to 0.66 ms and its frame median from 4.25-4.40 ms to 4.12-4.13 ms with GPU time unchanged (3.1-3.3 ms); perf_text and 02-fracture frames 60-120 are unchanged within run-to-run noise on both adapters (fracture 74.9-76.2 ms before, 74.7-75.8 ms after on the GPU; 1736-1758 ms before, 1745-1754 ms after on lavapipe), and on lavapipe perf_3d's median spread between pipelined runs (452-580 ms before, 288-559 ms after) is wider than any difference between the binaries. `buffers_created` is 0 on every warm frame of the three scenes. Frames are byte-identical on both adapters.
- The rest of what a 3D pass made for itself goes the same way: the vertex buffers of deformed (skinned or morphed) draws, the splat sorts' parameters and radix scratch, and the splat draws' object uniforms are kept in the pass's set or by the engine and written in place, and the IES atlas is kept while the lights hold the same profiles instead of being packed and uploaded every pass. Under f32 precision the f16 target a 3D pass draws into comes from the texture pool, which now keys its free textures by size and format, instead of being made for the frame and dropped. Morphing and skinning run per vertex on the rayon pool with the same sums in the same order, so the vertices are the same to the bit.

### Vector

- Each vector raster job appends its parameters, commands, ranges, pieces, backdrops and active tiles to six arenas the raster stage keeps between submits, at 256-byte offsets, bound by offset and size; an arena is replaced by a larger one when a job does not fit, and all are rewound after each submit. Before, every job made six buffers. `RenderStats.buffers_created` (`--stats`) counts the buffers a frame made for its 3D passes, particle batches and raster jobs: 0 for a warm frame.

### Particles

- Each particle batch writes its instances into a buffer the engine keeps between submits (up to 64), grown when a batch needs more, instead of a new buffer every frame.

### Compositor

- The compositor batches its draws by dependency rather than by adjacency. A draw whose blend mode reads the backdrop goes into the first batch after the batches of every earlier draw over any of its pixels, and a normal draw into the batch of the latest such draw, so draws that share no pixel share one render pass and one set of backdrop copies whatever lies between them in the composition; two draws over the same pixel keep their paint order. Before, a batch took only consecutive draws, and any normal draw ended it for the backdrop draws after it. The earlier draws to test come from a grid over the batch run's extent with about one cell per median draw (at most 64 a side), tested exactly on their bounds, so a run of thousands of small disjoint draws costs a test per neighbour and a run of a few hundred layer-sized draws a plain pairwise test (84 µs a frame on perf_layers). `--stats` and `render --bench --json` report the passes as `compositor_passes`. On perf_layers (1080p60, 200 layers in 33 blend modes, RTX 3050, 120 frames, three alternated runs each) the frame went from 175 compositor passes to 46, GPU time per frame from 12.6-12.7 ms to 12.2 ms and the frame median from 14.6-14.9 ms to 14.0-14.2 ms; the 174 backdrop copies remain. The first 24 frames are byte-identical to the previous binary's.
- A video layer's frames are converted, mixed and warped in passes recorded while the frame is planned and submitted once, ahead of the frame's own command buffer, into textures from the renderer's pool that go back to it when the frames leave the cache. The plane textures and conversion uniforms, the luma pyramids, flow levels, patch buffer and bindings of the optical flow, and the parameters of the blends are kept by size and reused by the next frame; a frame that converts two frames of one size takes two sets. Before, every decoded frame made a texture, a uniform buffer and a bind group and was submitted on its own, a mix or warp the same again, and an optical flow made and dropped its twelve luma levels, six flow levels and patch buffer and submitted itself, and the frame textures were destroyed when they left the cache. `textures_created` (`--stats`) now counts video frame textures: a playing clip's first frames, then 0. A time-displacement effect's flow is recorded into the frame's command buffer instead of flushing it and submitting on its own. On an RTX 3050 (1080p60, 120 frames, three alternated runs each, machine load 7-9 from other work) a clip at half speed with optical flow went from a frame median of 1.6-3.9 ms to 0.5-0.9 ms and a submit of 0.83-0.88 ms to 0.18-0.59 ms; with frame mixing the submit went from 0.57-0.60 ms to 0.26-0.48 ms with the frame median within its run-to-run spread (1.0-1.6 ms); a clip at full speed is unchanged (1.56-1.59 ms before, 1.55-1.57 ms after), its frame being the plane upload. Frames 20 to 31 of both blended clips are byte-identical to the previous binary's.
- A looped stateful shader (`<iterate>`, and the steps of a frame of a `PERSISTENT` pass) records its steps in chunks of up to 64: the uniform blocks of a chunk's passes go into one uniform buffer the effects engine keeps between frames in one write, each pass is drawn at its block's offset with one of a few bind groups (a pass reads the same textures at every step of one parity), and the next chunk takes the same place once the chunk's command buffer is submitted. The fixed effect passes of a frame and single custom passes write the same buffer. An `until="converged"` check compares each persistent buffer with a copy kept from the last check in a compute pass that reduces the largest change over every channel of every texel to one number per buffer (a NaN counts as no change, as before), reading back a few bytes; the copies are kept by size and format. Before, every step of every pass made a uniform buffer and a bind group, and a check read every persistent buffer back in full and compared it on the CPU. `buffers_created` (`--stats`) now counts the effects engine's buffers: one or two at the first frame, then 0. On an RTX 3050 (1280x720, a 5-tap Jacobi relaxation in two passes, 12 frames, two alternated runs each, the machine under a test suite) 2000 steps a frame went from a frame median of 552-559 ms to 517-521 ms with the CPU time of the frame from 274-334 ms to 178-185 ms, and 400 steps with a convergence check every 100 from 326-330 ms to 125-126 ms (GPU time 316-321 ms to 118-119 ms). The first four frames of both scenes, and three frames of the effects scene, are byte-identical to the previous binary's.
- The compositor keeps the bind groups of its (backdrop, matte) texture pairs and of its cached mask coverage between frames, keyed by the textures' ids (every texture now carries one), for 4 frames after their last use; before, they were made again every frame, the dummy pair included. `--stats` reports them as `bind_groups_created`: a few at the first frame of a composition, then 0. The change detection of a frame hashes each node's subtree once per target space (it was hashed from its root, then again from every isolated group, matte and effect source holding it) and each element's animated state once, reads a text node's animator depth once instead of twice, and hashes particle positions, soft-body offsets and connector paths as they stream instead of collecting them. On an RTX 3050 (1080p60, 120 frames, two alternated runs each) perf_layers, perf_text and perf_effects are within their run-to-run spread (13.8 ms, 2.6 ms and 3.3-3.5 ms frame medians before and after): the work removed had measured in the tens of microseconds a frame. Four frames of each scene are byte-identical to the previous binary's.

### Delivery

- The flash check's 48x27 grid is collected one frame after it is submitted, on a second mapped buffer, instead of being read back while the render thread waits for it. Only the last frame of a programme waits. On perf_layers (1080p60, 600 frames, RTX 3050) with the flash check on, the encode went from 11.2 s to 8.9-9.2 s, level with the same scene with no check (8.65 s); the grid's cells are the same.
- The sample times of a motion blur are evaluated in parallel on the rayon pool once the blur knows it will draw them all, and the seconds they take reach the encode report as `subframe_seconds` (summed over workers) and the text summary as `shutter sub-frames`. The counterfactual probes of a contrast check still evaluate serially. Frames are the same; on perf_3d with 32 samples and perf_effects with 16, evaluation is under a millisecond a frame, so frame times do not move.
- Outputs whose encoder reads planar YUV get those planes packed on the GPU: ProRes yuv422p10le (4444 profiles yuv444p10le, with alpha yuva444p10le), DNxHR yuv422p or yuv422p10le, FFV1 with alpha yuva444p10le, and x265, SVT-AV1, libaom, rav1e and VP9 the 4:2:0 planes at 8 or 10 bits; libx264 and the hardware encoders keep NV12 and P010, which they read as they are. Before, those outputs read back 16-bit RGBA (8 bytes a pixel) or NV12 and FFmpeg converted every frame in software. The 4:2:0 planes are the NV12 and P010 planes deinterleaved, and the 4:2:2 and 4:4:4 codes match the RGB path's within one level. On perf_layers (1080p60, 600 frames, RTX 3050) to ProRes HQ the readback stage went from 2.9 s to 1.9 s; elapsed (30-31 s) and FFmpeg's CPU (339-341 s user) stay where prores_ks holds them.
- Each pass of a two-pass or size-fitted encode reads the lossless intermediate itself. Before, a second FFmpeg decoded it to raw frames that the delivery thread copied into the encoder's pipe. The encoder counts the frames FFmpeg reports and fails the pass, leaving the previous file, unless the intermediate decoded to exactly the frames rendered, so a short or unreadable intermediate still produces no output. On perf_layers (1080p60, 600 frames, RTX 3050) encoded two-pass to H.264 the file is byte-identical, the run starts 4 FFmpeg processes instead of 6 and its system CPU falls from 7.6 s to 3.1-3.2 s; elapsed and user CPU stay where libx264 holds them.
- The output layer's texts are measured for contrast at the frames the check's budget schedules, over the delivered picture, like the composition's own texts, and they share that budget of 256 probes (32 a text). Before, a layer with text was probed on every frame of the delivery: the layer rendered again per text, composited and read back while the render thread waited. The report's sampled-contrast note names layer texts like any others. On perf_layers with a two-text output layer (1080p60, 600 frames, RTX 3050) the encode with the contrast check on went from 59-74 s to 14.3-14.8 s, where the same encode with the check off takes 14 s. The ratios a layer text reports are the minimum over its measured frames rather than all of them.
- `render` converts each frame to 16-bit display sRGB with its alpha on the output stage, reads one frame back while the next renders, and encodes the PNG files on a writer thread at most two frames behind, where before it read every frame back as float pixels, converted them on the CPU and encoded the file before rendering the next. `render --json` reports `stage_seconds` as an encode does (evaluation, render, the wait for a frame's readback, writing the files) and the text summary prints them as `stages:`. A frame that cannot be written still fails the run with the frames before it complete and none after it, and `--changed-only` records a frame's fingerprint only once its file is there. On perf_layers (1080p60, 120 frames, RTX 3050) a PNG sequence took 59.9 s at 8 bits and 67.1 s at 16; it takes 3.3 s and 6.2 s, with the files the same size and 119 of the 120 8-bit frames byte-identical (the other within one code).

### Fixes

- The first frame of a node motion-blurred with a shutter that opens before time 0 (the default `shutterPhase` of −90°) blurs over the samples before time 0, holding each property at its first key as the schema defines. Before, a node whose time window starts at 0 was left out of those samples, so the first frame drew it sharp. At 10 fps and 180°, a 20 px square moving 100 px a second draws 25 px wide with 8 partial pixels; it drew 20 px wide and sharp.
- DNxHR outputs are found where FFmpeg builds the encoder (probed with the `dnxhr_lb` profile), and MXF outputs take frame rates outside the muxer's broadcast list (`-strict unofficial`, which leaves a file at a listed rate byte-identical).
- A vector frame with a layer (a mask or a matte) and no backdrop rows no longer panics with `range end index 16 out of range for slice of length 0` in the raster stage. The layer's push and pop commands named backdrop row 0 instead of none, and hashing a tile for partial redraws read 16 rows there. A traced geo outline that stops short of the tiles to its right, such as sr-core's `srep-0036-geo-outline-p125`, was such a frame. It now draws as scene-render 0.5.0 drew it. The pixels of other frames are unchanged: the shader reads a command's backdrop only for coverage.

### Changes that can alter output from v0.7.4

- At an exact vertex of a motion path, an auto-oriented node takes the direction of the segment that leaves the vertex, skipping segments of zero length, as SREP 58 states; at a jump its point is the next subpath's start. Before, constant-speed progress took the direction of the segment that arrives there, so a bar on `M200 100 L300 100 L300 200` at progress 0.5 lay horizontal where it now stands vertical. Spatial keyframe paths and 3D follow-path cameras sample the same paths. Progress between vertices, and the end of a path, are unchanged.
- The PNG frames `render` writes come from the output stage's 16-bit conversion (the sRGB transfer in 32-bit float, rounded to 16 bits, with the 8-bit frame the 16-bit one rounded) instead of the CPU's conversion in 64-bit float, so a sample can differ by one code at either depth; the 16-bit pack and its 8-bit rounding are held to within one code of the CPU conversion in sr-gpu's tests.
- The first frame of a node motion-blurred with a shutter that opens before time 0 (the default `shutterPhase` of −90°) now blurs over the samples before time 0, so a node moving at time 0 draws blurred where it drew sharp.
- Outputs whose encoder reads planar YUV at 4:2:2 or 4:4:4 (ProRes, DNxHR, FFV1 with alpha) take planes packed on the GPU, whose codes match the RGB path's within one level. The 4:2:0 planes of x265, SVT-AV1, libaom, rav1e and VP9 are the NV12 and P010 planes deinterleaved.
- A vector frame with a mask or matte layer and no backdrop rows renders instead of panicking in the raster stage.
- The contrast ratios the report gives for an output layer's texts are the minimum over the frames the check's budget measured rather than over every frame.
- Every other change keeps frames and audio packets as in v0.7.4.

## v0.7.4 — 2026-10-23

This release renders 3D scenes with less GPU work and holds less memory while it delivers. The 3D renderer unrolls point, spot and area lights, keeps its shadow map while the shadow casters hold still, filters one-texel shadows with four gathers, blurs ambient occlusion with one load per tap and skips passes nothing reads. The vendored schema is still sr-core 1.7.0.

### 3D rendering

- The 3D renderer unrolls the light loop by light kind for up to four lights whenever every screen tile holds every light, point, spot and area lights included. Before, only ambient and directional lights were unrolled. The claymation's ambient, spot and point lights now take that path: its opaque pass takes 19.4 ms instead of 34.8 ms per frame on lavapipe, and a frame renders in 80.1 ms instead of 95.8 ms (median). Frames are the same.
- The 3D renderer keeps its shadow map between frames and skips the shadow passes while the shadow views (their matrices and size) and every caster's mesh and placement stay the same, for example when only the camera moves. Deformed and displaced casters still redraw it every frame. It keeps one map and reuses its texture when the key changes, so it holds no more memory than before. In a 1280x720 test scene with static casters, a spot and a point light that cast shadows, and a moving camera, the shadow passes (13.8 ms per frame on lavapipe) run on the first frame only and the median frame takes 10.6 ms instead of 24.7 ms. Its 24 frames are the same. The claymation's boiling clay casts shadows that change every frame, so its timing is unchanged.
- When a light's shadow taps are one texel apart (softness up to 1), the raster shadow filter reads its 4x4 shadow texels with four `textureGatherCompare` calls instead of nine filtered compare taps. The weights are the same, computed exactly rather than by the sampler's bilinear filter. On the 06-marx-engels-clay film the opaque pass took 8 % less time on lavapipe (18.86 → 17.24 ms per frame) and 2.5 % less on an RTX 3050. The sampled frames are byte-identical on both adapters. Softer shadows keep the nine taps.
- The depth and normal prepass for ambient occlusion, reflections and contact shadows compiles the opaque pass's variants: without an alpha-masked material it has no discard, which leaves early depth rejection to the driver; a plain material skips the general material code; and without screen-space reflections it no longer samples the BRDF table for a reflectance nothing reads. The prepass of the 1280x720 claymation took 6.62 ms instead of 7.43 ms per frame on lavapipe, with the same frames.
- The 3D renderer leaves out work nothing reads. It resolves multisampled depth only for depth of field, and the final post pass reads depth only when depth of field is on. The transparent pass runs only when something is drawn in it or the target must be resolved. A 3D pass copies the layers behind it only for glass or the path tracer. The aperture buffer, the empty IES table and the compositor's empty generator binding are created once instead of every frame. On the 1280x720 claymation on lavapipe, the depth resolve (1.13 ms per frame) and the empty transparent pass are gone and the final post pass takes 1.61 ms instead of 2.19 ms. Frames are the same.
- Screen-space ambient occlusion writes the occlusion and the view depth into one target, in the depth target's format, so its blur reads one texel per tap instead of two. The blurred occlusion is a one-channel half-float target instead of four channels. The blur now averages the occlusion at the depth target's precision rather than at half-float, which moves a frame by at most one half-float step. On the 1280x720 claymation on lavapipe, the blur takes 1.30 ms instead of 2.43 ms per frame and the occlusion pass 4.15 ms instead of 3.84 ms. Sample frames stay above 79 dB PSNR against the previous build.

### Path tracing

- The path tracer keys its packed storage cache on its textures' upload keys instead of comparing the whole texture atlas on every pass, and packs the atlas only when no cached storage holds those textures. The cache also keeps the packed atlas it built rather than a copy of it. A warm pass over unchanged textures packs no texels; before, it repacked every texture and compared all its pixels. New statistic: `pt_atlas_texels`. Frames are the same.
- The CPU path tracer builds its scene (records, BVH, texture atlas) and environment map once per pass and shares them across tiles. Before, every tile rebuilt them and copied the environment texels. New field `RenderTiming::cpu_scenes`. Frames are the same.

### Delivery and encoding

- Delivery extracts a frame's clay surfaces on the thread that evaluates frames ahead of the renderer, so that work overlaps the previous frame's render. The objects that changed are extracted in parallel with each other, and a surface whose blobs, finish, resolution and boil step are unchanged is reused. The renderer keys its clay meshes by a hash of those inputs instead of formatting every blob, and uploads the extracted vectors without copying them. Frames are unchanged: a 736-frame 1280x720 claymation with 94 clay objects, 71 of them boiling every frame, encoded to the same frames (framemd5) on lavapipe in 86.2 s instead of 91.7 s.
- Delivery reuses the buffers of frames read back from the GPU. Once the encoder thread has written a frame, it returns the frame's buffer to its output stage, and the next frame refills it. Before, every frame allocated a new frame-sized buffer: about 12 MB per frame at 3840x2160 NV12. The stage keeps at most 4 returned buffers. `OutputStage::wait` now returns `FrameBytes`, which reads as a byte slice; `into_vec` keeps the bytes. Output is the same.
- On Linux the encoder's frame pipe holds 1 MiB, or the most `/proc/sys/fs/pipe-max-size` allows, instead of the default 64 KiB. A frame crosses into FFmpeg in fewer, larger writes. Output is the same.
- The vector tile encoder tiles a frame's paths on rayon's persistent worker pool. Before, every frame spawned and joined its own threads. A worker's panic now reaches the caller; before, that worker's paths were silently left out of the frame. `set_threads` still caps how many shares a frame is split into. Output is the same.

### Memory

- On Linux with glibc, the `scene-render` binary keeps one malloc arena for all its threads, as the GPU test binaries already did. Before, each rendering thread had an arena that kept the hundreds of megabytes Mesa's software drivers freed on it. Encoding the showcase films on lavapipe, peak RSS fell 4-14 % (04-clay 556 → 480 MiB, 03-emergence 706 → 647 MiB), with wall time within run-to-run noise. A `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX` the user sets still decides the cap.
- Video decoders close once no layer has drawn their clip for four frames, like the frames they decoded, and the clip reopens when it is drawn again. Before, every clip's decoder, with its FFmpeg reader and queued frames, stayed open until the renderer was dropped. A decoder queues at most 64 MiB of frames ahead of the last request: 5 frames of a 3840x2160 8-bit 4:2:0 clip instead of 8. Frames that leave its ring with no other holder are decoded into again instead of being reallocated: 85 sequential frames of a test clip used at most 16 buffers instead of 87. Frames are the same.
- Delivery keeps only the mixed tracks and buses that shader effects read for the length of the render, and none when no shader has an audio input. Before, every track's post-fader output and the master stayed in memory until the render ended, about 23 MB per stereo track per minute at 48 kHz. An output with an overlay still keeps every channel, since the overlay's own shaders may read any of them. Frames are the same.
- Text libraries in one process share a font file's bytes: each parallel delivery worker read and kept its own copy of every face it used. A file replaced on disk (other length or modification time) is read anew.
- Renderers in one process share imported 3D models and splat clouds: each parallel delivery worker imported its own copy. An asset is imported anew once it or a file it reads changes length or modification time. Uploading a model's primitive keeps its prepared vertices as the host copy rather than copying them again.

Each of N workers keeps 1/N of the 256 MiB for path traced geometry, the path tracer's packed storage, and decoded volumes and baked frames. They also share the 96 MB queue in front of the encoders, still holding at least 3 frames per encoder. Before, every worker kept the full budgets. Per-frame limits, such as the largest volume frame, are unchanged. Frames are the same.
- Uploading a decoded image filters each mip level from the one before as it writes it. Before, the whole f32 chain was built before the upload. A 3840x2160 image no longer holds its full chain of about 44 MB of f32 levels below the base at once: at most two adjacent levels exist. Textures are the same.

This release renders, encodes and mixes faster without changing what it delivers: cached vector layers are rasterised again only where they changed, pipelines are compiled when first drawn, clay, simulations and rigid bodies use every core, and the audio chain and the video encoder start beside the picture instead of before or after it. ### Rendering

- A cached vector layer is rasterised again only in the tiles whose inputs changed since its last frame: each tile keeps a hash of its commands, pieces, backdrops and resolved paints, and a target reused from the previous frame redraws only the tiles whose hash moved, while a new target, a resized one or a different pipeline variant is rasterised whole. On lavapipe the first 20 s of a 1080p explainer chapter rendered in 9.22 s instead of 32.00 s, with the same frames.

### Clay, simulation and physics

- Clay objects mesh their field on every core: each pass of the surface-nets extraction runs over independent slabs and joins them in order, so the mesh is the same, vertex for vertex and index for index, as before. A fingerprinted seven-blob object at `resolution="160"` meshed in 359.5 ms on one core and meshes in 45.4 ms on a 12-thread Ryzen 5 5600X; boiling clay re-meshes on every boil frame, so a 5 s clay film encoded in 4.6 s instead of 21.7 s there.

The roughness moves the surface by a bounded amount and the joined field is 1-Lipschitz, so beyond that reach and two cell diagonals the smooth field alone gives the side a grid point is on, which is all the mesher reads there; the mesh is the same. A fingerprinted seven-blob object at `resolution="160"` evaluates it at 10 % of its 2.4 million grid points and meshes in 31 ms instead of 45 ms.

The fingerprinted showcase body evaluates its field 0.45 million times instead of 2.4 million and meshes in 22 ms; a 5 s clay film encoded in 3.0 s instead of 3.7 s on a Ryzen 5 5600X, its mesh and frames unchanged.

A simulated document gives each frame the same graph whether or not the frame after it was evaluated first, which every simulated corpus document is now tested for; the frames are unchanged. A 5 s film of a flock, a slime mould and a smoke solver spent 3.3 s instead of 4.0 s waiting on evaluation.
- Rigid bodies that are closed meshes, the pieces of every fracture among them, are decomposed into convex parts on every core when their world is built, each body then taking its own in order, so the world and every frame of it are the same as before. Twelve mesh pieces built in 1.32 s on one core and in 0.40 s on a 6-core Ryzen 5 5600X; a 5 s fracture film encoded in 6.7 s instead of 8.7 s there.

## v0.7.0 — 2026-10-20

This release lets the path tracer stop tracing pixels whose mean has converged, denoises lighting apart from surface colour, finds its hits with hardware ray queries where the adapter offers them, and compiles its media shaders on NVIDIA's OpenGL driver. Validation warns about, and `inspect` reports, the cost of each path-traced camera, and more of the declared 3D and layout attributes are implemented. The vendored schema is still sr-core 1.7.0.

### Path tracer

- `render --path-noise REL` and `encode --path-noise REL` stop tracing a path-traced pixel once, from 16 samples on, its luminance and coverage are within REL relative standard error of their means; `pathSamples` caps the rest. A stopped pixel keeps its mean, so denoised and tiled output match a full render's statistics. `render --stats` reports `paths` with the `traced` samples against the `budget`. Without the flag rendering is unchanged.
- The path tracer's denoiser divides each pixel by its coverage and first-hit albedo before filtering and multiplies it back after, and sets how far a tap may differ in luminance from the variance of each pixel's own samples. Against a 4096-sample reference it halves the error at 8 samples while keeping fine albedo texture (the previous filter kept 46 % of it and its error exceeded the noisy frame's), no longer raises the error of an already clean 256-sample frame (previously 5.7 times), and leaves silhouette coverage as traced (previously 1.6 times the noisy error). `denoise` defaults to true, so path-traced output intentionally changes; set `denoise="false"` for the raw estimate.
- Where the adapter offers hardware ray queries, the path tracer finds its hits through acceleration structures the driver builds for each pass, on the GPU's ray-tracing units where it has them, and otherwise walks its own BVH as before. Both trace the same paths with the same random numbers, so images agree up to rays whose nearest hit differs by rounding. `SR_GPU_RAY_QUERY=0` keeps the BVH walk. `gpus` reports `ray_query` for each adapter and `render --stats` reports `hardware_rays` under `paths`. The JSON envelope is `schema_version` 1.2.0.
- On NVIDIA's OpenGL driver, which inlines every function call, the path tracer's media shaders compile in bounded memory. A lit medium gathers its in-scattered light in one loop over lights, crossed domains and surfaces instead of marching a shadow ray inside every step of the transport march, and the shader specializer removes code under overrides that are false. Before, compiling a frame with lit smoke or gas could exhaust memory. Images do not change beyond rounding.

### Path-traced cost

- W15 warns when a `renderer="pathtrace"` camera traces more than 1920 × 1080 × 256 × 4 path segments per frame: its frame's width × height × `pathSamples` × `maxBounces`, the frame being its `viewport3D`'s or the project's. 320 samples at 20 bounces in 1080p is 6.25 times that.
- `inspect` lists each path-traced camera's frame size, samples, bounces and path segments per frame, marking those W15 warns about; in JSON they are `path_costs`.

### Command line

- `encode --output ID -o PATH` writes that output to PATH with every setting it declares. Before, `-o` silently replaced the named output with an ad-hoc one at the project size with default settings. PATH must keep the output's file type; several `--output` ids, an unknown id, or `--codec` with `--output` are usage errors (exit status 2).

### Declared attributes now implemented

- Instances honor `clipOut` for their playback span and automatic end, and `fit`/`boxWidth`/`boxHeight` for symbol placement, including cover clipping and a blurred cover behind contain-blur.
- Physical cameras use the sensor height for frames narrower than the sensor aspect, including depth of field. With default sensor dimensions, square and portrait scenes using `focalLength` intentionally change; wider frames retain the horizontal gate. Geodesic `pathSamples` above 4096 now emit W14 before clamping.
- `antialias3d` selects 1 (default), 2 or 4 raster samples, rounding 3 up to 4. Unsupported counts fall back with a warning. Previously the renderer always used four samples: default 3D edge coverage therefore intentionally changes; set 4 to retain that sampling.
- Plain fit output reframing honors layout and segment focus, including animated segment focus.
- A voxels object whose `palette` lists fewer materials than the indices its cells use, and that has no `material` of its own, colours those cells from the `.vox` file, as the schema states. Before, the frame failed with "the palette index N has no material" although the file has colours; `tests/corpus/valid/voxels.scene.xml` now renders.
- Medium albedo defaults to white in the volume library and renderer, using the library default as the renderer fallback.
- NaN opacity from animation keys becomes zero before inheritance, and repeat-copy opacity factors are clamped to 0..1. INERT-I17 identifies 2.5D attributes without threeD and alignment attributes without alignment; centered alignment still ignores margin.

### Examples

- `examples/glass-of-water/` drops a drop into a glass of water with native elements only: transmissive glass and water materials under the path tracer, an ocean bounded by the glass that the drop's `waterImpulse` drives, and the ocean's `whitewater` spray as the splash. A test holds it to those elements.

## v0.4.1 — 2026-10-11

This release vendors sr-core schema 1.7.0 byte-identical (document version 1.7) and implements its one change, SREP 87: ids that a program emits are scoped by the program's id. It also reports SREP 72's precision findings under the SREP's codes, and fixes how an included document's references are resolved.

### Schema 1.7.0 and SREP 87

- **Document version 1.7.** `scene/@version` accepts `1.7`. Version 1.7 adds no syntax and gates nothing, so a 1.7 document validates and renders as the same document at 1.6.
- **Program-emitted ids (SREP 87).** An id `E` that the build-mode program `P` emits becomes `P.E` in the document, so two programs, or a program and the document, can use the same local ids.
  - Inside an output, IDREF and IDREFS tokens, `prop` and `propAtTime` ids and the node id of `link/@source` resolve to the output's own ids first.
  - A bare reference from outside resolves when exactly one program emitted the id, with the new warning W13, and fails with the new error PRG17 when several did. The same holds for `override/@target` of a layout, a variant, an instance or an include.
  - The bone names in an emitted skeleton's skin weights are its local ids.
  - Local ids are kept per document of the build, so a written id of one document that equals an emitted id of another keeps its seed.
  - PRG14 covers a repeated id within one output and a namespaced id equal to another id.
- **Kit.** sr-core's 15 SREP 87 cases and the amended SREP 66 collision case pass on this engine. Four of them are failure cases that `run.py` cannot check; they were checked by hand.

### SREP 72 follow-ups

- **PREC1.** `project/@precision="f32"` in a document with 3D content gives the information finding PREC1, once, in `validate` and in the render report. Its message starts with SREP 72's words, "3D internal buffers stay f16". It replaces the engine's E23, which said the same and had no other use. PREC1 covers every kind the 3D pass draws: `object3D`, `particles3D`, `ocean`, `blackHole` and `accretionDisk`. sr-core's erratum of 2026-10-11 adds the last two to SREP 72's list, and SREP 72 is now Final.
- **PREC10.** A device without float32 blending or filtering already failed an f32 render, naming the missing feature. The error message now starts with "PREC10:", and the render report gives it the code PREC10, also when an output layer meets it first.

### Fixes

- **References inside an include name the include's own document.**
  - A transformConstraint's `pole` resolves in the constraint's own scope, as its `target` already did.
  - A target or pole that names a bone resolves to the bone of the skeleton in that scope. Before, the rig took the first skeleton in document order that declared a bone of that id, in any document.
  - A fracture's `source` resolves in its owner's scope (E11 when it names nothing there).
  - The 3D renderer's references (a parent, a camera's target and focus target, a joint's look-at) no longer fall back to the including document's node or light of the same id.
- **A constraint target or pole that names nothing** in its scope now gives an E11 warning. A top-level constraint no longer binds to a bone inside a symbol instance or a repeat copy, whose ids are scoped to that copy.
- **Each included document reads its own parameters:** `repeat/@over`, `param:` links, text placeholders and `param()` read the included document's defaults and data sources, never the including document's.
- **CI.** The Mesa shader cache is keyed by the runner's CPU. A cache written on a runner with other instruction-set extensions made llvmpipe crash with SIGILL in encode tests.

### Schema 1.6.0, vendored byte-identical

`schema/scene-render-1.1.xsd`, `schema/scene-render-1.1.sch` and `schema/capabilities-1.schema.json` are byte-identical to sr-core's release 1.6.0 (tag `schema-1.6.0`, commit 54af5c1). `schema/UPSTREAM` gives their SHA-256 and lists the SREPs this engine implements. ### SREP follow-ups

- **Version gates (SREPs 76, 80 and 83).** BH1, VOX1 and SREP 40's gates V8, MSQ1, P3D1, OCN1, CRT1, FRX1 and GEO1 refuse versions 1.0 to 1.2 only, and their messages say "1.3 or later". In v0.3.2 they refused every version but 1.3, so no document could hold a black hole, cells, a volume, pyro, an ocean, a crater, a fracture or globe relief together with a 1.6 feature such as `viewport3D`.
- **Warning renames (SREP 76).** The black-hole warnings are W11 (a hole or disk that no geodesic camera draws) and W12 (`denoise` on a geodesic camera). In v0.3.2 they were W03 and W04, codes that SREP 57 gives to its generated-marker warnings.
- **Boolean values (SREPs 76, 77 and 84).** Rules read an `xs:boolean` attribute by its value: "true" and "1" are true, "false" and "0" are false, and surrounding whitespace is ignored. This covers BH5 to BH8, W11, W12 and W05 (`geodesics`, `denoise`), CRT11 and CRT12 (`mantle`), C15, C43, R15, R48, VOL5, C80, GEO2, PYRO9 to PYRO11 and the warning W02. Integer attributes in these rules are read whatever their sign, leading zeros or padding.
- **Decimal and double values (SREP 85).** C13, C14, C15, C37, C38, C44, C52, C53, C54, C56, PYRO1, PYRO2, PYRO4, VOL4, P3D3, P3D10, OCN3, OCN5, PYRO11, PYC6, FRX11, ITR1 and SRT1 read their numeric attributes by value, so "+1" is 1. In v0.3.2, XPath's `number()` read "+1" as NaN. Exponent forms such as `1e0` follow the native validator, which is normative for them. INF, -INF and NaN compare as the Schematron compares them.
- **Disk profile (SREP 76).** The geodesic pass divides the thin-disk profile by its exact maximum, (6/7)^(3/2) 7^(-1/4) ≈ 0.48787, instead of 0.4880.
- **Supersample filter (SREP 89).** The down filter clamps each channel to [0, max(1, M)], where M is the largest sample of non-zero weight, instead of [0, 1]. Colour finishing (exposure, looks, tone mapping) runs on the filtered frame. A frame whose samples all lie within [0, 1] gives the same bytes as before.

### Fixes

- **Includes and instances.** References written inside an included document, a symbol instance or a repeat copy resolve in that scope. Before, the 3D renderer and the rigid-body world compared them as written, so an included black hole lost its disk and its camera lost its look-at target.
- **Included definitions.** An included document's materials, material maps, effects (and their shader files), fonts and maps resolve in that document. ### Scene features (document version 1.6 where gated)

- **SREP 71, materials and sky.** It adds a subsurface lobe (Christensen–Burley profile, pre-integrated over curvature), a procedural gradient sky with a sun on dome lights, and material maps fed by generator or image assets. The schema editor accepted these SREPs on 2026-10-10; they enter sr-core with schema 1.6.0. Until then the engine's vendored schema carries them as local extensions (see `schema/UPSTREAM`).

### Sound across transitions (SREP 82)

A transition now mixes the sound of the video layers on its sides, including layers inside a side, by its `audio` attribute, at the eased progress p:
- `crossfade` (the default) gives the outgoing side 1 − p and the incoming side p;
- `equal-power` gives cos(πp/2) and sin(πp/2);
- `cut` switches from one side to the other where p reaches ½;
- `none` leaves both sides at their own volume.

Sequence junctions crossfade, except that a `cut` junction cuts its sound with its picture. The gains multiply the layers' volume automation, one value per frame, interpolated between frames. Before this release `audio` was read by no code, and both sides played at full volume over the whole window.

### Fixes

- **Prewarm and stepsPerFrame memory.** A frame that takes several steps now runs them as one looped pass over two textures, as `iterate` does. Before, every step kept a new texture until the frame was submitted. At 1,000 prewarm steps (1280×720), peak memory fell from 4,088 MB to 258 MB; it is 278 MB at 6,000 steps. The pixels are byte-identical.
- **Odd frame sizes.** New error OUT1: H.264 and H.265 outputs whose frame the chroma format halves must have even dimensions. Validate reports it with the even size to set, and `encode -o` refuses it before rendering. An odd-sized AV1 frame is encoded with libaom.
- **Silent 3D cases.** Two new render-report warnings:
  - `3D-OFF-CAMERA`, for an object3D outside the view in every frame it is drawn;
  - `3D-METAL-NO-ENVIRONMENT`, for metallic 0.5 or more with no environment image.

  As warnings, they fail `encode --strict`.
- **Morph transition.** The luminance displacement described in the README is restored; a rewrite of the transition shader had reduced morph to a crossfade.
- **`--parallel auto`.** The split now depends only on the programme: one chunk per 10 s, at most 12, and one stream under 20 s. The host decides only how many chunks render at once, so the same scene splits the same way on any machine.

### Transmission rendering

Image changes in the path tracer for scenes with transmissive materials (`transmission` above 0); scenes without one render the same bytes at the same speed.

- Surfaces seen through water or glass now receive the analytic lights: the shadow ray is refracted at the interface instead of being blocked by it, with Fresnel transmittance and absorption. Before, a floor under water got no direct light at all. In `examples/cinematic-impact/impact.scene.xml` the mean brightness at t = 2.0 goes from 0.00663 to 0.00909 (plume and ejecta removed for the measurement). Shadows that glass casts on surfaces in air stay black.
- `attenuationColor` and `attenuationDistance` now take effect in path-traced renders (they already did in raster): after one attenuation distance the light left is the colour, per channel.
- Glass or water over nothing now shows the visible dome where it showed black, including the dome's reflection on water at the horizon.
- The ocean's default spray is transmissive, so frames with whitewater change slightly around the spray (a UHD hero frame: 394 pixels, at most 28 code values).
- Limits: a camera that starts under the water does not see the sun on the floor, caustics are not produced, and very steep waves can leave samples dark. See the cinematic impact SREP, "Light through water and glass".
