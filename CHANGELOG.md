# Changelog

The release notes of every tagged release, newest first. Generated by `tools/changelog.py` from `RELEASE_NOTES.md`
at each tag; edit `RELEASE_NOTES.md`, not this file. Entries are kept as released, so a document they cite may have
been removed since: "the cinematic impact SREP" is sr-core SREP 40, whose working draft this repository removed after v0.3.2.
Versions up to v0.4.1 were released under the MIT License from github.com/pedroanisio/rs-scene-render.

## Unreleased

### Path tracing

- On a GPU adapter the path tracer keeps the scratch its tiles work in from one pass to the next: the sums and guides of each pixel, the stand-in buffer, and the denoiser's two halves with their bind groups, grown only when a tile needs more and dropped when the tile budget changes. Before, every pass created and released those buffers, four of 32 bytes per pixel, about 118 MB for a 1280x720 pass. A CPU adapter still makes them for each pass: they are host memory its allocator recycles, and keeping them only raised the peak. The numbers are under the next entry, which was measured with this one.
- The CPU path tracer keeps what it builds from a scene for the passes over the same geometry and textures: the dense instance records, the atlas packed into words and its own top-level BVH, by geometry identity and atlas key, at most four scenes and 16 MB in all. It also reads the dome from the texels the engine keeps instead of copying them into every pass, 32 MB for a 2K dome. `Stats3.pt_cpu_scenes` counts the scenes a pass built: 0 when a kept one served or the GPU traced. With the kept scratch above, on 02-fracture frames 60-120 on an RTX 3050 (serial, alternated runs) `pt_pack_seconds` fell from 8.5-8.6 ms to 4.2-4.6 ms per frame, the frame median from 77.8-79.4 ms to 76.2-76.8 ms and peak host RSS from 930-936 MB to 878-879 MB; GPU time per frame is unchanged (63.3-63.9 ms). On lavapipe (pipelined) the frame median went from 1902-1921 ms to 1805-1877 ms and peak RSS from 1332-1341 MB to 1347-1356 MB: the fracture pieces move every frame, so every pass is another scene to the tracer there, and the kept scenes add their bound to the peak. Frames are the same.

### Evaluation

- Per-frame evaluation does less work that nothing reads. A node's keyframes, links and expressions are left unevaluated while the node is off screen and nothing reads them. Nodes are still evaluated when something reads them off screen: an expression, a link or z-order; a node another one is parented to, and that node's ancestors; or a transition. Constraints are looked for only on the nodes that have one, found when the program is built, not by walking every node's children each frame. Each slot keeps the noise channel of its property instead of hashing the property name on every `wiggle()` call. A number wiggled by a number takes a scalar path that allocates nothing. A map's fitted camera is kept for the program, so `geo()` no longer reads the fit files' metadata on every call. The frame's output lists are reserved for the nodes alive in it. On sr-eval's benchmark scene (1,020 nodes, 5,500 slots, 500 expressions, all on screen, no maps), the frame median fell from 0.864 ms to 0.805 ms: ten alternated pairs, each one faster. The off-screen and map savings scale with a document's off-screen nodes and `geo()` calls, which that scene does not have. Frames are the same.

### Simulation

- A 2D particle that collides is tested only against the colliders whose bounding box holds it. The boxes are found once per physics step, when the step's colliders are kept for the particles. Before, every live colliding particle was projected onto every collider on every step. On the physics benchmark scene (about 1,200 live sparks against 205 colliders), evaluating frame 300 went from 1.69 s to 1.17 s (five alternated runs each), with the same graph.
- A 3D particle emitter reserves room for the particles it carries rather than for its `maxParticles`. With the default cap that was about 1.8 MB on every fixed step and in the frame it publishes. The death times of the live particles are heaped in one pass.
- A smoke domain's solid mask is computed on every core. A domain with no colliders skips the mask altogether. In the profile of a 128x104x128 domain, the obstacle stage went from 24 ms to 14 ms a step with a slab and a sphere, and from 8 ms to 0.1 ms with none. The mask and the solid cells' face velocities are the same.

### Memory

- The 2D physics world and the 2D particle emitter keep a bounded set of replay checkpoints, as the 3D world and the other simulations already did: at most 65 under the shared 256 MB budget, thinned by doubling their spacing. Before, they kept one a second for the whole run. Evaluating the last frame of a 600-second copy of the physics benchmark scene (one run each) peaked at 912,404 KiB of RSS before and 115,740 KiB after, with the same graph.
- The 3D world charges a collider shape, or the kept cells of a body that can be cut, once for all the checkpoints that share it rather than once per checkpoint, and measures it only when a checkpoint first holds it. What the checkpoints share with the initial checkpoint, which is outside the budget, is not charged. A world with a 5,000-triangle ground used to thin to two checkpoints under an 8 MB budget, and now keeps one a second.
- A mesh sequence's blended frame shares the texture pixels of the frames it blends rather than copying them. With two 2048x2048 textures on a 50,000-vertex model, a blended sample went from 24.3 ms to 6.5 ms, and it no longer holds a 32 MB copy.
- Audio sources are decoded straight into one buffer per channel as FFmpeg writes them. Before, a source was held twice at its peak: the encoded bytes beside the samples, then the interleaved samples beside the per-channel buffers. A 20-second stereo source held 16.1 MB at once for 7.7 MB of audio; it now holds the audio once, plus at most a sixty-fourth and a second of room, which is given back.
- The process-wide geo caches (features, fitted cameras, tile archives and style files) keep one reading per file and replace it when the file changes on disk. Before, every revision of a file a preview session saw stayed cached. Each cache is also capped at 256 entries. The tile and elevation caches hold their archive weakly, so a replaced archive's address cannot be taken for the new one's.
- The grids of a cut body of cells are dropped with the revisions the 3D world drops from its budgeted history.
- The 3D engine drops the meshes and textures no pass of a frame used, least recently used first, once the idle ones of each kind exceed 256 MB (shared between delivery workers like the other budgets). Before, every mesh and texture a session uploaded was kept until the renderer closed. What a frame draws is never dropped, whatever the budget. `--stats` reports the count as `assets_evicted`.
- The volume cache drops the least recently used decoded volumes and baked frames until a new one fits its 256 MB budget or its 64 entries of a kind, instead of clearing every entry. A volume that does not fit the budget alone is used for the frame but not kept, and pushes nothing out.

### 3D

- Each 3D pass writes its frame, light, tile, shadow-matrix, object, material and draw-order data into buffers the engine keeps from one submit to the next, grown when a pass needs more, and keeps the material bind groups with them, remade only when the material buffer grew or a material's maps changed. Before, every pass created those seven buffers and all of its material bind groups. Passes recorded between two submits each get their own set, as a `queue.write_buffer` lands before the next submit's first command; the renderer tells the engine after each submit, and up to 16 sets are kept. `Stats3.buffers_created` and `Stats3.material_binds_created` count what a pass made: 0 for a warm pass. With the entries below, on an RTX 3050 (serial, alternated runs of 48 frames) perf_3d's `submit_ms` went from 0.75-0.79 ms to 0.66 ms and its frame median from 4.25-4.40 ms to 4.12-4.13 ms with GPU time unchanged (3.1-3.3 ms); perf_text and 02-fracture frames 60-120 are unchanged within run-to-run noise on both adapters (fracture 74.9-76.2 ms before, 74.7-75.8 ms after on the GPU; 1736-1758 ms before, 1745-1754 ms after on lavapipe), and on lavapipe perf_3d's median spread between pipelined runs (452-580 ms before, 288-559 ms after) is wider than any difference between the binaries. `buffers_created` is 0 on every warm frame of the three scenes. Frames are byte-identical on both adapters.
- The rest of what a 3D pass made for itself goes the same way: the vertex buffers of deformed (skinned or morphed) draws, the splat sorts' parameters and radix scratch, and the splat draws' object uniforms are kept in the pass's set or by the engine and written in place, and the IES atlas is kept while the lights hold the same profiles instead of being packed and uploaded every pass. Under f32 precision the f16 target a 3D pass draws into comes from the texture pool, which now keys its free textures by size and format, instead of being made for the frame and dropped. Morphing and skinning run per vertex on the rayon pool with the same sums in the same order, so the vertices are the same to the bit.

### Vector

- Each vector raster job appends its parameters, commands, ranges, pieces, backdrops and active tiles to six arenas the raster stage keeps between submits, at 256-byte offsets, bound by offset and size; an arena is replaced by a larger one when a job does not fit, and all are rewound after each submit. Before, every job made six buffers. `RenderStats.buffers_created` (`--stats`) counts the buffers a frame made for its 3D passes, particle batches and raster jobs: 0 for a warm frame.

### Particles

- Each particle batch writes its instances into a buffer the engine keeps between submits (up to 64), grown when a batch needs more, instead of a new buffer every frame.

### Delivery

- The flash check's 48x27 grid is collected one frame after it is submitted, on a second mapped buffer, instead of being read back while the render thread waits for it. Only the last frame of a programme waits. On perf_layers (1080p60, 600 frames, RTX 3050) with the flash check on, the encode went from 11.2 s to 8.9-9.2 s, level with the same scene with no check (8.65 s); the grid's cells are the same.
- The sample times of a motion blur are evaluated in parallel on the rayon pool once the blur knows it will draw them all, and the seconds they take reach the encode report as `subframe_seconds` (summed over workers) and the text summary as `shutter sub-frames`. The counterfactual probes of a contrast check still evaluate serially. Frames are the same; on perf_3d with 32 samples and perf_effects with 16, evaluation is under a millisecond a frame, so frame times do not move.
- Outputs whose encoder reads planar YUV get those planes packed on the GPU: ProRes yuv422p10le (4444 profiles yuv444p10le, with alpha yuva444p10le), DNxHR yuv422p or yuv422p10le, FFV1 with alpha yuva444p10le, and x265, SVT-AV1, libaom, rav1e and VP9 the 4:2:0 planes at 8 or 10 bits; libx264 and the hardware encoders keep NV12 and P010, which they read as they are. Before, those outputs read back 16-bit RGBA (8 bytes a pixel) or NV12 and FFmpeg converted every frame in software. The 4:2:0 planes are the NV12 and P010 planes deinterleaved, and the 4:2:2 and 4:4:4 codes match the RGB path's within one level. On perf_layers (1080p60, 600 frames, RTX 3050) to ProRes HQ the readback stage went from 2.9 s to 1.9 s; elapsed (30-31 s) and FFmpeg's CPU (339-341 s user) stay where prores_ks holds them.
- Each pass of a two-pass or size-fitted encode reads the lossless intermediate itself. Before, a second FFmpeg decoded it to raw frames that the delivery thread copied into the encoder's pipe. The encoder counts the frames FFmpeg reports and fails the pass, leaving the previous file, unless the intermediate decoded to exactly the frames rendered, so a short or unreadable intermediate still produces no output. On perf_layers (1080p60, 600 frames, RTX 3050) encoded two-pass to H.264 the file is byte-identical, the run starts 4 FFmpeg processes instead of 6 and its system CPU falls from 7.6 s to 3.1-3.2 s; elapsed and user CPU stay where libx264 holds them.
- The output layer's texts are measured for contrast at the frames the check's budget schedules, over the delivered picture, like the composition's own texts, and they share that budget of 256 probes (32 a text). Before, a layer with text was probed on every frame of the delivery: the layer rendered again per text, composited and read back while the render thread waited. The report's sampled-contrast note names layer texts like any others. On perf_layers with a two-text output layer (1080p60, 600 frames, RTX 3050) the encode with the contrast check on went from 59-74 s to 14.3-14.8 s, where the same encode with the check off takes 14 s. The ratios a layer text reports are the minimum over its measured frames rather than all of them.

## v0.7.4 — 2026-10-23

This release renders 3D scenes with less GPU work and holds less memory while it delivers. The 3D renderer unrolls point, spot and area lights, keeps its shadow map while the shadow casters hold still, filters one-texel shadows with four gathers, blurs ambient occlusion with one load per tap and skips passes nothing reads. Delivery extracts clay surfaces ahead of the renderer, reuses read-back frame buffers, closes idle video decoders, shares fonts, 3D assets and cache budgets across workers, and keeps one malloc arena. On lavapipe the 736-frame 1280x720 06-marx-engels-clay film encoded in 69.00 s and 68.72 s instead of 92.63 s and 92.11 s with v0.7.3 (user CPU 561 s and 560 s instead of 748 s and 739 s), with a peak RSS of 2.08 to 2.12 GiB instead of 3.65 to 3.75 GiB. Ambient occlusion now blurs at the depth target's precision, which moves frames on every adapter by at most one half-float step; the bounds are given below. The vendored schema is still sr-core 1.7.0.

### 3D rendering

- The 3D renderer unrolls the light loop by light kind for up to four lights whenever every screen tile holds every light, point, spot and area lights included. Before, only ambient and directional lights were unrolled. The claymation's ambient, spot and point lights now take that path: its opaque pass takes 19.4 ms instead of 34.8 ms per frame on lavapipe, and a frame renders in 80.1 ms instead of 95.8 ms (median). Frames are the same.
- The 3D renderer keeps its shadow map between frames and skips the shadow passes while the shadow views (their matrices and size) and every caster's mesh and placement stay the same, for example when only the camera moves. Deformed and displaced casters still redraw it every frame. It keeps one map and reuses its texture when the key changes, so it holds no more memory than before. In a 1280x720 test scene with static casters, a spot and a point light that cast shadows, and a moving camera, the shadow passes (13.8 ms per frame on lavapipe) run on the first frame only and the median frame takes 10.6 ms instead of 24.7 ms. Its 24 frames are the same. The claymation's boiling clay casts shadows that change every frame, so its timing is unchanged.
- When a light's shadow taps are one texel apart (softness up to 1), the raster shadow filter reads its 4x4 shadow texels with four `textureGatherCompare` calls instead of nine filtered compare taps. The weights are the same, computed exactly rather than by the sampler's bilinear filter. On the 06-marx-engels-clay film the opaque pass took 8 % less time on lavapipe (18.86 → 17.24 ms per frame) and 2.5 % less on an RTX 3050. The sampled frames are byte-identical on both adapters. Softer shadows keep the nine taps.
- The depth and normal prepass for ambient occlusion, reflections and contact shadows compiles the opaque pass's variants: without an alpha-masked material it has no discard, which leaves early depth rejection to the driver; a plain material skips the general material code; and without screen-space reflections it no longer samples the BRDF table for a reflectance nothing reads. The prepass of the 1280x720 claymation took 6.62 ms instead of 7.43 ms per frame on lavapipe, with the same frames.
- The 3D renderer leaves out work nothing reads. It resolves multisampled depth only for depth of field, and the final post pass reads depth only when depth of field is on. The transparent pass runs only when something is drawn in it or the target must be resolved. A 3D pass copies the layers behind it only for glass or the path tracer. The aperture buffer, the empty IES table and the compositor's empty generator binding are created once instead of every frame. On the 1280x720 claymation on lavapipe, the depth resolve (1.13 ms per frame) and the empty transparent pass are gone and the final post pass takes 1.61 ms instead of 2.19 ms. Frames are the same.
- Screen-space ambient occlusion writes the occlusion and the view depth into one target, in the depth target's format, so its blur reads one texel per tap instead of two. The blurred occlusion is a one-channel half-float target instead of four channels. The blur now averages the occlusion at the depth target's precision rather than at half-float, which moves a frame by at most one half-float step. On the 1280x720 claymation on lavapipe, the blur takes 1.30 ms instead of 2.43 ms per frame and the occlusion pass 4.15 ms instead of 3.84 ms. Sample frames stay above 79 dB PSNR against the previous build.

### Path tracing

- The path tracer keys its packed storage cache on its textures' upload keys instead of comparing the whole texture atlas on every pass, and packs the atlas only when no cached storage holds those textures. The cache also keeps the packed atlas it built rather than a copy of it. A warm pass over unchanged textures packs no texels; before, it repacked every texture and compared all its pixels. New statistic: `pt_atlas_texels`. Frames are the same.
- The CPU path tracer builds its scene (records, BVH, texture atlas) and environment map once per pass and shares them across tiles. Before, every tile rebuilt them and copied the environment texels. New field `RenderTiming::cpu_scenes`. Frames are the same.

### Delivery and encoding

- Delivery extracts a frame's clay surfaces on the thread that evaluates frames ahead of the renderer, so that work overlaps the previous frame's render. The objects that changed are extracted in parallel with each other, and a surface whose blobs, finish, resolution and boil step are unchanged is reused. The renderer keys its clay meshes by a hash of those inputs instead of formatting every blob, and uploads the extracted vectors without copying them. Frames are unchanged: a 736-frame 1280x720 claymation with 94 clay objects, 71 of them boiling every frame, encoded to the same frames (framemd5) on lavapipe in 86.2 s instead of 91.7 s.
- Delivery reuses the buffers of frames read back from the GPU. Once the encoder thread has written a frame, it returns the frame's buffer to its output stage, and the next frame refills it. Before, every frame allocated a new frame-sized buffer: about 12 MB per frame at 3840x2160 NV12. The stage keeps at most 4 returned buffers. `OutputStage::wait` now returns `FrameBytes`, which reads as a byte slice; `into_vec` keeps the bytes. Output is the same.
- On Linux the encoder's frame pipe holds 1 MiB, or the most `/proc/sys/fs/pipe-max-size` allows, instead of the default 64 KiB. A frame crosses into FFmpeg in fewer, larger writes. Output is the same.
- The vector tile encoder tiles a frame's paths on rayon's persistent worker pool. Before, every frame spawned and joined its own threads. A worker's panic now reaches the caller; before, that worker's paths were silently left out of the frame. `set_threads` still caps how many shares a frame is split into. Output is the same.

### Memory

- On Linux with glibc, the `scene-render` binary keeps one malloc arena for all its threads, as the GPU test binaries already did. Before, each rendering thread had an arena that kept the hundreds of megabytes Mesa's software drivers freed on it. Encoding the showcase films on lavapipe, peak RSS fell 4-14 % (04-clay 556 → 480 MiB, 03-emergence 706 → 647 MiB), with wall time within run-to-run noise. A `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX` the user sets still decides the cap.
- Video decoders close once no layer has drawn their clip for four frames, like the frames they decoded, and the clip reopens when it is drawn again. Before, every clip's decoder, with its FFmpeg reader and queued frames, stayed open until the renderer was dropped. A decoder queues at most 64 MiB of frames ahead of the last request: 5 frames of a 3840x2160 8-bit 4:2:0 clip instead of 8. Frames that leave its ring with no other holder are decoded into again instead of being reallocated: 85 sequential frames of a test clip used at most 16 buffers instead of 87. Frames are the same.
- Delivery keeps only the mixed tracks and buses that shader effects read for the length of the render, and none when no shader has an audio input. Before, every track's post-fader output and the master stayed in memory until the render ended, about 23 MB per stereo track per minute at 48 kHz. An output with an overlay still keeps every channel, since the overlay's own shaders may read any of them. Frames are the same.
- Text libraries in one process share a font file's bytes: each parallel delivery worker read and kept its own copy of every face it used. A file replaced on disk (other length or modification time) is read anew.
- Renderers in one process share imported 3D models and splat clouds: each parallel delivery worker imported its own copy. An asset is imported anew once it or a file it reads changes length or modification time. Uploading a model's primitive keeps its prepared vertices as the host copy rather than copying them again.
- Parallel delivery workers share the default budgets of the caches that grow with the scene. Each of N workers keeps 1/N of the 256 MiB for path traced geometry, the path tracer's packed storage, and decoded volumes and baked frames. They also share the 96 MB queue in front of the encoders, still holding at least 3 frames per encoder. Before, every worker kept the full budgets. Per-frame limits, such as the largest volume frame, are unchanged. Frames are the same.
- Uploading a decoded image filters each mip level from the one before as it writes it. Before, the whole f32 chain was built before the upload. A 3840x2160 image no longer holds its full chain of about 44 MB of f32 levels below the base at once: at most two adjacent levels exist. Textures are the same.

### Changes that can alter output from v0.7.3

- Ambient occlusion is blurred at the depth target's precision instead of after rounding to half-float, on every adapter. Six sample frames of the 06-marx-engels-clay film stay within 79.4 to 84.4 dB PSNR of v0.7.3 on lavapipe and within 79.6 to 83.6 dB on an RTX 3050.
- A light whose shadow softness is at most 1 filters its shadow with exactly weighted gathers instead of the sampler's bilinear filter, so its shadow edges can change at float level; the test bound is 1 % of the lit level. The sampled 06-marx-engels-clay frames are byte-identical to the nine-tap filter's on lavapipe and on an RTX 3050.
- A panic in a vector tiling worker now reaches the caller. Before, that worker's paths were left out of the frame without an error.
- Every other change keeps frames and audio packets as in v0.7.3.

## v0.7.3 — 2026-10-22

This release makes the CPU path, lavapipe and llvmpipe, faster: the path tracer traces supported scenes in Rust on every core instead of in a compute shader, ambient occlusion runs at half size, and the compositor, output converter, mixer and x264 hand-off do less work per frame. On lavapipe a 1280x720 claymation with ambient occlusion encoded in 101.47 s instead of 120.30 s, and a 120-frame path-traced fracture film in 139 s. GPUs keep their frames; on software adapters the path tracer's and the occlusion's frames change within the bounds given below. The vendored schema is still sr-core 1.7.0.

### Path tracing on software adapters

- On software adapters the path tracer traces on the CPU in Rust instead of in a lavapipe compute shader: a port of the shader's trace pass (BVH and instance traversal, textures and materials, the dome and directional lights with their shadows, the BSDF, Russian roulette and the sample seeding) fills the same accumulation and guide buffers on every core, and the GPU denoise and output passes run on them as before. It applies when a scene uses only what the port covers; point, spot, area, ambient and IES lights, transmission, subsurface scattering, normal maps, splats, depth of field, adaptive sampling, light grids and a procedural sky keep the GPU trace, and `SR_PT_CPU=0` turns it off. On the 02-fracture scene a post-impact frame takes about 2.0 s instead of 4.4 s, and the whole 120-frame CPU-only encode took 139 s; frames differ at float level (PSNR 69 to 84 dB against the shader's frames at frames 20, 40, 60, 80, 100 and 119).
- On a CPU adapter the path tracer visits its 8x8 trace workgroups in a coprime-stride order instead of row by row: lavapipe gives each thread a contiguous run of workgroups, and uneven rows left one thread working at the end of each dispatch. Frames 20 and 80 of the 02-fracture scene decode bit-identical to before; frame 80's trace pass took 4.16 s instead of 5.1-5.4 s. GPUs keep the grid order.

### Rendering

- On a software adapter (lavapipe, llvmpipe) a camera's ambient occlusion and the depth and normal prepass it reads run at half the frame's size when nothing else reads that prepass (no screen-space reflections or contact shadows); the lit pass reads the occlusion filtered. A GPU keeps the full-size passes and the same frames. On lavapipe a 147 s 1280x720 claymation with ambient occlusion encoded in 101.47 s and 102.18 s instead of 120.30 s (user CPU 847 s instead of 1064 s); every one of its 736 frames stays within 49.1 dB PSNR of the full-size pass (50.8 dB on average).
- The first draw into a cleared target is recorded as a texture copy when it would write each texel unchanged: a frame-sized vector target that lands texel for pixel, at full opacity with the normal blend and no mask. Over a transparent target that draw adds nothing to its source, so the copy gives the same frame without running the compositor's fragment shader on every pixel. On lavapipe a 10 s slice of a 1080p explainer chapter encoded in 3.21 s instead of 3.46 s (render 1.67 s instead of 1.94 s), with the same frames and audio on every slice compared.
- The output converter compiles a variant for a frame that covers the output unmoved and unscaled, the usual case, in which each pixel samples the frame at its own position without placing or bounds-testing it. On lavapipe two 10 s slices of 1080p chapters rendered in 1.47 s and 1.32 s instead of 1.49 s and 1.38 s, with the same frames and audio; the converter's byte-for-byte test now checks this variant against the general shader.

### Delivery, encoding and audio

- x264 receives the renderer's nv12 frames as they are: asking it for yuv420p put a software conversion of every frame in front of an encoder that interleaves the chroma again. The stream is byte for byte the same; on 140 frames of a 1080p chapter x264 used 8.21 s of user CPU instead of 8.59 s.
- The audio mixer allocates each silent channel zeroed instead of writing one channel of zeros and copying it into the others. Copying those buffers was a quarter of the main thread's CPU time in a 10 s lavapipe encode slice, whose mix still spans the whole programme; such slices encoded in 9.78 s instead of 10.26 s together, with the same frames and audio.
- A delivery rendered in segments starts its join as soon as the last segment's worker returns: the coordinator used to notice that only at its next 200 ms progress tick. On lavapipe a 25 s chapter encoded in 4.35 s instead of 4.43 s on average over four runs, with the same frames.

### Changes that can alter output from v0.7.2

- On a software adapter a path-traced scene the CPU tracer takes changes at float level: its frames differ from the shader's by 69 to 84 dB PSNR on the 02-fracture scene. `SR_PT_CPU=0` restores the shader. Scenes with media, water or foam, adaptive sampling or light grids keep the shader and their frames.
- On a software adapter a camera with ambient occlusion and no screen-space reflections or contact shadows renders its occlusion at half size, within 49.1 dB PSNR of the full-size pass on a 736-frame claymation.
- On a GPU every frame and audio packet is as in v0.7.2.

## v0.7.2 — 2026-10-22

This release renders, encodes and mixes faster without changing what it delivers: cached vector layers are rasterised again only where they changed, pipelines are compiled when first drawn, clay, simulations and rigid bodies use every core, and the audio chain and the video encoder start beside the picture instead of before or after it. A 13-chapter 1080p explainer series encoded on lavapipe in 142.5 s instead of 428.5 s and with NVENC on an RTX 3050 in 41.2 s instead of 87.2 s, every frame the same. GPU readbacks that outlast the driver's fence wait no longer read an unmapped buffer. The vendored schema is still sr-core 1.7.0.

### Rendering

- A cached vector layer is rasterised again only in the tiles whose inputs changed since its last frame: each tile keeps a hash of its commands, pieces, backdrops and resolved paints, and a target reused from the previous frame redraws only the tiles whose hash moved, while a new target, a resized one or a different pipeline variant is rasterised whole. On lavapipe the first 20 s of a 1080p explainer chapter rendered in 9.22 s instead of 32.00 s, with the same frames.
- The compositor, raster stage and output converter compile a pipeline the first time a draw uses it instead of all of them when a renderer is built, so a chapter pays for the variants it draws (ch09 draws two per frame and was building 29). A one-frame software encode of such a chapter took about 5.7 s instead of 7.6 s on llvmpipe; the frames are unchanged.
- A geodesic camera's picture reads each sample's orbit from a table instead of integrating it. A ray's orbit depends only on its impact parameter and on whether it starts toward the hole, so each frame tabulates u(φ) and du/dφ for 2048 impact parameters (f64 RK4, crowded round the critical 3√3 M where the higher-order images are thin) and each sample interpolates its crossings and its escape angle from the two rows around it. A 1280 × 720 film at 64 samples per pixel encoded in 2.4 s instead of 16.0 s on an RTX 3050; its frames differ from the traced ones by 54.4 dB PSNR at worst (56.1 dB on average).

### Clay, simulation and physics

- Clay objects mesh their field on every core: each pass of the surface-nets extraction runs over independent slabs and joins them in order, so the mesh is the same, vertex for vertex and index for index, as before. A fingerprinted seven-blob object at `resolution="160"` meshed in 359.5 ms on one core and meshes in 45.4 ms on a 12-thread Ryzen 5 5600X; boiling clay re-meshes on every boil frame, so a 5 s clay film encoded in 4.6 s instead of 21.7 s there.
- Clay evaluates its fingerprint roughness only near the surface. The roughness moves the surface by a bounded amount and the joined field is 1-Lipschitz, so beyond that reach and two cell diagonals the smooth field alone gives the side a grid point is on, which is all the mesher reads there; the mesh is the same. A fingerprinted seven-blob object at `resolution="160"` evaluates it at 10 % of its 2.4 million grid points and meshes in 31 ms instead of 45 ms.
- Clay also skips the field itself away from the surface: a block of 4 × 4 × 4 grid points whose centre lies further from the surface than the block's half-diagonal is wholly on one side of it, and keeps the centre's value for its sign. The fingerprinted showcase body evaluates its field 0.45 million times instead of 2.4 million and meshes in 22 ms; a 5 s clay film encoded in 3.0 s instead of 3.7 s on a Ryzen 5 5600X, its mesh and frames unchanged.
- Documents with a simulation evaluate the next frame on a helper thread while the current one renders, as documents without one already did. A simulated document gives each frame the same graph whether or not the frame after it was evaluated first, which every simulated corpus document is now tested for; the frames are unchanged. A 5 s film of a flock, a slime mould and a smoke solver spent 3.3 s instead of 4.0 s waiting on evaluation.
- Rigid bodies that are closed meshes, the pieces of every fracture among them, are decomposed into convex parts on every core when their world is built, each body then taking its own in order, so the world and every frame of it are the same as before. Twelve mesh pieces built in 1.32 s on one core and in 0.40 s on a 6-core Ryzen 5 5600X; a 5 s fracture film encoded in 6.7 s instead of 8.7 s there.
- `slime` and `fluid` step on every core with the same results to the bit: Physarum agents move in parallel from the trail as it was at the start of the step and deposit in agent order, the trail diffuses by rows, and the smoke solver relaxes its pressure one colour of its red-black order at a time with the colours held apart, advecting velocity and dye by rows. At the size of a 384 × 480 panel a 60 000-agent step took 4.97 ms on one thread and takes 1.22 ms on a 12-thread Ryzen 5 5600X, a 128-cell smoke step 7.70 ms and 4.10 ms; a film of both and a flock encoded in 3.9 s instead of 6.5 s there.

### Delivery, encoding and audio

- `encode` starts choosing its video encoder (a test encode with each hardware encoder in turn) as delivery begins, beside the audio mix and the renderer's setup, instead of after them; a caller that asks while the probe runs waits for its answer instead of probing again. A one-frame encode of a 5 s film took about 0.2 s less on an RTX 3050 with NVENC; the encoder chosen and the output are the same.
- A video split into chunks opens each chunk's encoder while the chunk before it renders, finishes a chunk's encoder on a thread of its own, and queues up to 48 frames (at most about 96 MB) in front of an encoder instead of 3, which a hardware encoder needs while it opens its session. The chunks, their encoder arguments and the frames are unchanged. NVENC is also given nv12 frames as nv12 instead of through a software conversion to yuv420p.
- The audio mix decodes all its sources at once, each in an FFmpeg of its own, before building its nodes, instead of one after another as each node is built; a source that cannot be decoded still fails where it is used. Five 5 s films with three to seven tracks each encoded with the same audio, bit for bit, in 15.2 s together instead of 18.4 s on a Ryzen 5 5600X with an RTX 3050, this change and the encoder probe above taken together.
- An AAC soundtrack held to the master's true-peak ceiling is encoded once. The ceiling's last probe is the encode of the mix as it is delivered, so an MP4, MOV or MKV muxes that stream as it is instead of decoding the mix's WAV and encoding it again, and the WAV that second encode read is not written. The audio packets are the same (MD5 of the stream copied out, before and after, on two films with GPU and with `--hw software`), and so are the frames.
- The audio chain (mix, loudness, true-peak ceiling, the files the mux reads) runs on a thread of its own while the picture renders when nothing the picture draws reads the mix: no expression, link or audiogram reads a track's analysis, no beat grid takes its beats from a track, no shader effect declares an `audio` or `audioFFT` input, and the output has no overlay. The encoder is started, or the chunks joined, once the chain's files are ready; the report's `audio_beside_picture` says which order a delivery used. Otherwise the mix still comes first. Frames and audio packets are the same as when the mix came first, for one stream, joined chunks and two-pass.

### GPU readback

- GPU readbacks wait for their mapping and report why one failed. A frame whose OpenGL submission outlasted the driver's single fence wait (`w15-path-cost` at 1920 × 1080) made the wait return `Timeout`, which was ignored, and the frame was read from a buffer that was not mapped yet ("Buffer is not mapped"). Every readback now polls until the map callback delivers its result (at most 120 s) and fails with the cause (map error, device error, poll error or timeout) instead.

### Changes that can alter output from v0.7.1

- A geodesic camera's frames change: each sample's orbit is interpolated from the frame's table instead of integrated, within 54.4 dB PSNR of the traced frames. Every other change leaves frames and audio packets as they were.
- A GPU readback whose mapping fails or does not arrive within 120 s fails the render with its cause instead of reading the buffer unmapped.

## v0.7.0 — 2026-10-20

This release lets the path tracer stop tracing pixels whose mean has converged, denoises lighting apart from surface colour, finds its hits with hardware ray queries where the adapter offers them, and compiles its media shaders on NVIDIA's OpenGL driver. Validation warns about, and `inspect` reports, the cost of each path-traced camera, and more of the declared 3D and layout attributes are implemented. The vendored schema is still sr-core 1.7.0.

### Path tracer

- `render --path-noise REL` and `encode --path-noise REL` stop tracing a path-traced pixel once, from 16 samples on, its luminance and coverage are within REL relative standard error of their means; `pathSamples` caps the rest. A stopped pixel keeps its mean, so denoised and tiled output match a full render's statistics. `render --stats` reports `paths` with the `traced` samples against the `budget`. Without the flag rendering is unchanged.
- The path tracer's denoiser divides each pixel by its coverage and first-hit albedo before filtering and multiplies it back after, and sets how far a tap may differ in luminance from the variance of each pixel's own samples. Against a 4096-sample reference it halves the error at 8 samples while keeping fine albedo texture (the previous filter kept 46 % of it and its error exceeded the noisy frame's), no longer raises the error of an already clean 256-sample frame (previously 5.7 times), and leaves silhouette coverage as traced (previously 1.6 times the noisy error). `denoise` defaults to true, so path-traced output intentionally changes; set `denoise="false"` for the raw estimate.
- Where the adapter offers hardware ray queries, the path tracer finds its hits through acceleration structures the driver builds for each pass, on the GPU's ray-tracing units where it has them, and otherwise walks its own BVH as before. Both trace the same paths with the same random numbers, so images agree up to rays whose nearest hit differs by rounding. `SR_GPU_RAY_QUERY=0` keeps the BVH walk. `gpus` reports `ray_query` for each adapter and `render --stats` reports `hardware_rays` under `paths`. The JSON envelope is `schema_version` 1.2.0.
- On NVIDIA's OpenGL driver, which inlines every function call, the path tracer's media shaders compile in bounded memory. A lit medium gathers its in-scattered light in one loop over lights, crossed domains and surfaces instead of marching a shadow ray inside every step of the transport march, and the shader specializer removes code under overrides that are false. Before, compiling a frame with lit smoke or gas could exhaust memory. Images do not change beyond rounding.

### Path-traced cost

- W15 warns when a `renderer="pathtrace"` camera traces more than 1920 × 1080 × 256 × 4 path segments per frame: its frame's width × height × `pathSamples` × `maxBounces`, the frame being its `viewport3D`'s or the project's. 320 samples at 20 bounces in 1080p is 6.25 times that.
- `inspect` lists each path-traced camera's frame size, samples, bounces and path segments per frame, marking those W15 warns about; in JSON they are `path_costs`.

### Command line

PATH must keep the output's file type; several `--output` ids, an unknown id, or `--codec` with `--output` are usage errors (exit status 2).

Before, the frame failed with "the palette index N has no material" although the file has colours; `tests/corpus/valid/voxels.scene.xml` now renders.

This release draws 3D objects on OpenGL, gives every command a versioned JSON output and a read-only MCP server, adds image scopes and fuller loudness measurement, implements schema attributes that earlier versions declared but did not apply, and turns malformed or oversized input into reported errors instead of panics. ### Declared attributes now implemented

- Pixel motion blur declares its amount and sample controls and respects the quality-tier sample budget (8 in draft), retaining the existing 64-tap maximum.

## v0.4.1 — 2026-10-11

This release vendors sr-core schema 1.7.0 byte-identical (document version 1.7) and implements its one change, SREP 87: ids that a program emits are scoped by the program's id. It also reports SREP 72's precision findings under the SREP's codes, and fixes how an included document's references are resolved.

### Schema 1.7.0 and SREP 87

- **Document version 1.7.** `scene/@version` accepts `1.7`. Version 1.7 adds no syntax and gates nothing, so a 1.7 document validates and renders as the same document at 1.6.
- **Program-emitted ids (SREP 87).** An id `E` that the build-mode program `P` emits becomes `P.E` in the document, so two programs, or a program and the document, can use the same local ids.
  - Inside an output, IDREF and IDREFS tokens, `prop` and `propAtTime` ids and the node id of `link/@source` resolve to the output's own ids first.
  - A bare reference from outside resolves when exactly one program emitted the id, with the new warning W13, and fails with the new error PRG17 when several did. The same holds for `override/@target` of a layout, a variant, an instance or an include.
  - The bone names in an emitted skeleton's skin weights are its local ids.
  - Local ids are kept per document of the build, so a written id of one document that equals an emitted id of another keeps its seed.
  - PRG14 covers a repeated id within one output and a namespaced id equal to another id.
- **Kit.** sr-core's 15 SREP 87 cases and the amended SREP 66 collision case pass on this engine. Four of them are failure cases that `run.py` cannot check; they were checked by hand.

### SREP 72 follow-ups

- **PREC1.** `project/@precision="f32"` in a document with 3D content gives the information finding PREC1, once, in `validate` and in the render report. Its message starts with SREP 72's words, "3D internal buffers stay f16". It replaces the engine's E23, which said the same and had no other use. PREC1 covers every kind the 3D pass draws: `object3D`, `particles3D`, `ocean`, `blackHole` and `accretionDisk`. sr-core's erratum of 2026-10-11 adds the last two to SREP 72's list, and SREP 72 is now Final.
- **PREC10.** A device without float32 blending or filtering already failed an f32 render, naming the missing feature. The error message now starts with "PREC10:", and the render report gives it the code PREC10, also when an output layer meets it first.

### Fixes

- **References inside an include name the include's own document.**
  - A transformConstraint's `pole` resolves in the constraint's own scope, as its `target` already did.

### SREP follow-ups

- **Version gates (SREPs 76, 80 and 83).** BH1, VOX1 and SREP 40's gates V8, MSQ1, P3D1, OCN1, CRT1, FRX1 and GEO1 refuse versions 1.0 to 1.2 only, and their messages say "1.3 or later". - **Warning renames (SREP 76).** The black-hole warnings are W11 (a hole or disk that no geodesic camera draws) and W12 (`denoise` on a geodesic camera). In v0.3.2 they were W03 and W04, codes that SREP 57 gives to its generated-marker warnings.
- **Boolean values (SREPs 76, 77 and 84).** Rules read an `xs:boolean` attribute by its value: "true" and "1" are true, "false" and "0" are false, and surrounding whitespace is ignored. This covers BH5 to BH8, W11, W12 and W05 (`geodesics`, `denoise`), CRT11 and CRT12 (`mantle`), C15, C43, R15, R48, VOL5, C80, GEO2, PYRO9 to PYRO11 and the warning W02. Integer attributes in these rules are read whatever their sign, leading zeros or padding.
- **Decimal and double values (SREP 85).** C13, C14, C15, C37, C38, C44, C52, C53, C54, C56, PYRO1, PYRO2, PYRO4, VOL4, P3D3, P3D10, OCN3, OCN5, PYRO11, PYC6, FRX11, ITR1 and SRT1 read their numeric attributes by value, so "+1" is 1. In v0.3.2, XPath's `number()` read "+1" as NaN. Exponent forms such as `1e0` follow the native validator, which is normative for them. INF, -INF and NaN compare as the Schematron compares them.
- **Disk profile (SREP 76).** The geodesic pass divides the thin-disk profile by its exact maximum, (6/7)^(3/2) 7^(-1/4) ≈ 0.48787, instead of 0.4880.
- **Supersample filter (SREP 89).** The down filter clamps each channel to [0, max(1, M)], where M is the largest sample of non-zero weight, instead of [0, 1]. Colour finishing (exposure, looks, tone mapping) runs on the filtered frame. A frame whose samples all lie within [0, 1] gives the same bytes as before.

### Fixes

- **Includes and instances.** References written inside an included document, a symbol instance or a repeat copy resolve in that scope. Before, the 3D renderer and the rigid-body world compared them as written, so an included black hole lost its disk and its camera lost its look-at target.
- **Included definitions.** An included document's materials, material maps, effects (and their shader files), fonts and maps resolve in that document. Before, the renderer looked them up in the including document only, so an included `object3D` failed with "material not found" or took the including document's material of the same id. The assets, effects and materials an include merges animate on the include's clock, as its content does. A compute node's `src` and a transition's `shader` resolve from the directory of the document that writes them. Sections an include does not merge (lights, paints, styles, physics, audio, markers, parameters and the others) are reported as E12.
- **threeD groups.** A group, instance, include or repeat with `threeD` is placed in 2.5D as one picture, with or without effects. Before, a group without effects drew its children flat and dropped its `zDepth`, `rotationX` and `rotationY` without a warning. A `threeD` child of such a group goes through the camera once, and the group's sheet keeps what it draws up to 2048 px beyond the frame. Repeat copies are not placed one by one. In 360 renders, a group with a `threeD` of its own is placed as one picture.
- **Validation with programs.** `validate` reports errors that appear only once build-mode programs run, such as two programs emitting the same id (PRG14). Before, these documents passed validation and failed at render.
- **Bloom knee.** The soft knee of bloom and halation never reaches below black. With a threshold under 0.5, dark ground was lifted (#050505 became #1C1C1C).
- **Texel-aligned composites.** An offscreen or layer whose texels land one to one on whole target pixels is copied with `textureLoad` rather than sampled. Sampling weighed in the neighbouring texel on llvmpipe and rounded about a quarter of the edge channels one step down.
- **ISF pass buffers.** Reads at a texel centre return that texel exactly, so cellular automata and jump-flood passes do not freeze in bands or stripe as they did on llvmpipe. Sampler names are bound per scope, and a failed compile names `[GLSL-F64]` or `[GLSL-SAMPLER-ARG]` with the user's line.
- **Memory.** Decoded images unused for a few frames are evicted. Vector targets beyond a two-frame budget are rasterised just before their draw and released after it, instead of all at once for the whole frame. The text face cache keeps only faces whose font bytes are still in use.
- **Speed.** Zero-opacity leaves are skipped, adjustment layers reuse their backdrop snapshot, and parsed font faces are cached. Renderer CPU per frame fell by 20 to 23 % on a measured film, with byte-identical frames.

### Sound across transitions (SREP 82)

A transition now mixes the sound of the video layers on its sides, including layers inside a side, by its `audio` attribute, at the eased progress p:
- `crossfade` (the default) gives the outgoing side 1 − p and the incoming side p;
- `equal-power` gives cos(πp/2) and sin(πp/2);
- `cut` switches from one side to the other where p reaches ½;
- `none` leaves both sides at their own volume.

Sequence junctions crossfade, except that a `cut` junction cuts its sound with its picture. The gains multiply the layers' volume automation, one value per frame, interpolated between frames. Before this release `audio` was read by no code, and both sides played at full volume over the whole window.

- **Prewarm and stepsPerFrame memory.** A frame that takes several steps now runs them as one looped pass over two textures, as `iterate` does. - **Morph transition.** The luminance displacement described in the README is restored; a rewrite of the transition shader had reduced morph to a crossfade.
- **`--parallel auto`.** The split now depends only on the programme: one chunk per 10 s, at most 12, and one stream under 20 s. The host decides only how many chunks render at once, so the same scene splits the same way on any machine.
