# scene-render v0.9.1

This release renders one video on several machines. `scene-render serve` is a render worker, and `encode --workers` hands chunks of a video output to workers on other machines beside the one that encodes; the chunks are joined without encoding again, and a lossless output decodes to the frames of a serial render. A scene document may now hold 256 MiB and 16,000,000 XML nodes. The release also carries what was merged after v0.9.0: the relativity draft of sr-core for documents of version 1.8, a Dockerfile that compiles any git reference with a wrapper that runs the image in place of the host binary, and two fixes to `schedule`. A render without workers gives the frames it gave in v0.9.0. The CLI output schema is 1.4.0 with one more command name, `serve`, and each output's report gains `remote`.

## Rendering on several machines

- `scene-render serve [--listen ADDRESS] [--parallel N] [--cache DIR] [--cache-bytes N] [--token-file PATH] [--once]` runs a render worker, and `encode --workers HOST[:PORT],…` (or the environment variable `SR_WORKERS`) has workers render chunks of its single-pass video outputs. The coordinator splits an output into chunks of at least two seconds, three for each worker here and remote, and keeps them in one queue: every worker takes the next chunk as it finishes the last, the workers here from the start of the output and the remote ones from its end, so a faster machine renders more. Each chunk is encoded from a keyframe with the output's own settings, as a parallel delivery's chunks are, and the coordinator joins them without encoding again and muxes the one audio mix. `sr_deliver::remote` holds the delivery's side: `Options::remote` (the workers, behind the `RemoteWorkers` trait), `Options::segment` (render one chunk and nothing else, which is what a worker is asked for) and `Report::remote` (what each worker rendered).
- A lossless output joined from remote chunks decodes to exactly the frames of a serial render (`crates/sr-deliver/tests/remote_workers.rs`, `crates/scene-render/tests/cli/workers.rs`). Measured on a 509-frame 1080 × 1920 film of one acted 3D character, software H.264, coordinator on a Tesla P40: 50 s serially, 30 s with `--parallel 3`, and 14 s with `--parallel 3` and three workers of two chunks at once each (an RTX 4050 Laptop, an RTX 3070 Ti Laptop and an RTX 3050).
- A worker that cannot be reached, is another version, holds another token or cannot load the scene is left out with a note, and the encode goes on with the others or alone. A chunk a worker fails (it was stopped, the link broke, no message came for `SR_WORKER_TIMEOUT` seconds, 600 by default) returns to the queue for another worker; that worker takes no more, the report lists it under `remote` with the reason, and a warning is added. Outputs that render as one stream (two passes, `maxFileSize`, flash or contrast checks, the kinds `--parallel` does not split) render on the coordinator alone, with a warning that the workers were not used. With workers, `--hw auto` encodes in software, so that every chunk comes from the same encoder; a family named with `--hw` is asked of every machine.
- The coordinator sends the document and every file it names (what `watch` follows), each under its SHA-256 and once. The worker keeps them in `--cache`, lays each scene out under a directory of its own, holds the last two scenes loaded in memory, removes the files used longest ago once the cache passes `--cache-bytes` (32 GiB by default, never the scene being opened) and forgets scene directories not opened for seven days. A file the document names by an absolute path, or a font it takes from the system, is not sent: the worker's load says what it misses and that worker is left out. A worker never writes outside its cache and runs nothing it is sent.
- `serve --parallel N` renders N chunks at once, each on a GPU device of its own, and a coordinator opens one link for each (`host #1`, `host #2` in the report). When the GPU runs out of memory the chunk fails (another worker renders it), the render thread that met it destroys its device and renders no more, and later coordinators are told the lower count; chunks that fail together lower it by one, once. On a 6 GB and on an 8 GB card, three 1080 × 1920 renders at once settle at two.
- The link is JSON-RPC 2.0 over TCP, protocol `scene-render-worker/1` (`crates/scene-render/src/net.rs`). Each message carries an HMAC-SHA256, under a key both sides derive from a shared token (the environment variable `SR_WORKER_TOKEN`, or `--token-file`) and from random bytes each contributes to the session, of the sender's side, its message count and the message; a file follows the message that announces its length and SHA-256. A side without the token cannot open a link, and a message that was altered, repeated or reordered ends it. Nothing is encrypted: across a network others can read, the link belongs in a tunnel.
- Machines with different GPUs do not draw the same pixels. Between the four cards above a frame of that film differs by 36 to 39 dB PSNR, along outlines, where two renders on one card are 50 dB apart (the extra keyframes); at a join between chunks of two cards, two neighbouring frames differ by 36 dB where one card gives 47 dB. Frames that are measured or compared are rendered without workers.

## Render threads and the NVIDIA driver

- A worker's chunks render on threads it starts once and never ends. With NVIDIA's driver 580 on Linux, a Vulkan device destroyed while another thread that used the driver ends can stop both for good: caught in a debugger, one thread was inside `vkDestroyDevice` and another, ending, inside the driver's per-thread cleanup. It showed as two deliveries with several devices each, at once in one process, stopping about one run in ten; in separate processes none of 120 did. A session's thread never touches the driver, and a device is destroyed only on its own render thread. The tests of `sr-deliver` that open several devices run one after the other for the same reason.

## Document limits

- A scene document may hold 256 MiB (it was 64 MiB) and 16,000,000 XML nodes (4,000,000). A cast of 3D characters keyed on every frame reaches 64 MiB in a few seconds of film, with about four million nodes by then, so the two limits move together. Loading grows faster than the size: on one machine a 69 MiB document loads in 20 s with 3.8 GB of memory and a 238 MiB one in 162 s with 12 GB. A document past a limit is still refused before it is read.

## Relativity

- A 1.8 document may hold the relativity draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-relativity.md`): an `axes` gains a `spacetime` child (c, boost, grid, compact, blackHole) with `event`, `worldline` and `lightRay` children, drawn as path shapes for its grid, the light cones of its events, its worldlines, its light rays and its event points; `sr_3d::relativity` holds the draft's kernel. A light ray's length is in units of t, and a boost that reaches |β| ≥ 1 is a frame problem that draws nothing.

## Docker

- The `Dockerfile` compiles `scene-render` from any git reference (`SR_GIT` at `SR_REF`) instead of the build context, so it builds every version, and `tools/docker/scene-render` runs the image in place of the host binary: it reaches the providers `resolve` runs on the host, and stops its container when it is killed. NVIDIA GPUs in the container need `NVIDIA_DRIVER_CAPABILITIES=all`.

## Fixes

- `schedule` counts Mesa's OpenGL name of a GPU as that GPU, and `--rate` can name a GPU whose whole name is part of another's.

## What can alter output from v0.9.0

- Nothing for a render without workers: the frames are the same. An encode with `--workers` splits the output into more chunks than `--parallel` alone does, so a lossy file has keyframes at other places, and chunks rendered on another GPU carry that GPU's rounding.
- A document between 64 MiB and 256 MiB, or with 4,000,000 to 16,000,000 XML nodes, was refused and now loads.
