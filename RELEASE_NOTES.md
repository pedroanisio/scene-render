# scene-render v0.7.5

This release keeps what renderers make from one frame to the next and shares it between them, bounds what simulations and caches hold over a long run, and moves delivery work off the render thread. 3D passes, vector raster jobs, particle batches, video layers, looped shaders and the path tracer keep their buffers and bind groups between submits; renderers on one GPU device share their pipelines; the compositor batches its draws by dependency. The 2D physics world and emitter keep a bounded set of checkpoints, the 3D engine and the volume cache drop their least recently used entries, and audio sources, mesh sequences and the geo caches hold one copy of their data. Delivery reads the flash check one frame late, evaluates motion blur samples in parallel, packs planar YUV on the GPU and reads two-pass intermediates in FFmpeg, and `render` writes PNG files on a writer thread. A path traced scene with water and media compiles one march through the media, which cuts the NVIDIA OpenGL link time of its scattering variants by about 40 %. Each change is measured below. The 736-frame 1280x720 06-marx-engels-clay film, whose time is the 3D passes and x264 on the CPU, encodes on lavapipe to the same frames as with v0.7.4 in 69.64 s and 70.97 s instead of 69.80 s and 76.75 s (user CPU 573 s and 575 s instead of 570 s and 573 s, peak RSS 2.02 to 2.10 GiB instead of 2.02 to 2.04 GiB). The changes that can alter output are listed at the end. The CLI output schema is 1.3.0, and the vendored schema is still sr-core 1.7.0.

## Path tracing

- On a GPU adapter the path tracer keeps the scratch its tiles work in from one pass to the next: the sums and guides of each pixel, the stand-in buffer, and the denoiser's two halves with their bind groups, grown only when a tile needs more and dropped when the tile budget changes. Before, every pass created and released those buffers, four of 32 bytes per pixel, about 118 MB for a 1280x720 pass. A CPU adapter still makes them for each pass: they are host memory its allocator recycles, and keeping them only raised the peak. The numbers are under the next entry, which was measured with this one.
- The CPU path tracer keeps what it builds from a scene for the passes over the same geometry and textures: the dense instance records, the atlas packed into words and its own top-level BVH, by geometry identity and atlas key, at most four scenes and 16 MB in all. It also reads the dome from the texels the engine keeps instead of copying them into every pass, 32 MB for a 2K dome. `Stats3.pt_cpu_scenes` counts the scenes a pass built: 0 when a kept one served or the GPU traced. With the kept scratch above, on 02-fracture frames 60-120 on an RTX 3050 (serial, alternated runs) `pt_pack_seconds` fell from 8.5-8.6 ms to 4.2-4.6 ms per frame, the frame median from 77.8-79.4 ms to 76.2-76.8 ms and peak host RSS from 930-936 MB to 878-879 MB; GPU time per frame is unchanged (63.3-63.9 ms). On lavapipe (pipelined) the frame median went from 1902-1921 ms to 1805-1877 ms and peak RSS from 1332-1341 MB to 1347-1356 MB: the fracture pieces move every frame, so every pass is another scene to the tracer there, and the kept scenes add their bound to the peak. Frames are the same.
- A path traced scene with water and media (smoke, fog, volumes) compiles one march through the media instead of two: the water's march, which dims the media's light by the water in front of it, also marches the media outside the water, where it absorbs nothing. NVIDIA's OpenGL driver inlines every call, so each march carried its own copy of the media's scattering and of the scattering random walk. With the driver's shader cache off on an RTX 3050, a lit water variant links in 21.9 s instead of 28.9 s and with scattering in 35.6 s instead of 59.0 s, and its peak host memory falls from 4.63 GB to 2.47 GB; with a volume grid, 33.0 s instead of 42.4 s and 48.3 s instead of 77.6 s (5.30 GB to 2.70 GB). Variants without water are unchanged. Frames are the same.

## Evaluation

- Per-frame evaluation does less work that nothing reads. A node's keyframes, links and expressions are left unevaluated while the node is off screen and nothing reads them. Nodes are still evaluated when something reads them off screen: an expression, a link or z-order; a node another one is parented to, and that node's ancestors; or a transition. Constraints are looked for only on the nodes that have one, found when the program is built, not by walking every node's children each frame. Each slot keeps the noise channel of its property instead of hashing the property name on every `wiggle()` call. A number wiggled by a number takes a scalar path that allocates nothing. A map's fitted camera is kept for the program, so `geo()` no longer reads the fit files' metadata on every call. The frame's output lists are reserved for the nodes alive in it. On sr-eval's benchmark scene (1,020 nodes, 5,500 slots, 500 expressions, all on screen, no maps), the frame median fell from 0.864 ms to 0.805 ms: ten alternated pairs, each one faster. The off-screen and map savings scale with a document's off-screen nodes and `geo()` calls, which that scene does not have. Frames are the same.

## Simulation

- A 2D particle that collides is tested only against the colliders whose bounding box holds it. The boxes are found once per physics step, when the step's colliders are kept for the particles. Before, every live colliding particle was projected onto every collider on every step. On the physics benchmark scene (about 1,200 live sparks against 205 colliders), evaluating frame 300 went from 1.69 s to 1.17 s (five alternated runs each), with the same graph.
- A 3D particle emitter reserves room for the particles it carries rather than for its `maxParticles`. With the default cap that was about 1.8 MB on every fixed step and in the frame it publishes. The death times of the live particles are heaped in one pass.
- A smoke domain's solid mask is computed on every core. A domain with no colliders skips the mask altogether. In the profile of a 128x104x128 domain, the obstacle stage went from 24 ms to 14 ms a step with a slab and a sphere, and from 8 ms to 0.1 ms with none. The mask and the solid cells' face velocities are the same.

## Memory

- The 2D physics world and the 2D particle emitter keep a bounded set of replay checkpoints, as the 3D world and the other simulations already did: at most 65 under the shared 256 MB budget, thinned by doubling their spacing. Before, they kept one a second for the whole run. Evaluating the last frame of a 600-second copy of the physics benchmark scene (one run each) peaked at 912,404 KiB of RSS before and 115,740 KiB after, with the same graph.
- The 3D world charges a collider shape, or the kept cells of a body that can be cut, once for all the checkpoints that share it rather than once per checkpoint, and measures it only when a checkpoint first holds it. What the checkpoints share with the initial checkpoint, which is outside the budget, is not charged. A world with a 5,000-triangle ground used to thin to two checkpoints under an 8 MB budget, and now keeps one a second.
- A mesh sequence's blended frame shares the texture pixels of the frames it blends rather than copying them. With two 2048x2048 textures on a 50,000-vertex model, a blended sample went from 24.3 ms to 6.5 ms, and it no longer holds a 32 MB copy.
- Audio sources are decoded straight into one buffer per channel as FFmpeg writes them. Before, a source was held twice at its peak: the encoded bytes beside the samples, then the interleaved samples beside the per-channel buffers. A 20-second stereo source held 16.1 MB at once for 7.7 MB of audio; it now holds the audio once, plus at most a sixty-fourth and a second of room, which is given back.
- The process-wide geo caches (features, fitted cameras, tile archives and style files) keep one reading per file and replace it when the file changes on disk. Before, every revision of a file a preview session saw stayed cached. Each cache is also capped at 256 entries. The tile and elevation caches hold their archive weakly, so a replaced archive's address cannot be taken for the new one's.
- The grids of a cut body of cells are dropped with the revisions the 3D world drops from its budgeted history.
- The 3D engine drops the meshes and textures no pass of a frame used, least recently used first, once the idle ones of each kind exceed 256 MB (shared between delivery workers like the other budgets). Before, every mesh and texture a session uploaded was kept until the renderer closed. What a frame draws is never dropped, whatever the budget. `--stats` reports the count as `assets_evicted`.
- The volume cache drops the least recently used decoded volumes and baked frames until a new one fits its 256 MB budget or its 64 entries of a kind, instead of clearing every entry. A volume that does not fit the budget alone is used for the frame but not kept, and pushes nothing out.
- Renderers on one GPU device share their pipelines instead of each compiling and holding its own. The compositor's layouts, shader and pipelines are shared per device and working format. The 3D engine's are shared per device, G-buffer format and sample count, the path tracer's and the rasterizer's per device and format, and the video engine's and the built-in effects' per device. Custom shader effects keep their own. A pipeline is still compiled the first time a draw uses it, now once for every renderer on the device, and the shared set is dropped when the last renderer on the device that uses it is. Parallel delivery workers render on one device, and so do concurrent tests. On lavapipe, which holds about 35 MiB for a compositor pipeline, the sr-gpu test binaries run with 20 test threads in two alternated runs each: `volume_grids` peaked at 536-539 MB of RSS instead of 2.38-2.44 GB and took 11.5-12.1 s instead of 92.5-93.9 s; `include_scope` peaked at 559-567 MB instead of 1.88-1.91 GB and took 2.3 s instead of 17.2 s. Frames are the same.

## 3D

- Each 3D pass writes its frame, light, tile, shadow-matrix, object, material and draw-order data into buffers the engine keeps from one submit to the next, grown when a pass needs more, and keeps the material bind groups with them, remade only when the material buffer grew or a material's maps changed. Before, every pass created those seven buffers and all of its material bind groups. Passes recorded between two submits each get their own set, as a `queue.write_buffer` lands before the next submit's first command; the renderer tells the engine after each submit, and up to 16 sets are kept. `Stats3.buffers_created` and `Stats3.material_binds_created` count what a pass made: 0 for a warm pass. With the entries below, on an RTX 3050 (serial, alternated runs of 48 frames) perf_3d's `submit_ms` went from 0.75-0.79 ms to 0.66 ms and its frame median from 4.25-4.40 ms to 4.12-4.13 ms with GPU time unchanged (3.1-3.3 ms); perf_text and 02-fracture frames 60-120 are unchanged within run-to-run noise on both adapters (fracture 74.9-76.2 ms before, 74.7-75.8 ms after on the GPU; 1736-1758 ms before, 1745-1754 ms after on lavapipe), and on lavapipe perf_3d's median spread between pipelined runs (452-580 ms before, 288-559 ms after) is wider than any difference between the binaries. `buffers_created` is 0 on every warm frame of the three scenes. Frames are byte-identical on both adapters.
- The rest of what a 3D pass made for itself goes the same way: the vertex buffers of deformed (skinned or morphed) draws, the splat sorts' parameters and radix scratch, and the splat draws' object uniforms are kept in the pass's set or by the engine and written in place, and the IES atlas is kept while the lights hold the same profiles instead of being packed and uploaded every pass. Under f32 precision the f16 target a 3D pass draws into comes from the texture pool, which now keys its free textures by size and format, instead of being made for the frame and dropped. Morphing and skinning run per vertex on the rayon pool with the same sums in the same order, so the vertices are the same to the bit.

## Vector

- Each vector raster job appends its parameters, commands, ranges, pieces, backdrops and active tiles to six arenas the raster stage keeps between submits, at 256-byte offsets, bound by offset and size; an arena is replaced by a larger one when a job does not fit, and all are rewound after each submit. Before, every job made six buffers. `RenderStats.buffers_created` (`--stats`) counts the buffers a frame made for its 3D passes, particle batches and raster jobs: 0 for a warm frame.

## Particles

- Each particle batch writes its instances into a buffer the engine keeps between submits (up to 64), grown when a batch needs more, instead of a new buffer every frame.

## Compositor

- The compositor batches its draws by dependency rather than by adjacency. A draw whose blend mode reads the backdrop goes into the first batch after the batches of every earlier draw over any of its pixels, and a normal draw into the batch of the latest such draw, so draws that share no pixel share one render pass and one set of backdrop copies whatever lies between them in the composition; two draws over the same pixel keep their paint order. Before, a batch took only consecutive draws, and any normal draw ended it for the backdrop draws after it. The earlier draws to test come from a grid over the batch run's extent with about one cell per median draw (at most 64 a side), tested exactly on their bounds, so a run of thousands of small disjoint draws costs a test per neighbour and a run of a few hundred layer-sized draws a plain pairwise test (84 µs a frame on perf_layers). `--stats` and `render --bench --json` report the passes as `compositor_passes`. On perf_layers (1080p60, 200 layers in 33 blend modes, RTX 3050, 120 frames, three alternated runs each) the frame went from 175 compositor passes to 46, GPU time per frame from 12.6-12.7 ms to 12.2 ms and the frame median from 14.6-14.9 ms to 14.0-14.2 ms; the 174 backdrop copies remain. The first 24 frames are byte-identical to the previous binary's.
- A video layer's frames are converted, mixed and warped in passes recorded while the frame is planned and submitted once, ahead of the frame's own command buffer, into textures from the renderer's pool that go back to it when the frames leave the cache. The plane textures and conversion uniforms, the luma pyramids, flow levels, patch buffer and bindings of the optical flow, and the parameters of the blends are kept by size and reused by the next frame; a frame that converts two frames of one size takes two sets. Before, every decoded frame made a texture, a uniform buffer and a bind group and was submitted on its own, a mix or warp the same again, and an optical flow made and dropped its twelve luma levels, six flow levels and patch buffer and submitted itself, and the frame textures were destroyed when they left the cache. `textures_created` (`--stats`) now counts video frame textures: a playing clip's first frames, then 0. A time-displacement effect's flow is recorded into the frame's command buffer instead of flushing it and submitting on its own. On an RTX 3050 (1080p60, 120 frames, three alternated runs each, machine load 7-9 from other work) a clip at half speed with optical flow went from a frame median of 1.6-3.9 ms to 0.5-0.9 ms and a submit of 0.83-0.88 ms to 0.18-0.59 ms; with frame mixing the submit went from 0.57-0.60 ms to 0.26-0.48 ms with the frame median within its run-to-run spread (1.0-1.6 ms); a clip at full speed is unchanged (1.56-1.59 ms before, 1.55-1.57 ms after), its frame being the plane upload. Frames 20 to 31 of both blended clips are byte-identical to the previous binary's.
- A looped stateful shader (`<iterate>`, and the steps of a frame of a `PERSISTENT` pass) records its steps in chunks of up to 64: the uniform blocks of a chunk's passes go into one uniform buffer the effects engine keeps between frames in one write, each pass is drawn at its block's offset with one of a few bind groups (a pass reads the same textures at every step of one parity), and the next chunk takes the same place once the chunk's command buffer is submitted. The fixed effect passes of a frame and single custom passes write the same buffer. An `until="converged"` check compares each persistent buffer with a copy kept from the last check in a compute pass that reduces the largest change over every channel of every texel to one number per buffer (a NaN counts as no change, as before), reading back a few bytes; the copies are kept by size and format. Before, every step of every pass made a uniform buffer and a bind group, and a check read every persistent buffer back in full and compared it on the CPU. `buffers_created` (`--stats`) now counts the effects engine's buffers: one or two at the first frame, then 0. On an RTX 3050 (1280x720, a 5-tap Jacobi relaxation in two passes, 12 frames, two alternated runs each, the machine under a test suite) 2000 steps a frame went from a frame median of 552-559 ms to 517-521 ms with the CPU time of the frame from 274-334 ms to 178-185 ms, and 400 steps with a convergence check every 100 from 326-330 ms to 125-126 ms (GPU time 316-321 ms to 118-119 ms). The first four frames of both scenes, and three frames of the effects scene, are byte-identical to the previous binary's.
- The compositor keeps the bind groups of its (backdrop, matte) texture pairs and of its cached mask coverage between frames, keyed by the textures' ids (every texture now carries one), for 4 frames after their last use; before, they were made again every frame, the dummy pair included. `--stats` reports them as `bind_groups_created`: a few at the first frame of a composition, then 0. The change detection of a frame hashes each node's subtree once per target space (it was hashed from its root, then again from every isolated group, matte and effect source holding it) and each element's animated state once, reads a text node's animator depth once instead of twice, and hashes particle positions, soft-body offsets and connector paths as they stream instead of collecting them. On an RTX 3050 (1080p60, 120 frames, two alternated runs each) perf_layers, perf_text and perf_effects are within their run-to-run spread (13.8 ms, 2.6 ms and 3.3-3.5 ms frame medians before and after): the work removed had measured in the tens of microseconds a frame. Four frames of each scene are byte-identical to the previous binary's.

## Delivery

- The flash check's 48x27 grid is collected one frame after it is submitted, on a second mapped buffer, instead of being read back while the render thread waits for it. Only the last frame of a programme waits. On perf_layers (1080p60, 600 frames, RTX 3050) with the flash check on, the encode went from 11.2 s to 8.9-9.2 s, level with the same scene with no check (8.65 s); the grid's cells are the same.
- The sample times of a motion blur are evaluated in parallel on the rayon pool once the blur knows it will draw them all, and the seconds they take reach the encode report as `subframe_seconds` (summed over workers) and the text summary as `shutter sub-frames`. The counterfactual probes of a contrast check still evaluate serially. Frames are the same; on perf_3d with 32 samples and perf_effects with 16, evaluation is under a millisecond a frame, so frame times do not move.
- Outputs whose encoder reads planar YUV get those planes packed on the GPU: ProRes yuv422p10le (4444 profiles yuv444p10le, with alpha yuva444p10le), DNxHR yuv422p or yuv422p10le, FFV1 with alpha yuva444p10le, and x265, SVT-AV1, libaom, rav1e and VP9 the 4:2:0 planes at 8 or 10 bits; libx264 and the hardware encoders keep NV12 and P010, which they read as they are. Before, those outputs read back 16-bit RGBA (8 bytes a pixel) or NV12 and FFmpeg converted every frame in software. The 4:2:0 planes are the NV12 and P010 planes deinterleaved, and the 4:2:2 and 4:4:4 codes match the RGB path's within one level. On perf_layers (1080p60, 600 frames, RTX 3050) to ProRes HQ the readback stage went from 2.9 s to 1.9 s; elapsed (30-31 s) and FFmpeg's CPU (339-341 s user) stay where prores_ks holds them.
- Each pass of a two-pass or size-fitted encode reads the lossless intermediate itself. Before, a second FFmpeg decoded it to raw frames that the delivery thread copied into the encoder's pipe. The encoder counts the frames FFmpeg reports and fails the pass, leaving the previous file, unless the intermediate decoded to exactly the frames rendered, so a short or unreadable intermediate still produces no output. On perf_layers (1080p60, 600 frames, RTX 3050) encoded two-pass to H.264 the file is byte-identical, the run starts 4 FFmpeg processes instead of 6 and its system CPU falls from 7.6 s to 3.1-3.2 s; elapsed and user CPU stay where libx264 holds them.
- The output layer's texts are measured for contrast at the frames the check's budget schedules, over the delivered picture, like the composition's own texts, and they share that budget of 256 probes (32 a text). Before, a layer with text was probed on every frame of the delivery: the layer rendered again per text, composited and read back while the render thread waited. The report's sampled-contrast note names layer texts like any others. On perf_layers with a two-text output layer (1080p60, 600 frames, RTX 3050) the encode with the contrast check on went from 59-74 s to 14.3-14.8 s, where the same encode with the check off takes 14 s. The ratios a layer text reports are the minimum over its measured frames rather than all of them.
- `render` converts each frame to 16-bit display sRGB with its alpha on the output stage, reads one frame back while the next renders, and encodes the PNG files on a writer thread at most two frames behind, where before it read every frame back as float pixels, converted them on the CPU and encoded the file before rendering the next. `render --json` reports `stage_seconds` as an encode does (evaluation, render, the wait for a frame's readback, writing the files) and the text summary prints them as `stages:`. A frame that cannot be written still fails the run with the frames before it complete and none after it, and `--changed-only` records a frame's fingerprint only once its file is there. On perf_layers (1080p60, 120 frames, RTX 3050) a PNG sequence took 59.9 s at 8 bits and 67.1 s at 16; it takes 3.3 s and 6.2 s, with the files the same size and 119 of the 120 8-bit frames byte-identical (the other within one code).

## CLI

- `watch` takes `--path-noise REL`, as `render` and `encode` do, so a preview stops tracing a path-traced pixel at the noise level the final render will use.
- The encode report carries each output's path samples: `outputs[].paths` holds the sample budget, the samples traced and those traced with hardware rays (null for an output with no path-traced pass), and the text summary prints `paths: T of B samples traced (x%)`. The CLI output schema is 1.3.0. At 64x48 with 64 path samples on lavapipe, two frames traced 393,216 of 393,216 samples without `--path-noise`, 321,232 (81.7 %) at 0.02 and 150,240 (38.2 %) at 0.05.
- `schedule FILE [--rate NAME=RATE]...` assigns a path-traced document's frames to the GPU adapters `gpus` lists, from each frame's path cost and each adapter's measured rate in path segments per second: each frame goes to the adapter that would finish it first, as one contiguous range per adapter. Without rates every frame goes to the best adapter (hardware with ray queries, then other hardware, then software), and a device listed on two backends counts once. The JSON envelope's `schema_version` is 1.4.0.

## Fixes

- The first frame of a node motion-blurred with a shutter that opens before time 0 (the default `shutterPhase` of −90°) blurs over the samples before time 0, holding each property at its first key as the schema defines. Before, a node whose time window starts at 0 was left out of those samples, so the first frame drew it sharp. At 10 fps and 180°, a 20 px square moving 100 px a second draws 25 px wide with 8 partial pixels; it drew 20 px wide and sharp.
- DNxHR outputs are found where FFmpeg builds the encoder (probed with the `dnxhr_lb` profile), and MXF outputs take frame rates outside the muxer's broadcast list (`-strict unofficial`, which leaves a file at a listed rate byte-identical).
- A vector frame with a layer (a mask or a matte) and no backdrop rows no longer panics with `range end index 16 out of range for slice of length 0` in the raster stage. The layer's push and pop commands named backdrop row 0 instead of none, and hashing a tile for partial redraws read 16 rows there. A traced geo outline that stops short of the tiles to its right, such as sr-core's `srep-0036-geo-outline-p125`, was such a frame. It now draws as scene-render 0.5.0 drew it. The pixels of other frames are unchanged: the shader reads a command's backdrop only for coverage.

## Changes that can alter output from v0.7.4

- At an exact vertex of a motion path, an auto-oriented node takes the direction of the segment that leaves the vertex, skipping segments of zero length, as SREP 58 states; at a jump its point is the next subpath's start. Before, constant-speed progress took the direction of the segment that arrives there, so a bar on `M200 100 L300 100 L300 200` at progress 0.5 lay horizontal where it now stands vertical. Spatial keyframe paths and 3D follow-path cameras sample the same paths. Progress between vertices, and the end of a path, are unchanged.
- The PNG frames `render` writes come from the output stage's 16-bit conversion (the sRGB transfer in 32-bit float, rounded to 16 bits, with the 8-bit frame the 16-bit one rounded) instead of the CPU's conversion in 64-bit float, so a sample can differ by one code at either depth; the 16-bit pack and its 8-bit rounding are held to within one code of the CPU conversion in sr-gpu's tests.
- The first frame of a node motion-blurred with a shutter that opens before time 0 (the default `shutterPhase` of −90°) now blurs over the samples before time 0, so a node moving at time 0 draws blurred where it drew sharp.
- Outputs whose encoder reads planar YUV at 4:2:2 or 4:4:4 (ProRes, DNxHR, FFV1 with alpha) take planes packed on the GPU, whose codes match the RGB path's within one level. The 4:2:0 planes of x265, SVT-AV1, libaom, rav1e and VP9 are the NV12 and P010 planes deinterleaved.
- A vector frame with a mask or matte layer and no backdrop rows renders instead of panicking in the raster stage.
- The contrast ratios the report gives for an output layer's texts are the minimum over the frames the check's budget measured rather than over every frame.
- Every other change keeps frames and audio packets as in v0.7.4.

## Unreleased

- The path tracer's denoiser divides each pixel's colour, albedo and normal sums by their counts once, in a preparation pass, instead of in every à-trous tap that reads the pixel. On lavapipe, the denoiser's time on 02-fracture frames 80-84 fell from 290-308 ms to 238 ms per frame (alternated rounds, measured in one binary with the CPU tracer changes below). Frames are the same on lavapipe; on an NVIDIA GPU, one of five frames differs by one 8-bit code in the blue channel (PSNR 107.8 dB), from the driver's float arithmetic (two runs of the code before this change give the same frames there).
- The CPU path tracer answers a shadow ray at the first hit when every material of the scene is opaque and casts shadows, instead of stepping from one nearest hit to the next; it reads a top-level leaf's triangles from a table of corners and edges laid out in leaf order; and it visits the nearer child of a BVH node first. On lavapipe, a 02-fracture frame (80-84, median of four) rendered in 1885 ms and 1920 ms instead of 2001-2117 ms in alternated rounds, measured with the denoiser change above and the first two of these three; the near-first order keeps the same frames. Frames are the same on 02-fracture and 06-marx-engels-clay.
- Clay extraction reads its roughness noise from lattice tables built once per mesh over the grid's bounds, instead of hashing the eight lattice corners of every sample twice. The noise is the same bit for bit, so meshes and frames are the same.
- Encoding on a software adapter renders two automatic segments at once on a machine of 8 cores or more, instead of one. The automatic split depends on the output's duration alone, so the segments, and the file, are those of a render one segment at a time. The 736-frame 1280x720 06-marx-engels-clay film encodes on lavapipe to the same frames in 63.11 s and 68.11 s instead of 75.23 s and 75.71 s (alternated runs, with every other change in this section; user CPU 601 s and 608 s instead of 587 s and 587 s, peak RSS 2.33 to 2.34 GiB instead of 1.88 to 1.99 GiB, the second segment's renderer).
- A positive matte-choke `amount` erodes the matte and a negative one dilates it, whatever the effect's `position`, as the schema and SREP 29 define. Before, a positive amount at the default position (`outside`) dilated and at `inside` eroded, and a negative amount reversed both, so a matte-choke with a positive amount and no position now erodes where it grew. sr-core's case `srep-0029-matte-choke-grow` passes.
- `resample="bicubic"` and `resample="mitchell"` read the sixteen texels of a magnified sample with their exact weights instead of nine bilinear fetches, and a sample within 1/1024 texel of a texel centre is at that centre (t = 0), as SREP 32 states. Before, the interpolated position fell a few ulp short of the centre, so the anti-ringing clamp took the texel to the left: on sr-core's case `srep-0032-resample-mitchell`, pixels 34, 38, 42, 46 and 50 were 28, 0, 125, 128 and 250 where SREP 32 gives 0, 28.6, 128, 139.3 and 255. The case passes. Other magnified bicubic and Mitchell samples can change too, since the weights are no longer the sampler's bilinear ones.
- On a software adapter, shadow maps are at most 1024 × 1024: the rasteriser shades every shadow texel on the CPU, and a 2048 × 2048 map, the default `shadowMapSize`, costs four times as many. GPU adapters keep the asked size within the 512 MB budget. On lavapipe, perf_3d's frames (four spot lights at 2048, lights orbiting) have a PSNR of 50.05 to 50.69 dB against those with 2048 × 2048 maps, the change lying at shadow edges, and a frame (0-30, median) renders in 284.7, 234.1, 263.4 and 261.3 ms instead of 302.5, 303.4, 289.4 and 271.6 ms in alternated runs. 06-marx-engels-clay's frames are the same: its one shadow map is drawn once and kept.
- The CPU path tracer's top-level BVH has four children per node: the binned-SAH binary tree is collapsed so that each node holds the children of a binary node with its largest inner children opened, their boxes side by side so one pass tests all four. The walk visits the hit children nearest entry first and skips a child whose box begins beyond a hit found since it was queued. Its stack holds every child a tree of the build's depth can queue; the binary walk dropped children when its stack of 64 was full. On lavapipe, a 02-fracture frame (80-84, median of four) renders in 1540, 1515 and 1485 ms instead of 2048, 1834 and 1846 ms in alternated rounds. Frames are the same on 02-fracture and 06-marx-engels-clay.
