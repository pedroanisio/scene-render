# scene-render v0.8.0

This release accepts documents of version 1.8, which carry six drafts of sr-core: random streams, media phase functions, plots, space transforms, semantic TeX and reactive values. Simulations of a 1.8 document draw from the lattice-hash streams. A medium takes a Henyey-Greenstein, Rayleigh, Draine or fog phase function. An `axes` node and an axes object draw grids, ticks and plots of expressions. The `matrix`, `map` and `complex` modifiers map outlines through functions of the plane. A formula's tagged parts animate and morph. `value` assets hold numbers that step in fixed time steps or fold over an index, and charts follow them. Version 1.8 follows sr-core drafts that sr-core has not yet accepted, so a later sr-core release may change what a 1.8 document means. A document of version 1.7 or earlier renders as in v0.7.5, except for formula placement and the rounding listed below.

## Unreleased

- A 1.8 document may hold the `field` node of the fields draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-fields.md`): a `vector`, `scalar` or `complex` field over a box of `width` × `height` pixels showing `xMin`..`xMax` × `yMin`..`yMax`, or over the box and ranges of its enclosing `axes`. Its one source is expressions `u` and `v` of `x`, `y` and `time` (`u` alone for a scalar field); a complex `w` written "re; im", or a built-in complex `function` (`mobius` with eight `coefficients`); a `grid` naming a data source of rows x, y, u, v (x, y, s for a scalar field), bilinear between its lattice nodes; a physics `forceField`, with its animated values at the frame; or `charge`, `current`, `dipole` and `wire` children, a `wire` being a straight segment in the plane from (`x1`, `y1`) to (`x2`, `y2`) with the charge `q` per unit length, whose finite line charge adds to the field, and the current `i` from its first point to its second, whose magnetic field is out of the plane. Its presentations are `arrows` on a grid, along the field or, with `show="poynting"`, along the Poynting vector E × B of the charges, dipoles and wires, evenly spaced `streamlines`, the field `lines` of its charges and the `contours` of a scalar field, each a stroked path in its own `stroke`, `strokeWidth` and dash or else the field's, and a `lic` (line integral convolution of lattice-hash noise, from its `seed` or else the project seed) or `portrait` (hue by arg w, shaded by the fractional part of log_base |w| when `modulus` is set), a raster that fills the box at its `opacity`. The evaluator recomputes every part at every frame; a scalar field draws only its contours and the lic of its gradient. FLD1 to FLD6 check that the source fits the kind, the ranges, that each presentation fits its field, the streamline bounds, that a field outside an axes gives its box and that Poynting arrows have a wire carrying a current and a charge, and V17 refuses fields before 1.8. A field the evaluator cannot evaluate, such as a grid whose rows are not a lattice or a `forceField` that names no force field of physics, is the build error FLDE1. `sr_eval::field_node` lowers the node, and `FrameNode::raster` carries a lic's or portrait's pixels to the renderer. Output changes only for 1.8 documents.
- A 1.8 document may hold the time-frames draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-time-frames.md`): a `space` after `physics`, whose `epoch` is UTC text (Z or a ±hh:mm offset, from 1972-01-01) or a TT or TDB Julian day, whose `rate` is seconds of ephemeris time per second of scene time, about an `origin` body in the `icrf`, `eclipticJ2000` or `bodyFixed` frame of a `frameBody`; an `ephemeris` asset, a JPL SPK file of type 2 segments pinned by `digest="sha256:…"`, or the approximate Keplerian elements (planets 1 to 8 about the Sun, years −3000 to 3000); and `object3D` `body`, `positionFrom` and `orientation`. At each frame an object with `positionFrom="ephemeris"` stands at its body's position about the origin, (x, −z, y) × 1000 × `physics/@pixelsPerMeter` in the frame's axes, and one with `orientation="bodyFixed"` turns with its body's IAU fixed axes; scale and parenting stay the object's. TF1 refuses a file of another kind or digest, TF2 an epoch out of its form or range, TF3 a body-fixed frame without rotation elements, TF4 a placed object without a body, a space or a body the ephemeris holds, TF5 a non-finite rate, an ephemeris that names no asset or an origin the ephemeris does not hold, and V17 the syntax before 1.8; W16 warns that `x`, `y` and `z` are ignored under `positionFrom="ephemeris"`. A frame the ephemeris cannot place, outside the file's span or past the elements' years, fails and names the object. The `sr-space` crate reads SPK files, the time scales, the elements and the frames, and `sr_eval::time_frames` places the bodies. Output changes only for 1.8 documents.
- A 1.8 document may hold the star-fields draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-star-fields.md`): a `catalogue` asset, the draft's gzipped CSV of stars pinned by `digest="sha256:…"`, and a dome light's `starField` child, which draws the catalogue's stars to `limitMagnitude`, each a disc `pointSize` pixels across whose pixels sum to `magnitudeZero` × 10^(−0.4 V) times the star's blackbody colour and the dome's gain, at its direction moved by proper motion to the space's epoch or to `epoch`. The field is behind every surface and turns with the dome; the raster pass draws it, or, when the frame has no 3D pass, it is the frame's background. `constellations="labels"` draws each constellation's abbreviation 14 output pixels high in the default sans-serif face, centred on the mean direction of all its catalogue stars, and `constellations="boundaries"` draws the constellation boundaries one output pixel wide, precessed from 1875 to J2000; both are grey at 0.18 times the dome's gain. SF1 refuses a file of another kind or digest or a proper motion beyond 100 arcseconds a year, SF2 a field off a dome light, a second field or one naming no catalogue asset, SF3 a non-finite `limitMagnitude` (the schema keeps `magnitudeZero` and `pointSize` positive), SF4 an epoch outside −8000 to 12000, and V17 the syntax before 1.8; W17 warns that `epoch` is ignored beside a space. `sr_space` reads the catalogue and its flux, colour, directions and constellations. Output changes only for 1.8 documents.
- A 1.8 document may give a dome light the physical sky of the physical-sky draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-physical-sky.md`): `sky="physical"` is the sky of its `date` and observer (`latitude`, `longitude`, `altitude`, `pressure`, `temperature`, `deltaT`), the sun of the SPA and the moon at their places seen through a Rayleigh, Mie and ozone atmosphere with multiple scattering (`atmosphere`, `aerosol`, `ozone`, `planetRadius`, `atmosphereHeight`, `groundAlbedo`), lit by `sunIrradiance` × `sunIntensity`, an absent `sunIntensity` reading as 1. The raster pass and the frame's background draw the sky with the sun's disc of `sunSize` in `sunColor` and the moon's lit disc, clipped at the horizon, and the scene is lit through a bake that holds the sky and their power. The surfaces of a 3D pass are seen through the air (`aerialPerspective`, true by default) and a height fog of `fogDensity` per metre and `fogHeight` metres, marched along each camera ray or, without the march or the air, in the fog's closed form toward the horizon's radiance; lengths and heights are in metres through `physics/@pixelsPerMeter`, and a height below the planet's surface reads as 0. The path tracer applies both to the first surface each camera ray meets and sees the bake as its sky. A dome's `starField` under the physical sky is dimmed star by star by the transmittance from the observer toward each star, and the ground hides the stars below the horizon (the star-fields draft's rule 6). PSKY2 checks the date's form and years (−2000 to 6000), PSKY3 the observer's and the ground's ranges, PSKY4 refuses a hand-placed sun beside the physical sky, V17 refuses the sky before 1.8, and W18 (the draft's PSKY1) warns that the physical sky's attributes under another sky are ignored; `sunIntensity` has no schema default, so under `sky="gradient"` an absent one still reads as 0. `sr_space` computes the sun and moon, and `sr_3d::atmosphere`, `sr_3d::physical_sky` and `sr_3d::aerial` the sky, its discs and the air along a camera's rays. Output changes only for 1.8 documents.
- The path tracer's denoiser divides each pixel's colour, albedo and normal sums by their counts once, in a preparation pass, instead of in every à-trous tap that reads the pixel. On lavapipe, the denoiser's time on 02-fracture frames 80-84 fell from 290-308 ms to 238 ms per frame (alternated rounds, measured in one binary with the CPU tracer changes below). Frames are the same on lavapipe; on an NVIDIA GPU, one of five frames differs by one 8-bit code in the blue channel (PSNR 107.8 dB), from the driver's float arithmetic (two runs of the code before this change give the same frames there).
- The CPU path tracer answers a shadow ray at the first hit when every material of the scene is opaque and casts shadows, instead of stepping from one nearest hit to the next; it reads a top-level leaf's triangles from a table of corners and edges laid out in leaf order; and it visits the nearer child of a BVH node first. On lavapipe, a 02-fracture frame (80-84, median of four) rendered in 1885 ms and 1920 ms instead of 2001-2117 ms in alternated rounds, measured with the denoiser change above and the first two of these three; the near-first order keeps the same frames. Frames are the same on 02-fracture and 06-marx-engels-clay.
- Clay extraction reads its roughness noise from lattice tables built once per mesh over the grid's bounds, instead of hashing the eight lattice corners of every sample twice. The noise is the same bit for bit, so meshes and frames are the same.
- Encoding on a software adapter renders two automatic segments at once on a machine of 8 cores or more, instead of one. The automatic split depends on the output's duration alone, so the segments, and the file, are those of a render one segment at a time. The 736-frame 1280x720 06-marx-engels-clay film encodes on lavapipe to the same frames in 63.11 s and 68.11 s instead of 75.23 s and 75.71 s (alternated runs, with every other change in this section; user CPU 601 s and 608 s instead of 587 s and 587 s, peak RSS 2.33 to 2.34 GiB instead of 1.88 to 1.99 GiB, the second segment's renderer).
- A positive matte-choke `amount` erodes the matte and a negative one dilates it, whatever the effect's `position`, as the schema and SREP 29 define. Before, a positive amount at the default position (`outside`) dilated and at `inside` eroded, and a negative amount reversed both, so a matte-choke with a positive amount and no position now erodes where it grew. sr-core's case `srep-0029-matte-choke-grow` passes.
- `resample="bicubic"` and `resample="mitchell"` read the sixteen texels of a magnified sample with their exact weights instead of nine bilinear fetches, and a sample within 1/1024 texel of a texel centre is at that centre (t = 0), as SREP 32 states. Before, the interpolated position fell a few ulp short of the centre, so the anti-ringing clamp took the texel to the left: on sr-core's case `srep-0032-resample-mitchell`, pixels 34, 38, 42, 46 and 50 were 28, 0, 125, 128 and 250 where SREP 32 gives 0, 28.6, 128, 139.3 and 255. The case passes. Other magnified bicubic and Mitchell samples can change too, since the weights are no longer the sampler's bilinear ones.
- On a software adapter, shadow maps are at most 1024 × 1024: the rasteriser shades every shadow texel on the CPU, and a 2048 × 2048 map, the default `shadowMapSize`, costs four times as many. GPU adapters keep the asked size within the 512 MB budget. On lavapipe, perf_3d's frames (four spot lights at 2048, lights orbiting) have a PSNR of 50.05 to 50.69 dB against those with 2048 × 2048 maps, the change lying at shadow edges, and a frame (0-30, median) renders in 284.7, 234.1, 263.4 and 261.3 ms instead of 302.5, 303.4, 289.4 and 271.6 ms in alternated runs. 06-marx-engels-clay's frames are the same: its one shadow map is drawn once and kept.
- The CPU path tracer's top-level BVH has four children per node: the binned-SAH binary tree is collapsed so that each node holds the children of a binary node with its largest inner children opened, their boxes side by side so one pass tests all four. The walk visits the hit children nearest entry first and skips a child whose box begins beyond a hit found since it was queued. Its stack holds every child a tree of the build's depth can queue; the binary walk dropped children when its stack of 64 was full. On lavapipe, a 02-fracture frame (80-84, median of four) renders in 1540, 1515 and 1485 ms instead of 2048, 1834 and 1846 ms in alternated rounds. Frames are the same on 02-fracture and 06-marx-engels-clay.
- Threads that submit and wait on one GPU device at once do not panic inside wgpu-core with "queue empty with a fence below the wait index": wgpu-core 30.0.1 reads the fence and then triages the queue as two steps, so a second poll or a submit, which triages the queue too, between them could empty the queue first. The engine polls and submits to each device from one thread at a time, in waits of at most 10 ms so that a long wait does not hold back the other threads (`sr_gpu::gpu::poll_wait` and `sr_gpu::gpu::submit`).
- Through an orthographic camera the raster pass shades every surface point along the view axis, the line of sight of every pixel of an orthographic projection. Before, it took the direction from the point toward the camera's position, so specular highlights, Fresnel terms and image-based reflections leaned toward that position, more so the nearer the camera stood: with the camera 50 units in front of a sphere of radius 100, the surface point 50 units off the axis was shaded as if seen 38° off the axis. Frames of lit surfaces through an orthographic camera change; the path tracer already traced along the axis. A visible dome's sky behind an orthographic camera is its radiance along the axis over the whole frame, in the 3D pass's background and in the frame's background without a 3D pass, as the path tracer's rays that miss every surface read it; before, each pixel read the direction from the camera's position through its point on the far plane, so the sky's gradient showed across the frame.
- An `exr-sequence` output keeps values above 1.0: the renderer hands FFmpeg the three float planes of an opaque EXR (`gbrpf32le`, `sr_media::encode::InputFormat::Gbrpf32`) or the four of one with alpha, which FFmpeg writes as they are. Before, an opaque EXR was handed four planes, and FFmpeg's conversion to the three it writes passes through 16-bit integers, so every value was clipped to 1.0 and quantised; `twoPass` on an EXR sequence went through a 16-bit intermediate and was clipped the same way. An EXR sequence encodes in one pass, and a `maxFileSize` on a sequence output is refused, since a sequence has no bitrate to fit a file size (before, the fit failed on a missing file).

## Random streams

- Documents of version 1.8 are accepted: the version of the random-streams draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-random-streams.md`), vendored as sr-core 1.7.0 plus the one `1.8` value of the version enumeration. Their simulations draw from the lattice-hash streams of the draft. A draw is S(e, p, t, i) = U(e, p + t · 2³², i), addressed by the element seed e = E(project, id, `@seed`, "stream") (the CRC-32 of the four, so the project seed enters every simulation), a purpose p per drawn quantity, the step t and the index i. 2D particle emitters, flocks, slimes, erosion, 3D particle emitters and the ejecta of their crater bursts, whitewater, pyro turbulence and force-field noise draw this way. A whitewater and a pyro have no id of their own: they use their ocean's or object's under the purposes `stream:whitewater` and `stream:pyro`. Where the schema gives `@seed` a default, the default is the `@seed` of the element seed. A document of version 1.7 or earlier draws as before, bit for bit; so do, at 1.8, the agents of programs, program nodes, clay, voxel crater cuts and ocean wave spectra, which the draft leaves out. `sr_vector::d24` adds `stream`, `stream_signed`, `stream_normal` and `unpack`, and the draft's 37 reference values are tests. Output changes only for 1.8 documents, which were refused before.

## Media

- A medium of a 1.8 document takes a phase function from the media draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-media.md`): `phase` is `hg` (the default, Henyey-Greenstein with `anisotropy`), `rayleigh`, `draine` (with `anisotropy` and `phaseAlpha`, default 1) or `fog` (the fitted mixture of a Henyey-Greenstein and a Draine lobe for a `dropletDiameter` of 5 to 50 µm, default 10). The schema and the native rules check the draft's MED1 to MED3, and MED4 refuses the three attributes before version 1.8. `sr_volume::medium::Phase` evaluates and samples each kind: Henyey-Greenstein by inversion, Draine by bisection of its cumulative, fog by drawing a lobe first. The cumulative of Draine is written in a form that never divides by g; the draft's form loses its digits for 1e-6 ≤ |g| ≲ 1e-4. The path tracer packs each medium's phase as a mixture of the two lobes and samples it the same way, and a medium whose phase is not isotropic is lit from the directional dome grids. On the GPU the Henyey-Greenstein denominator 1 + g² − 2gμ is now evaluated as (1 − g)² + 2g(1 − μ), which keeps its digits at a forward peak, so a medium without the attributes can differ from before by f32 rounding.

## Plots

- A 1.8 document may hold the `axes` node of the plots draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-plots.md`): a box of `width` × `height` pixels showing the data ranges `xMin`..`xMax` × `yMin`..`yMax` as a `cartesian` plane, a `complex` plane, `polar` circles and rays, or a `number-line`, with `plot` children of the kinds `explicit` (y of x), `parametric` (x and y of t), `polar` (r of θ) and `implicit` (f(x, y) = `level`, by marching squares). The evaluator lowers an `axes` to path shapes for its grid, axis lines, ticks and plots, a group of its box that clips its child nodes, and its tick labels, which the renderer typesets as formulas in the axes' `stroke` colour at `labelSize`. The ranges, steps and the plots' expressions are read at every frame, so an animated range moves the plots and the children. A child node's `x` and `y` are data coordinates and its `anchorX` and `anchorY` data offsets; its other attributes stay in pixels. A frame at which an animated range closes (`xMin` ≥ `xMax`) reports the problem and draws the axes' parts empty. `sr_eval::axes` computes each frame's parts from the draft's rules in `sr_eval::plots`.
- A 1.8 document may hold an `object3D primitive="axes"` of the same draft: its `axes3D` maps data to the object's local space, the data centre at its origin and +y data up, and draws the three axis lines and their ticks as cylinders of `lineWidth` and `tickLength` in its `color`. Each `plot3D` is a tube of `tubeRadius` and `tubeSegments` around the curve (x, y, z)(t), sampled at `samples` values of t from `t0` to `t1` and read at every frame, in its `color`. A ring whose point is not finite is left out with its two cells, and the tube's parallel-transport frames run over the finite points. The colours are vertex colours, which multiply the object's material, and a `plot3D` with a `material` is drawn with that material instead. The `axes3D` attributes are read from the document, not animated. A colour given as a token or a paint reference draws white, leaving the material's colour unchanged. A `plot3D` whose points make no tube (fewer than two distinct finite points) is reported as a problem and left out. `FrameNode::param_parts` holds the meshes of the plots with their own material.

## Space transforms

- A 1.8 document may give a `deform` the modifier types `matrix`, `map` and `complex` of the space-transforms draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-space-transforms.md`), and a `group` a `deform`. `matrix` is SVG's `matrix(a, b, c, d, e, f)` about `centerX`/`centerY`; `map` gives the point's new data coordinates as expressions `x` and `y` of `u` and `v` (+v up, `unit` pixels a unit), read at every frame; `complex` applies one of ten complex functions (`square` to `joukowski`, and `mobius` with eight `coefficients`) in the same frame. With one of them in a node's chain, a shape's outline is mapped before its trim, fill, stroke and markers, so a stroke keeps its width; each segment is split at its midpoint until its mapped chord is within `tolerance` (0.25 px by default), at most 10 times, and a point that maps to infinity breaks the outline into open pieces. Text and vector layer content maps its drawn outlines the same way, and a chain of the fourteen existing types deforms as before. A group's deform maps all its descendants in the group's space, after their own and centred on the union of their boxes. The rules SPT1 and SPT2 check the types' attributes and that a Möbius map is invertible, and V17 refuses the types and a group's deform before 1.8. An image layer under one of the three types deforms its grid mesh by the same chain, without the outline breaking of the shapes. `FrameNode::deform_maps` and `FrameGraph::point_map` give a map's point function.

## Semantic TeX

- A 1.8 document may use the semantic-tex draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-semantic-tex.md`). A formula's TeX may tag parts with `\part{name}{body}`, and the tags change nothing in the layout. A `formulaAnimator` child of a formula layer moves, scales, turns, fades, recolours, strokes or blurs the glyphs of one part about the part's box. It takes `x`, `y`, `scale`, `scaleX`, `scaleY`, `rotation`, `opacity`, `fill`, `stroke`, `strokeWidth`, `blur`, `anchorX` and `anchorY`, animated as a `textAnimator`'s are and combined as one with a selection amount of 1. A nested part moves with its container. A part the formula lacks is an error of the layer. A `formulaMorph` asset turns its `from` formula into its `to` formula at its animated `progress`. Both are typeset at its `size` and share the fit of their union box. Outermost parts pair by `name` or `tex`, and pairs with equal TeX move along the box path. Pairs with different TeX morph their outlines, resampled to 64 points by arc length, aligned and paired by centroid. Unpaired parts and untagged glyphs fade. A `shape` with `morph="true"` interpolates the keys of its `animate property="path"` by the same path morph. The rules FRM1 and FRM2 check that an animator sits on a formula layer and that a morph names formulas, and V17 refuses all of it before 1.8. INERT-I18 reports a `morph` without path keys. `sr_text::parts`, `sr_text::formula_anim` and `sr_vector::morph` hold the draft's rules. Output changes only for 1.8 documents, which were refused before.

## Reactive values

- A 1.8 document may hold the `value` assets of the reactive-values draft of sr-core (`srep/srep-0000-reactive-values.md`): a named number or number list that draws nothing. `prop("id.value")`, `propAtTime` and `link source="id.value"` read it from anywhere in the document. Its keys, expression or link give it as for any property. With `update` and `step` it is a time fold: from its keyed value at `start` it advances in fixed steps of `step` seconds, and its value at t is the state after ⌊(t − start) / step + 10⁻⁹⌋ steps. With `between="linear"` it interpolates between steps. With `update` and `count` it is an index fold, whose update runs `count` times at every frame. An update reads `value`, `index`, `count`, `time`, `dt`, `random()`, which draws by step instead of by frame, and `draw(channel, i)`, a draw shared by every value of one seed at one step. Time folds of one `step` and `start` that read each other step together in gather form: each reads the others' state before the step. A read across steps or starts, or of an index fold inside an update, is the build error RVE1. A time fold of more than 1,000,000 steps by the composition's end is RVE2. An update that gives a non-finite state is a render error. The evaluator keeps a checkpoint of each system every 1024 steps, so a frame rendered alone equals the frame played in sequence, exactly. A chart `series` whose `values` is linked or expressed reads them at every frame, so a counter chart shows a live count. The rules RV1 and RV2 check that `update` goes with exactly one of `step` and `count` and that `between` belongs to a time fold, and V17 refuses values before 1.8. `sr_eval::fold` holds the draft's rules, and the draft's numeric table, its Fourier centre of mass and its Einstein solid are tests. Output changes only for 1.8 documents, which were refused before.

## Fixes

- A formula is centred in its box on its layout's extent. Before, it was drawn higher than its box's middle by its ascent less its descent, so a formula asset sat high in its box and could leave it at the top. Every `formula` asset draws lower than before by that amount.
- `watch` renders a change once the watched files settle: after a stamp changes it polls until two polls one interval apart agree. Before, a writer that truncated a file and then wrote it could be caught between the two, and `watch` rendered the empty document, reported an XML error and, as the last run of `--max-runs`, exited with code 1. With a scene held truncated from 45 ms to 75 ms after the ready line at the default 50 ms interval, the previous loop failed 30 of 30 runs and this one none of 30.

## Changes that can alter output from v0.7.5

- Every `formula` asset is centred on its layout's extent, which draws it lower than before by its ascent less its descent (see Fixes).
- On the GPU the Henyey-Greenstein denominator is evaluated as (1 − g)² + 2g(1 − μ), so a path traced medium without the media draft's attributes can differ from before by f32 rounding.
- Documents of version 1.8 were refused before; every other change keeps frames and audio packets as in v0.7.5.
