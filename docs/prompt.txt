Create a comprehensive **editorial and visual production guide for transforming news, reports, documents, presentations, and structured information into stop-motion-style videos**.

The guide must define not only how to animate content, but also **how to make editorial decisions**: what visual language to use, what assets to generate or source, how to preserve factual integrity, how to adapt the same story to different platforms, and how to create a recognizable and consistent house style.

The final guide should function as a practical handbook for editors, designers, animators, producers, and AI-assisted production systems.

## 1. Editorial principles

Define the core editorial principles for converting factual content into video.

Cover:

- factual accuracy
- source fidelity
- visual clarity
- narrative compression
- hierarchy of information
- distinction between fact, interpretation, context, and speculation
- how to avoid visually implying claims that the source does not support
- when visualization should be literal versus metaphorical
- how to preserve nuance while simplifying complex stories
- how to use humor, irony, urgency, drama, or emotional framing responsibly
- how visual style should change according to subject matter

Establish rules for maintaining editorial credibility even when using highly stylized stop-motion, collage, miniature, paper-cut, clay-like, photographic, or generated imagery.

## 2. Editorial lines by subject

Define distinct editorial treatments for different types of stories.

Include at minimum:

### Breaking news
Fast, clear, restrained, fact-first.

### Politics and public affairs
Neutral visual framing, clear attribution, contextual timelines, documents, quotes, maps, institutions, voting or legislative mechanics where relevant.

### Business and economics
Data-led storytelling, charts, numbers, company assets, market context, products, documents, and timelines.

### Technology and AI
Product interfaces, diagrams, conceptual objects, workflows, code, infrastructure, devices, abstract systems, and explanatory metaphors.

### Science and space
Scale, diagrams, physical processes, spatial movement, explanatory labels, simulations, and scientifically informed visualizations.

### Investigations
Documents, timelines, connections, annotations, maps, evidence chains, and restrained dramatic treatment.

### Culture and entertainment
More expressive visual treatment, stronger rhythm, personality, archive material, typography, and references.

### Sports
Faster pacing, statistics, trajectories, player or team imagery, scoreboards, field or court diagrams.

### Historical stories
Archive media, maps, documents, dates, recreated environments, timelines, and clearly identified reconstruction.

### Explainers
Visual-first teaching, progressive construction, labeled objects, diagrams, comparisons, and repeated visual motifs.

For each editorial category, specify:

- recommended visual tone
- pacing
- color logic
- typography
- camera behavior
- use of generated imagery
- use of archive or stock
- use of diagrams
- use of text
- audio approach
- acceptable level of stylization
- common editorial risks
